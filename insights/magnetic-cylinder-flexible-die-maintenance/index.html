Magnetic Cylinder and Flexible Die Care: A Practical Maintenance Routine

Prevent die-cut variation by cleaning, inspecting, mounting and storing magnetic cylinders and flexible dies with a repeatable shift routine.

18 August 20268 min readRoyal Coat

A magnetic cylinder can look clean and still carry a thin patch of adhesive, dried ink or corrosion under the flexible die. That raised area changes how the die sits and can show up as an incomplete cut, a liner strike or a fault that appears in the same position every repeat. Adding pressure may hide the symptom for a short run, but it also loads the die, bearers and anvil. A better routine starts with the two mating surfaces, then checks the cylinder, die, pressure and storage record in that order.

Treat a repeated cut fault as evidence

Mark the faulty label and confirm whether the defect repeats at the cylinder repeat. If it stays in the same machine-direction position, inspect the corresponding area of the flexible die, magnetic cylinder and anvil before changing pressure. If the fault moves across the web or follows a material splice, the substrate, liner or web path may be involved instead.

Record whether the problem is an incomplete face cut, liner marking, adhesive build-up, matrix break or local high spot. These symptoms do not all have the same cause. The first check should narrow the station, not create a new variable.

Clean both mating surfaces before mounting

Wilson advises cleaning the magnetic-cylinder surface before every use with a clean, lint-free cloth and wiping in one direction across the cylinder, then along the bearer. The back of the flexible die also needs to be clean. A fibre, adhesive particle or dried ink film trapped between the two surfaces can hold part of the die away from the cylinder.

Rotometal likewise notes that ink and adhesive residue can create an uneven surface and lift the flexible die. Use only the cleaning method approved for the cylinder, die, coating and residue. Remove the die for any cleaning operation that the supplier does not permit on the cylinder, and leave both surfaces dry before mounting.

Inspect the cylinder before blaming the die

Check the magnetic surface, bearers and gear under good light. Look for corrosion, dents, raised damage, adhesive at the die edge, worn gear teeth and marks that can be felt even when they are difficult to see. Wilson recommends feeling the cleaned surface with a clean bare hand to identify small imperfections, while keeping jewellery and loose tools away from the cutting edges.

Runout, bearer condition and undercut affect the working gap. If cut pressure has been rising across repeat jobs, or a fault remains after cleaning and remounting, measure the tooling rather than continuing to add load. A qualified inspection can determine whether the cylinder needs repair, regrinding or replacement.

Mount and remove the flexible die without bending it

Support the flexible die while presenting it to the cylinder and apply it progressively. Do not let one corner snap down or allow the sheet to kink. A permanent bend can stop the die from seating evenly and may damage a cutting rule. Confirm that the leading and trailing edges are held correctly before the station is closed.

Use a purpose-made die lifter or removal tool when repositioning or removing the sheet. Pulling the cutting edge by hand risks injury and can distort the die. Rings, knives, spanners and other hard objects should not be on the mounting surface or near the exposed cutting rules.

Run at the minimum pressure that gives the approved cut

Set pressure from a known mechanical condition and the material for which the die was specified. Wilson advises using the least pressure needed to obtain the cut and checking it again after warm-up because tooling diameters expand as temperature rises. Pressure gauges make the change visible and allow start and running values to be recorded.

Keep bearer wipers clean, correctly positioned and lubricated where the station design requires them. Debris at the bearers and heat from friction can damage the contact surfaces. Release station pressure during a prolonged stop or adjustment according to the machine procedure, then return to the verified running value.

Clean, protect and store the tooling after the run

Remove ink, adhesive and loose debris with the approved materials before storage. The cylinder and die must be dry. Where the tooling supplier specifies a corrosion inhibitor or light oil, apply the recommended product and amount; an unsuitable cleaner or lubricant can attack a coating or leave a film that causes the next mounting problem.

Return the flexible die to its rust-inhibiting protective bag and store it in the intended hanging system or container without weight on the cutting rules. Keep the magnetic cylinder in its protected storage arrangement, away from moisture, loose hardware and temperature extremes. Original packaging is useful because it supports the tool without allowing another object to contact the precision surfaces.

Keep a maintenance record that helps the next shift

Identify the magnetic cylinder, flexible die and anvil in the job record. Save the repeat, teeth, undercut where applicable, substrate and liner construction, starting pressure, warm running pressure, speed and the exact position of any fault. Add a photograph of surface damage or contamination before it is removed.

Set a stop rule for a raised mark, corrosion, damaged gear, recurring liner strike or pressure that cannot return to the approved range. The record should show whether the die, cylinder or anvil was cleaned, inspected, measured or sent for repair. That is more useful than a note saying only that extra pressure was required.

Send the tooling and job facts for an application review

For a useful review, provide the press and die-station model, magnetic-cylinder repeat or tooth count, undercut, flexible-die identification, face stock, adhesive, liner, speed and the defect pattern. Include photographs of the cut result and the cleaned tooling surface, plus any runout or pressure readings already taken.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about rotary tooling maintenance

Should a magnetic cylinder be cleaned before every job?

Inspect and clean the mating surface before mounting each flexible die. Use the method approved by the tooling supplier and make sure the surface is dry.

Can more die pressure correct an incomplete cut?

It may mask the symptom, but first check contamination, mounting, material, gap, anvil condition and tooling wear. Excess pressure can shorten tool life and mark the liner.

Can an operator pull a flexible die off by hand?

Use a purpose-made die lifter or removal tool. Pulling at the cutting edge risks injury, bending and local damage.

What should be recorded when a cut fault repeats?

Record the repeat position, die and cylinder identity, material construction, pressure before and after warm-up, speed, symptom and any measured runout or damage.

When should the magnetic cylinder be professionally inspected?

Arrange measurement when cleaning and remounting do not remove a repeatable fault, pressure keeps increasing, or the surface, bearers, gear, runout or undercut may be outside the approved condition.
