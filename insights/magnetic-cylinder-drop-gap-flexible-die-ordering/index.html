Magnetic Cylinder DROP and GAP

Magnetic Cylinder DROP or GAP? Confirm the Dimension Before Ordering a Flexible Die

Prevent flexible-die cutting errors by distinguishing magnetic-cylinder DROP from radial GAP and confirming the press, cylinder and label construction before ordering.

3 November 20266 min readRoyal Coat

DROP and GAP describe related dimensions in a magnetic-cylinder die-cutting station, but they are not the same number. Confusing them can put the flexible die at the wrong working height before the job reaches the press. State which dimension is being used, confirm it against the cylinder drawing and order the die for the actual face material, adhesive and liner. That discipline is more reliable than trying to recover an incorrect geometry with extra cutting pressure.

Use the correct term before discussing cutting pressure

Rotometal defines DROP as the difference between the bearer diameter and the working-body diameter of a magnetic cylinder. It is a diameter measurement. GAP is the corresponding radial clearance between the magnetic-cylinder body and the anvil, so the DROP value is twice the GAP value. A drawing that states 0.96 mm DROP therefore describes a nominal radial GAP of 0.48 mm, not another 0.96 mm clearance on each side.

Write both the value and the basis on every enquiry: for example, 'DROP on diameter' or 'radial GAP'. Do not pass on a bare number copied from an old die packet, spreadsheet or verbal note. Also record whether the value comes from the original cylinder drawing, a recent measurement or an assumption. The tooling supplier needs to know which evidence it can use.

The dimension matters because the magnetic-cylinder geometry and the flexible die's total blade height work together to establish the cutting position. Flexographic Technical Association guidance makes the same connection between die blade height and cylinder diff or drop. Pressure closes the station and maintains stable contact at the bearers; it should not be used to make an incorrectly specified die tall enough to reach the job.

Build the order from the complete die-cutting stack

Begin with the press make, model and die station. Add the magnetic-cylinder identification, tooth count or repeat, face length, bearer diameter, working-body diameter and confirmed DROP or GAP. Include the anvil identification and any support-roll arrangement. If the magnetic cylinder has been reground, repaired or moved from another press, do not assume that the original library record still describes its present condition.

Then describe the material that the die must cut. Send the face material, adhesive, liner type and liner thickness, total laminate construction, web width, label shape, corner radius, number across and around, gap around and across, and the required cut direction. State whether the job must cut to the liner, cut through, perforate or use more than one cutting level. The liner is part of the cutting system, not simply the carrier under the label.

Use an actual material sample whenever possible. A generic description such as 'paper label' or 'clear film' does not define the caliper, compressibility, adhesive behaviour or release liner. FTA guidance for flexible-die orders specifically calls for the magnetic-cylinder gap when it differs from the expected standard and notes that the face material influences cutting angle while the liner influences die height. Accepted and failed samples give the supplier a much clearer target than a product name alone.

Separate wrong geometry from wear or an unstable station

If a new die cuts on one side, leaves uncut areas or strikes the liner, first confirm that the die was made for the installed magnetic cylinder and material. Then clean the magnetic-cylinder body and the back of the die, seat the die without trapped debris and inspect the bearers, anvil and bearings. Compare the result with a known-good die on the same station. This separates an order-data error from a machine or tooling-condition problem.

Measure rather than compensate blindly. Check the clearance at the operator side, centre and drive side, then repeat at several cylinder positions. Variation across the face points toward parallelism, bearing, support or anvil condition; variation through rotation points toward runout or local damage. FTA's magnetic-cylinder audit guidance warns that an out-of-tolerance air gap can make a good flexible die appear defective and that excess pressure contributes to bearer wear.

An adjustable or plus/minus anvil can be useful when it is specified for the station and material range. Rotometal's current catalogue documents its rotoGAP adjustable anvil with a stated adjustment range of plus or minus 100 micrometres and a minimum adjustment of 0.5 micrometres, and also lists plus or minus anvil cylinders for material-thickness compensation. These are product-specific capabilities, not permission to ignore the correct DROP, die height or material specification.

Prove the setup with the real laminate and save the result

Run the approval trial with production material from the intended construction. Start with the normal documented station setting and the minimum pressure needed for stable bearer contact. Inspect the cut across the full web and around the complete repeat. Check matrix stripping, corner release, fine bridges, internal waste and dispensing where the label will be automatically applied. A clean top cut is not enough if the liner is marked or weakened.

Record the press, station, magnetic cylinder, flexible die, anvil, material batch, web width, speed, pressure reference and any gap adjustment. Retain a labelled sample of the accepted web and a section showing the liner from the reverse side. If a correction was needed, record the measured reason and the approved setting rather than leaving only an operator's memory of 'more pressure' or 'half a turn'.

When the same die moves to another press or cylinder, verify the DROP or GAP again before production. Matching repeat or tooth count does not prove matching working geometry. For a Royal Coat review, send the press and cylinder drawings, cylinder identification, repeat, DROP or radial GAP, die thickness, anvil details, laminate and liner specification, normal speed and accepted or failed samples. Those details allow the discussion with Rotometal to start from the actual cutting stack.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about magnetic cylinder drop and gap

Are magnetic-cylinder DROP and GAP the same number?

No. DROP is the diameter difference between the bearer and working body. GAP is the corresponding radial clearance, so DROP is twice the GAP value.

Can a flexible die be ordered from the repeat alone?

No. The supplier also needs the press and cylinder details, confirmed DROP or GAP, die and anvil information, face material, adhesive, liner and required type of cut.

Can extra pressure correct the wrong cylinder DROP?

No. Pressure cannot correct an incorrect die and cylinder geometry, and excessive pressure can damage the liner, die, bearers and anvil.

When can an adjustable anvil help?

It can compensate within its specified range for controlled material or cutting variations, but it must be qualified for the press, tooling and actual laminate.

What should be retained after the trial?

Keep the complete setup record, material batch, speed, pressure or gap setting, tooling identities and approved samples showing both the label face and liner.
