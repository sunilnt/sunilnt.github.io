Chamber Doctor Blade End-Seal Leakage

Chamber Doctor Blade Leaking at the Ends? Fix Seal Fit and Alignment Before Adding Pressure

Diagnose flexo chamber leaks by checking the leak pattern, blade and seal fit, chamber cleanliness, alignment, ink flow and minimum working pressure.

25 October 20267 min readRoyal Coat

Ink dripping from one end of a chambered doctor-blade system often produces the same first response: move the chamber harder against the anilox. That may slow the leak for a few minutes while bending the blades, accelerating seal wear and increasing risk to the anilox surface. Preserve the leak pattern and inspect the blade, seal, chamber and ink circuit in order. The useful question is not how much pressure stops the drip. It is why the chamber cannot contain ink and meter the anilox at its normal low working pressure.

Record where and when the ink escapes

Stop and isolate the press under the approved procedure before touching a chamber, blade or seal. Follow the ink safety data sheet and plant controls for skin contact, splashes, solvent vapour, UV materials and waste. A doctor blade edge can cut even when it looks blunt, so use the specified handling tools and protective equipment.

Before cleaning away the evidence, record the press, station, colour, ink and batch, anilox ID, chamber ID, blade and seal products, speed, pump setting, viscosity, ink temperature and chamber pressure or actuator setting. Mark whether the leak starts at operator side, drive side, both ends or across the full chamber. Note whether it begins at startup, only above one speed, after the ink warms, after a stop and restart or late in the run.

Keep a printed sample from before and during the leak. Record any density or colour change, dirty print, streaks, foam, starvation or metal contamination.

Separate an end-seal leak from back doctoring

An end-seal leak is concentrated where the seal, anilox and blade ends meet. Back doctoring can look different: ink builds on or is removed by the containment blade and may drip along a wider part of the chamber. Flexographic Technical Association guidance notes that incomplete chamber setting can leave ink on the anilox surface and allow the containment blade to doctor that film again.

Observe the location from a safe position while the guarded press is operating. Do not place hands, cloth or tools near a rotating anilox to prove the source. If ink runs from the chamber ends, inspect seal fit and blade dimensions first. If it accumulates across the containment blade, include blade orientation, chamber alignment, ink return behaviour and pressure in the diagnosis.

A drip tray may contain the mess, but it does not restore controlled metering.

Match the blades and seals to the chamber

Confirm the blade width, thickness, edge profile, length and installed orientation against the chamber and press specification. FTA technical guidance notes that blade width and length have to match the end seal. A blade cut too short, too long or to the wrong width can disturb how the seal closes around the anilox and encourage an operator to compensate with pressure.

Check the end-seal part number, material, geometry and anilox diameter or radius for the job. The seal must sit squarely in its cavity and meet the blade ends and roll surface as designed. A seal from another chamber may look close enough on the bench but compress differently under ink temperature, solvent exposure and production speed.

Inspect removed seals and blades as a set. Uneven compression, one polished side, torn corners, dried-ink tracks or unequal blade wear can indicate poor seating, chamber misalignment or an incorrect part. Keep the used components labelled by station and side for the maintenance or supplier review instead of mixing them in general waste.

Clean the holder and verify chamber alignment

Dried ink under a blade clamp or in an end-seal cavity changes the installed geometry. Clean the chamber body, clamps and seal seats by the approved method, then inspect for burrs, damage, loose fasteners and distorted surfaces. Fit blades straight and tighten the clamp in the specified sequence without overtightening.

Bring the chamber to the anilox evenly. FTA guidance states that misalignment in any axis can require extra blade pressure and lead to print variation. Check that both ends reach the roll together and that the chamber is parallel at the upper and lower blade positions. Use the press manufacturer's alignment method rather than judging only from the pressure gauge.

If one side repeatedly needs more load, do not make that offset the new standard. Check the actuator, pivots, mount, chamber straightness, end-seal seats and anilox position. Repeated one-sided wear is maintenance evidence.

Set ink flow and the lowest working pressure

Confirm that supply and return hoses are connected correctly, unrestricted and free from trapped debris. Record the approved pump setting, reservoir level, filter condition and return position. Excess flow can raise chamber pressure and force ink past a seal, while inadequate flow can starve the anilox. Change the circuit only within the chamber, pump and ink supplier's operating instructions.

Apply only enough chamber load to contain the ink and produce a clean, correctly metered anilox at production speed. FTA guidance repeatedly recommends the least practical pressure. Extra pressure can deflect the metering blade, enlarge its contact area and accelerate wear of the blades, end seals and anilox.

Prove the repair through the complete running condition

Run the approved ink through startup, normal speed, the planned maximum speed, stop and restart and a representative production period. Check both chamber ends, the containment blade, hose connections, print sample, density or colour, blade wipe and any abnormal sound. Record ink temperature and viscosity because a cold startup pass does not prove the chamber will remain stable after the system warms.

After the run, inspect the blades and seals for even contact and unexpected wear. Keep the final blade and seal IDs, chamber alignment result, pump setting, working pressure, ink condition and accepted sample with the station record. This provides a baseline for the next leak instead of starting again from an unknown setup.

For a chamber-leak review, send Royal Coat the press and station, chamber and anilox IDs, blade and seal specifications, photographs of the leak before cleaning, used-component wear pattern, ink and viscosity method, temperature, pump and pressure settings and labelled print samples. Those details help separate a consumable mismatch, alignment fault, ink-circuit problem and anilox risk before another set is installed.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about chamber doctor blade end-seal leakage

Should chamber pressure be increased when ink leaks from an end seal?

Only within the approved setup and after checking blade dimensions, seal geometry, cleanliness, alignment and ink flow. Excess pressure can hide the fault while increasing wear.

How can an end-seal leak be distinguished from back doctoring?

An end-seal leak is concentrated at the chamber end. Back doctoring usually produces ink buildup or dripping along the containment blade. Record the exact location before cleaning.

Can the correct seal still leak?

Yes. A correct seal can leak if it is damaged, contaminated, incorrectly seated, chemically affected, unevenly compressed or used with a misaligned chamber or incorrect blade.

What should be checked after the leak stops?

Verify containment and clean metering through startup, production speed, stop and restart, then inspect print quality and component wear after a representative run.
