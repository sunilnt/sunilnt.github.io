Solvent Exposure and Reuse of Flexo Plates

Reused Flexo Plate Prints Patchy Solids? Compare Plate Condition Before a Remake

Diagnose patchy solids on repeat solvent-flexo jobs using retained print, plate thickness and hardness records, and a controlled comparison with a fresh plate.

28 November 20266 min readRoyal Coat

The same solid printed evenly on the first order. On the repeat, the stored plate leaves pinholes or a weak band at the trailing edge of the solid. The plate may have changed, but a different anilox, ink blend or mounting tape can produce a misleading comparison. On wide-web solvent flexo jobs, check the plate on its own terms: identify the defect on the printed web, compare the dry plate with its original record, and see whether the problem follows the plate under controlled conditions. A single gauge reading is poor grounds for a remake.

Use the last approved print as the reference

Pull the retained sample from the previous accepted run and check that the artwork revision, colour, film and print direction match. Mark the affected repeat. Is the problem made up of small voids scattered through the solid, or is it a narrow band at the edge that leaves impression last? Compare the result across the web and over several repeats. Record the first acceptable and first faulty pulls if the appearance deteriorates during production.

Measure the agreed solid colour or density and look at the solid under the same illumination and magnification. A reading averaged over a patch can look acceptable even when individual voids are easy to see. The Flexographic Technical Association's Flexo Quality Consortium found this distinction in its trial on surface-patterned plates: visible pinholing and trailing-edge voids were clearer signs of print change than the measured differences in solid ink density.

Separate swelling during the run from dry plate condition

Certain ink solvents can swell and soften photopolymer during contact on press. In the FQC study of solvent-based film printing, test plates measured after solvent exposure and drying became thinner and harder under the conditions studied. Those are different observations at different points in the cycle. A plate that was suspected of swelling during the previous run need not measure thick when it comes back dry.

Compare a clean, dry plate with its original inspection record or an unused plate of the same construction. Use the plate supplier's measurement method and record the reading locations. Do not compare a reading taken over relief or a mounted tape-and-plate assembly with the original bare plate gauge. If hardness is part of the supplier's procedure, use the same test setup and state the condition of the sample. Keep photographs of the solid image area so that damage or residue is not mistaken for a thickness change.

Check what the plate was exposed to

Collect the ink product, actual reducer and blend additions, cleaner, length of previous runs and any extended stoppages with ink on the plate. Check that the ink and cleaner used at the press match the job record. A stable viscosity does not identify the solvent composition. Separately record whether the defect was present before any mid-run correction; a failing sample and an ink addition on the same shift are not proof that one caused the other.

Use compatibility guidance for the actual plate and ink combination. The Cyrel compatibility guide distinguishes the individual solvent from its share of a specific blend, and flags longer exposure for some entries. Its figures do not apply to all plate grades or cleaning methods. Get the plate and ink suppliers to review the actual products when the recipe is in doubt. If they recommend a compatibility test, use their approved method on a spare sample under the site's chemical-handling procedure.

Run a controlled two-plate comparison if the cause is still unclear

Verify the anilox ID and condition, tape construction, sleeve and substrate lot before comparing the used plate with a fresh plate of the same grade, relief and surface pattern. Use the same deck if practical. Set each plate at the normal minimum impression that gives complete contact and record both settings. A copied setting can put two plates with different thicknesses under different load.

Run both plates at the same approved speed and ink condition. Save matching pulls from the solid, a screened area and the trailing edge. If both plates show the defect, look again at what they share before ordering additional plates. If only the used plate fails, check whether the fault follows it after mounting has been verified. Read the new print against the original acceptance limits so a change that clears pinholes but damages tones is not counted as a fix.

Set a local reuse rule based on print

The FQC project did not establish an industry-wide thickness or hardness cutoff. The severity of the defects changed when the same plates were tested in a different press environment, and ordinary impression adjustments resolved the less severe defects in the later trial. Use gauge and hardness trends to flag a plate for review. Make the reuse decision against the actual job's print standard, plate condition and acceptable setup range.

For a Royal Coat application review, send the last accepted and current pulls, plate grade and surface pattern, plate readings and measurement method, ink and reducer used, mount construction, anilox data and the speed at which the defect appears. Include the leading and trailing edges of the affected solid so the defect can be located on the repeat.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about solvent exposure and reuse of flexo plates

Can a plate swell on press but measure thinner on the next job?

Yes, that can occur with some solvent-exposed photopolymer plates. Separate the on-press observation from the dry measurement and compare each with the correct reference.

Does one thickness reading tell me when to remake a flexo plate?

No. Track thickness under a repeatable method, but use the printed result and a controlled comparison before deciding that a plate is no longer fit for the job.

Why do solids look patchy when the density reading still passes?

The sampled area can include both printed and unprinted spots. Compare the actual pinholes and trailing edge under consistent viewing conditions as well as the agreed density or colour reading.
