UV-Curing Thermal Control for Heat-Sensitive Films

Shrink Film Narrows or Curls After UV Curing? Measure Web Temperature Before Changing Tension

Diagnose shrink film or monofoil narrowing after UV curing by mapping web temperature, speed, exposure, cooling and tension before changing the press recipe.

23 November 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A shrink film or thin monofoil can enter the UV station flat and leave it narrower, curled or unstable in register. The problem may be blamed on web tension because the guide begins correcting and the repeat moves, but the first change can be thermal. More UV output, a dirty cooling route, a slow restart or a failed shutter can raise the substrate temperature enough to alter its dimensions before the operator sees a winding fault. Measure the web before and after the curing zone, then separate heat exposure from tension and cure performance.

Map the dimensional change across the curing zone

Mark a representative repeat and record the substrate grade, thickness, treatment side, print sequence, ink or coating system, web width, press speed, tension zones, UV unit, power setting and cooling condition. Measure width and repeat at a stable point before curing and again after the web has cooled. Use the plant's approved non-contact temperature method at defined positions across the web. Do not touch or reach into the running curing area.

Check whether the web narrows uniformly, one edge draws in, the centre bags, or the change appears only under one lamp or LED segment. Record operator side, centre and drive side rather than one average temperature. A cross-web difference points towards airflow, reflector, cooling or exposure uniformity. A uniform change that rises with thermal load may belong to the material and curing window rather than the web guide.

Separate thermal distortion from ordinary tension movement

Run the approved job at setup speed and normal speed, then include a planned slowdown and restart. Keep the unwind, infeed and rewind settings unchanged during the comparison. If width or repeat changes mainly at low speed, the web is receiving more exposure time per metre unless the system compensates. If the fault follows a tension-zone change but temperature remains stable, inspect that zone before classifying the problem as UV heat.

Retain samples taken before curing, immediately after curing and after cooling. Let them relax under the same conditions before comparison. A hot web can appear dimensionally different and then recover partly, while a heat-sensitive film may retain distortion. Record both states. Do not correct a temporary hot measurement with a permanent tension change that creates wrinkles or register error elsewhere in the roll.

Prove cure without using excess power as the safety margin

Check the finished ink or coating with the approved cure, adhesion, rub, odour or migration procedure required for the job. Record irradiance and dose with a maintained instrument where the process standard uses them, together with lamp or LED identity, wavelength, distance and speed. A dashboard percentage is not a cure result. More power may increase thermal load without correcting an ink, wavelength, film thickness or oxygen-inhibition mismatch.

Flexographic Technical Association guidance notes that heat-sensitive substrates usually need additional cooling with conventional UV, while LED UV transfers less heat and is widely used for shrink films and other sensitive webs. That is an application difference, not proof that every LED setup is cold. The ink or coating must be formulated for the installed wavelength, and the cooling system must still remove heat from the LED array and the web path.

Inspect the complete heat-management route

Check filters, fans, ducts, water flow, coolant temperature, chill-roll contact, quartz screens, reflectors and shutters according to the equipment maintenance procedure. Look for contamination, damaged seals, blocked airflow and alarms that occur only after warm-up. Confirm that the cooling roll is actually controlling the web at the intended wrap and that condensation or contamination is not reducing contact.

IST METZ describes optimised airflow and a quartz-glass screen in its air-cooled narrow-web UV system for managing heat on temperature-sensitive materials. Its reflector guidance also distinguishes cold-mirror reflectors designed to transmit less infrared energy to the substrate. These components work as a system. A clean lamp with a blocked filter, incorrect reflector or poor web contact can still create a thermal problem.

Check speed-linked output, standby and restart behaviour

Verify that UV output follows press speed as the machine specification intends. Test startup, steady production, slowdown, stop and restart while recording temperature and cure. With conventional UV, confirm that shutters close and shield the stationary or slow web during standby. With LED, confirm segment control, dimming and cooling status. Do not bypass an interlock to keep production moving; the original thermal fault can become a substrate or safety failure.

IST METZ states that its web-printing LED systems can switch segments as required and emit a lower proportion of infrared radiation than conventional lamps. Its MBS Li documentation describes a shutter that blocks radiation from the web in standby and electronic power control that can link output to machine speed. Use the actual installed system's manual and service limits, because these functions and settings vary by model and retrofit.

Qualify the film through finishing and storage

After correcting the confirmed cause, run the intended speed range and finished-roll diameter. Recheck width, repeat, register, curl, blocking, winding and the customer's downstream process. For shrink sleeves, include the approved seaming and shrink test. For wrap-around or in-mould work, verify the actual application and dimensional tolerance after the web has cooled and rested for the specified time.

Save the substrate batch, thickness, width, print and coating sequence, UV or LED unit, ink system, measured irradiance and dose, speed, web-temperature map, cooling settings, tension profile and accepted samples. Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about uv-curing thermal control for heat-sensitive films

Why does shrink film distort more when the press slows down?

The web can receive more exposure time per metre at lower speed unless output is compensated. Record temperature and cure through slowdown and restart before changing tension.

Does UV LED eliminate heat problems on thin film?

No. LED UV generally sends less infrared energy towards the web, but the LED array and process still require correct cooling, wavelength matching, distance, dose and web handling.

Should I reduce UV power when film narrows after curing?

Only after measuring cure and temperature. Lower power may reduce heat but can leave ink or coating under-cured if the real problem is cooling, speed compensation or system condition.

What temperatures should be measured across a UV curing unit?

Use the equipment and substrate suppliers' approved method at defined operator-side, centre and drive-side positions before curing, after curing and after cooling. There is no universal limit for every film.

What records help diagnose UV heat distortion?

Record substrate and batch, width and repeat, ink system, lamp or LED identity, irradiance and dose, press speed, cross-web temperatures, cooling status, tension zones and samples before and after curing.
