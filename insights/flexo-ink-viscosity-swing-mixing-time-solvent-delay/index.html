Ink Mixing and Viscosity Control

Flexo Ink Viscosity Keeps Swinging? Check Mixing Time Before Changing the Setpoint

Diagnose unstable flexo ink viscosity by checking agitation, circulation, solvent addition, mixing delay and temperature before changing the setpoint.

2 September 20268 min readRoyal Coat

The viscosity trend rises above target, the controller adds make-up fluid, and the reading then falls too far. A few minutes later it starts again. Changing the setpoint may hide the pattern for a short time, but it does not correct an ink loop that is poorly mixed or a control delay that is shorter than the circulation time. Start with the trend, the pail and the return flow. The controller needs a representative measurement and enough time for each addition to mix before it decides whether another correction is required.

Read the trend before changing any control value

Save a viscosity and temperature trend for one affected station. Mark the job start, speed changes, ink additions, fresh-ink top-ups, cleaning activity and every manual intervention. A repeated rise-and-fall pattern after each automatic addition suggests a different problem from a slow drift during the complete run. The first calls for a review of dosing and mixing. The second may follow normal fluid loss, temperature movement or a gradual change in the ink inventory.

Compare the displayed value with a correctly taken cup reading at a stable point in the run. Use the same clean cup, ink-sample location, temperature and timing method used for the approved job standard. The cup check is a process cross-check, not a reason to chase every small difference between two measurement methods.

Do not change the setpoint, alarm band and solvent delay together. Record the present values and alter one controlled parameter at a time. Otherwise an apparently improved trend will not show which correction worked.

Make sure the ink is homogeneous before judging the sensor

InkSpec notes that many inks and coatings are non-Newtonian and may show thixotropic behaviour. Their measured flow condition depends partly on shear and shear history. Ink left with inadequate agitation can therefore appear heavier or behave differently from the same ink after it has circulated and mixed properly.

Inspect the pail while the station is running. Look for pigment or heavier material settling, a quiet zone away from the suction and return, surface skin, foam, or a return stream that short-circuits directly back to the pump. Check whether the mixer is actually turning the complete inventory rather than only creating movement at the centre.

The pump type matters to the mixing assessment. InkSpec explains that centrifugal pumps historically provided agitation as they circulated ink, while diaphragm and peristaltic pumps may not provide the same mixing action. This does not make either pump type unsuitable. It means the installation may need a separate mixer or a different return arrangement to keep the pail uniform.

Match the solvent delay to the real circulation loop

An automatic viscosity loop measures the ink, compares the value with the setpoint, adds make-up fluid when required and then allows that addition to mix. InkSpec calls this final interval mixing time or solvent delay. The delay prevents the controller from ordering another addition before the first one has reached the measured ink and become evenly distributed.

Establish the actual response for each station. The pail volume, hose length and diameter, pump output, chamber or pan volume, return route and ink level all affect how long an addition takes to reach a representative condition. A preset copied from a smaller pail or shorter circuit can cause repeated additions while the first dose is still travelling through the system.

Test the delay under a controlled job condition. Make one known addition through the normal valve, observe the trend and record the time until the reading reaches a new stable level. Use the equipment supplier's commissioning procedure when adjusting the control. The correct result is a stable response without repeated overshoot, not the shortest possible delay.

Check the addition point, dose and make-up fluid

The make-up fluid must suit the ink system and the approved formulation. Solvent, amine solution or water should not be treated as interchangeable. Confirm the fluid identity, concentration, supply pressure and valve condition before blaming the viscosity sensor for a large correction.

Inspect where the valve discharges. The addition should enter a part of the circuit where it can disperse through the ink inventory before the next control decision. A concentrated addition striking the sensor path can create a temporary low reading, while an addition placed in a poorly mixed corner may take too long to appear. Review the location with the system supplier rather than moving it without an application check.

Verify that the valve delivers repeatable increments and closes cleanly. A leaking valve continues thinning the ink after the controller has ended the command. An oversized dose can also move the process beyond the setpoint even when the delay is correct. Keep valve tests and maintenance in the station record.

Separate temperature movement from fluid loss

A viscosity number without temperature can be misleading. InkSpec's technology measures viscosity and temperature so the operator can see both conditions. If the ink warms during circulation, press acceleration or a long run, its measured viscosity may move even when no material has been lost. Solvent or amine evaporation can act at the same time, so the two trends need to be read together.

Record the start-up temperature, stable running temperature and any station-to-station difference. If viscosity swings follow temperature cycling, inspect the ink-temperature control, chilled-water supply, heat exchanger and pressroom conditions before increasing automatic additions. A solvent correction cannot repair an unstable temperature loop.

Bring prepared ink close to its approved running condition before enabling automatic control. InkSpec recommends allowing the ink to circulate, confirming the colour or cup reference and then setting the control point. Automatic control is better at maintaining a known condition than correcting a poorly prepared pail during the first metres of the job.

Release the loop with evidence from a production run

After correction, run the station at normal production speed and retain the viscosity-temperature trend. Check colour, density or the job's approved print criterion at planned intervals. Confirm that automatic additions are spaced sensibly, the return remains mixed and the process stays inside the agreed control band without repeated manual intervention.

Save the ink, setpoint, temperature range, pail volume, pump and mixer arrangement, solvent-delay value, valve setting and stable trend against the job or ink family. Do not assume one delay suits every station. Different hose routes, pail sizes and circulation volumes can produce different response times on the same press.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about ink mixing and viscosity control

Why does automatic viscosity control keep adding too much solvent?

Check whether the first addition has enough time to circulate and mix before the controller decides again. Also inspect valve leakage, dose size, addition location, pail agitation and temperature movement.

What is solvent delay in a viscosity-control system?

It is the mixing interval after a controlled addition. The delay allows the make-up fluid to reach a representative condition before the next control decision.

Can the circulation pump mix the ink without a separate mixer?

It depends on the pump, pail and return arrangement. InkSpec notes that centrifugal pumps can provide agitation, while diaphragm and peristaltic installations may need separate mixing to keep the ink homogeneous.

Should the viscosity setpoint be changed when the trend oscillates?

Not as the first response. Preserve the approved setpoint and check mixing, circulation time, addition control and temperature. Change only one controlled parameter at a time.

What records help diagnose viscosity hunting?

Keep the viscosity and temperature trend, ink and make-up fluid, pail volume, pump and mixer arrangement, hose route, valve location, dose setting, solvent delay, speed changes and manual interventions.
