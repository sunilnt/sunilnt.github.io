Flexographic Plate Cleaning and Storage

Flexo Plate Prints Dirty After Storage? Check Cleaning and Drying Before Adding Impression

Diagnose repeat-job plate contamination, cracking, curl and poor ink transfer by controlling cleaning chemistry, drying, handling, identification and storage.

16 November 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A repeat flexo job can start with filled reverses, weak ink transfer or more dot gain even though the same plates printed correctly on the previous run. Extra impression may hide the problem for a few metres, but it also changes the print condition and risks damaging the plate. Before altering the press recipe, inspect how the plates were cleaned, dried, demounted, identified and stored. A plate returned to press with dried ink, trapped cleaning fluid, surface contamination or storage distortion is no longer in the same condition as the approved plate from the earlier job.

Compare the stored plate with the approved print

Keep the first failed pull beside the approved retained sample from the earlier run. Mark the colour station, plate ID, print direction and defect location. Then inspect the plate under controlled light and magnification. Look for dried ink in fine reverses, blocked highlight areas, shiny contamination, cracking, swollen patches, curled edges and damage around the plate joint. If the fault follows one plate when it is moved or remounted, treat the plate condition as evidence before changing ink or impression.

Confirm that the correct plate revision has been issued. Match the plate ID, artwork revision, colour, cylinder or sleeve, mounting coordinates and tape specification to the job record. A similar-looking plate stored in the wrong envelope can create a convincing process fault. Photograph the plate face and back before cleaning again so the production and prepress teams can compare the observed condition with the storage record.

Clean the plate before the ink becomes difficult to remove

Miraclon advises cleaning plates as soon as possible after the run because dried solvent-ink or water-based ink residue becomes harder to remove. The extra scrubbing or stronger chemistry then needed can damage the plate. Move the plate from press to cleaning without allowing ink to dry in the fine image areas. If immediate cleaning is impossible, follow the plate supplier's approved interim method rather than leaving the plate exposed beside the press.

Use the cleaner, concentration and contact time approved for the actual plate and ink system. DuPont warns that an incompatible cleaner can soften, swell or deteriorate a photopolymer plate. Do not replace a controlled cleaning method with whichever solvent is nearest to the press. Record the plate family, ink type, cleaner name, dilution where applicable and cleaning time so that a later print change can be traced to a real process condition.

Use a soft tool and controlled pressure

Aggressive rubbing can damage fine dots and plate shoulders before the plate looks visibly worn. Miraclon recommends gentle manual cleaning with a soft horsehair brush or soft sponge for its referenced plate system, while DuPont advises a moist soft sponge or lint-free material and cautions against abrasive fabrics. Follow the guidance for the supplied plate rather than adopting one brush or cloth for every material in the plate room.

Work methodically across the plate instead of scrubbing only the obvious dirty area. Pay attention to fine reverse text, screens and edges where ink remains trapped. Rinse as required by the approved cleaning procedure so that loosened ink and cleaning residue are removed rather than redistributed. Inspect the tool itself: a dirty sponge, hardened brush or contaminated automatic-cleaner filter can return debris to a plate that has just been cleaned.

Dry completely before stacking or packing

A plate that feels dry at the edge may still hold cleaning fluid in the image or back surface. DuPont notes that storing plates dry helps avoid problems associated with solvent entrapment, including misregister, embossing or cracking. Miraclon likewise instructs users to dry plates completely before storage or stacking. Give the cleaned plate a defined drying stage on a clean support rather than placing it immediately into an envelope or beneath another plate.

Do not use uncontrolled local heat to accelerate drying. Protect the plate from dust while air can reach both surfaces, and verify that the face, back and edges are dry before packing. If plates are mounted later with adhesive tape, residue or retained fluid on the back can also weaken contact and encourage edge lift. Record cleaning and packing times when the drying interval is important to the repeat-job standard.

Store plates flat, protected and easy to identify

DuPont's plate-storage guidance calls for clean, dry plates protected from temperature extremes, ambient ultraviolet light, ozone sources and excess weight. Its technical tip recommends flat storage with foam interleaving and limits stack height to 15 cm for the referenced plate range. Use the exact conditions specified by the plate supplier, but keep the principle consistent: no uncontrolled light, heat, electrical discharge, heavy uneven stacks or tight rolling that can distort the printing surface.

Use a rigid envelope, drawer or rack that supports the plate without a fold or point load. Separate plates so one relief image cannot emboss another. Label the package with customer, job, revision, colour, plate ID, first-use date, last cleaning date and condition status. Store rejected or damaged plates separately. A plate marked only with a job name is too easy to mix with an obsolete revision or a plate awaiting assessment.

Inspect again before mounting the repeat job

Open the package in the mounting area and inspect the plate before tape is exposed. Check both surfaces for dust, oil, cleaner residue, curl, cracks and edge damage. Confirm the sleeve or cylinder is clean, dry and within the approved condition, then use the saved mounting coordinates and tape specification. Do not stretch a curled plate flat with hand tension or compensate for a distorted edge with extra adhesive patches.

Mount one controlled plate first when storage condition is in doubt. Verify joint and edge contact, then compare the first pull with the retained sample at the approved kiss impression, ink condition and speed. If the plate still prints differently, keep the press variables stable long enough to separate plate condition, mounting, ink transfer and mechanical causes. Several simultaneous corrections can produce an acceptable sheet without identifying what must be fixed before the next repeat.

Build a plate history instead of relying on memory

For each plate set, record the plate type, ID, artwork revision, ink system, press runs, cleaning method, cleaner batch where useful, drying and storage dates, mounting tape, sleeve or cylinder, observed damage and final disposition. Attach photographs of recurring faults and the accepted print. A simple history shows whether the same plate, cleaner, storage position or handling step is linked to repeat-job variation.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about flexographic plate cleaning and storage

Why does a stored flexo plate print differently on the next run?

Dried ink, incompatible cleaner, retained fluid, contamination, physical damage, storage distortion or the wrong plate revision can change the result. Confirm plate condition before altering the approved press recipe.

Should a dirty plate be cleaned with a stronger solvent?

Not without plate-supplier approval. An incompatible or aggressive cleaner can soften, swell or damage photopolymer. Use the approved chemistry, tool, concentration and contact time.

Can flexo plates be packed while slightly damp?

They should be completely dry before storage or stacking. Retained cleaning fluid can contribute to distortion, cracking and inconsistent mounting or print performance.

Should flexo plates be stored rolled?

Follow the specific plate supplier's instructions. For the DuPont guidance cited here, plates are stored clean, dry and flat with protective interleaving rather than tightly rolled or heavily stacked.

What information helps diagnose a repeat-job plate problem?

Provide plate and revision IDs, ink and cleaner details, cleaning and drying records, storage method, tape and sleeve references, press settings, accepted previous print and the first failed sample.
