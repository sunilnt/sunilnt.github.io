Anilox Care & Cleaning

How to Clean and Maintain Anilox Rolls Safely

A practical routine for cleaning, inspecting and storing ceramic anilox rolls while protecting cell volume, surface condition and print repeatability.

11 August 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A dirty anilox usually appears first as a colour or transfer problem. Operators may add ink, change viscosity or increase impression even though dried residue has reduced the working cell volume. Clean before deposits harden, use chemistry that suits both the ink and roller construction, and verify the result instead of judging the surface by eye. Handling matters too; one impact on a ceramic surface can turn a cleaning job into a roller repair.

Clean before residue becomes a deep-cleaning problem

The easiest deposit to remove is the one that has not dried inside the cell. Build cleaning into every ink change, planned stop and end-of-job procedure. If ink circulation is interrupted, do not allow the anilox surface to continue drying while attention moves elsewhere on the press.

Dried ink, varnish, adhesive or coating can demand a more aggressive process than fresh residue. That increases cleaning time and creates more opportunity for incompatible chemistry or rough handling to damage the roller. A documented shutdown sequence helps every shift isolate the ink system, keep the surface wet where appropriate and begin cleaning consistently.

  • Define who starts the cleaning cycle during a press stop
  • Record the correct cleaner for each ink or coating family
  • Prevent the chamber and blade system from running dry
  • Rinse before residues from the cleaner can dry on the surface
  • Inspect before the roller returns to production or storage

Match the cleaner to the ink and roller construction

There is no universal cleaning chemical for every ink, coating and roller. The selected product must be suitable for the material being removed and for the roller's ceramic surface, base construction, end components and any exposed metal. Always follow the cleaner supplier's dilution, temperature, contact-time, rinsing and personal-protection instructions.

Zecher's care guidance warns that strongly acidic products can be unsuitable for steel rollers, while strongly alkaline products can attack aluminium structures. The safe operating window therefore depends on the complete component, not only the visible ceramic engraving. When changing chemistry, confirm compatibility with the roller and the press-cleaning system before standardising the new procedure.

Use a repeatable daily cleaning sequence

Begin with the press manufacturer's isolation and safety procedure. Remove the ink or coating, circulate the approved cleaning solution as specified, and give it enough time to work without exceeding the supplier's limits. Where manual attention is required, use tools intended for anilox cells rather than improvised brushes, metal scrapers or abrasive pads.

After cleaning, rinse thoroughly with clean water so that detergent and loosened contamination are not left in the cells. Dry the surface with a soft, clean and absorbent material, then inspect it under consistent lighting. The roller should not be returned to the press simply because the wash cycle has finished; cleanliness and surface condition should be confirmed first.

Know when routine cleaning is no longer enough

If a correctly prepared ink repeatedly produces lower density than the approved standard, blocked cells may have reduced the effective volume. Other warning signs include uneven transfer across the web, recurring colour adjustment and deposits that remain visible after the normal cleaning cycle. These symptoms should trigger measurement and inspection rather than progressively harsher manual cleaning.

External cleaning options include purpose-designed washing systems, ultrasonic processes and laser cleaning. The correct method depends on the roller construction, engraving, contamination, dimensions and supplier recommendations. Zecher describes laser cleaning as a controlled way to remove ink, varnish and other residues without chemical cleaning agents, but process settings and handling still need to be matched to the specific roller.

Verify cleanliness instead of judging by appearance alone

A roller can look clean to the naked eye while contamination remains near the bottom of its cells. Use a suitable microscope or inspection device to compare representative positions across the face. Where volume measurement is available, record the measured condition against the roller's identity and approved baseline.

Trend data is more useful than an isolated reading. A cleaning register can record the jobs run, ink or coating type, cleaning method, inspection result, measured volume where available and any surface damage. This helps the team separate contamination from wear and decide whether the roller needs further cleaning, repair assessment or replacement.

  • Check the operator and drive sides as well as the centre
  • Look for blocked cells, scoring, chips and edge damage
  • Use the same inspection method for meaningful comparisons
  • Track cleaning history against print-performance changes
  • Escalate unexplained volume loss for specialist assessment

Protect the roller during handling and storage

Cleaning is only one part of anilox care. A clean engraving can still be damaged by contact with hard tools, blade fragments, jewellery, unprotected lifting equipment or another roller. Use designated lifting and transport equipment, protect journals and edges, and keep the engraved surface away from impact points.

Store rolls and sleeves in a clean, dry location using supports designed for their format and weight. Fit suitable covers only after the surface is dry and inspected. Identification should remain visible so operators can select the intended volume and line count without unnecessary handling or trial installation.

Turn anilox care into a pressroom standard

A useful standard identifies the approved cleaner, concentration, contact time, tools, rinsing method, inspection points and escalation criteria for each ink system. Train every shift to the same sequence and review the record when print density or transfer begins to move. This makes maintenance preventive rather than a response to a failed job.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about anilox care & cleaning

How often should an anilox roll be cleaned?

Routine cleaning should be part of ink changes, production interruptions and end-of-job procedures so residue does not dry in the cells. The interval for deeper cleaning should be based on inspection, measured volume, materials run and the roller supplier's guidance.

Can a wire brush be used on a ceramic anilox roll?

Use only tools and materials specifically approved for the roller and cleaning chemistry. Improvised or overly aggressive mechanical methods can damage the micro-structured surface, so the roller supplier's care instructions should control the choice.

How can a printer tell whether cells are blocked or worn?

Inspect the engraving under magnification and compare measured cell volume with the roller's known baseline where possible. If approved cleaning restores volume or transfer, contamination was a major factor; persistent loss or visible surface damage needs specialist assessment.

Is laser cleaning suitable for every anilox roll?

Laser cleaning can be effective for ceramic and chrome anilox surfaces when the equipment and settings are appropriate, but compatibility should be confirmed for the specific roller, engraving, contamination and component construction before treatment.
