Printed-Film Scratch Diagnosis in Slitting and Rewinding

Printed Film Gets Scratched on the Slitter-Rewinder? Map the First Contact Point Before Changing Tension

Trace scratches added during slitting and rewinding by mapping their direction, web side, lane and first contact point before changing tension or speed.

21 November 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A printed or coated web can enter the slitter looking acceptable and leave with a fine scratch, haze line or scuffed lane. The mark may be continuous, intermittent or visible only under angled light. Do not begin by reducing tension across the whole job. First prove that the damage is being added on the finishing line, identify which side of the web is affected and find the earliest point where the mark appears. Direction, lane and frequency then turn a general complaint into a short list of physical contacts to inspect.

Prove where the scratch enters the process

Stop on a representative fault and mark machine direction, operator side, drive side, printed side and repeat position. Keep an untouched sample from the parent roll, a sample after unwind and guiding, and a finished sample from the affected lane. Inspect all three under the same angled light and with the same magnification. If the mark is already present on the parent roll, hold the slitter settings and trace the earlier printing, coating, laminating, storage or handling stage.

If the incoming sample is clean, move the inspection point downstream in safe, accessible steps. A removable witness strip or approved inspection sample taken after a section can help locate the first appearance. Never reach into a moving web path. Follow the machine's isolation and sampling procedure, and restart from a known condition after every check. The objective is to identify the first contact that can create the mark, not merely the place where the operator first notices it.

Use direction, lane and frequency as physical clues

A straight mark in the machine direction that stays in one lane usually points towards a fixed cross-web position: a stationary contaminant, rough roller zone, dragging idler, guide surface, sensor bracket, knife-area contact or another part touching that side of the web. Flexographic Technical Association troubleshooting guidance uses the direction and recurrence of a streak to narrow the source and specifically includes a stopped and dragging idler or a dragging substrate among the checks.

Measure the interval when the mark repeats. A repeat matching a roller circumference moves that roller high on the list; a mark tied to acceleration, deceleration or one diameter range suggests a changing web-to-roller relationship. A diagonal or wandering mark may follow web tracking rather than a single damaged lane. Record the position from a stable datum such as the web edge, printed image or slit line instead of describing it only as 'near the middle'.

Trace both web faces through the complete path

Draw the actual threading route for the current job and note which web face touches each roller, bar, table and nip. Include the unwind, splice table, guide sensor and actuator, inspection section, slitting section, waste handling, lay-on contact and rewind. Check clearances at guards, cable routes, vacuum slots, edge devices and temporary tools. A route that is safe for a paper labelstock may expose a delicate metallised, varnished or clear-film surface to a contact it cannot tolerate.

Clean only with the approved material and method for each surface. Look for dried adhesive, ink, coating, metal burrs, blade fragments, paper dust, damaged roller covering, sharp tape edges and hardened deposits. Run a clean lint-free witness across a stationary accessible surface only where the maintenance procedure permits it. Do not polish a roller or change its covering on the assumption that smoother is always better; surface finish, traction and release must suit the substrate and machine design.

Check roller rotation before changing the recipe

With the line isolated, confirm that the relevant idlers rotate freely and evenly, bearings are sound, shafts are aligned and roller faces are clean and undamaged. During an authorised guarded test, observe whether each roller starts and follows the web through acceleration and deceleration. FTA guidance warns that roller inertia at higher speed can create relative motion between a sensitive web and the roller surface; that slip can cause scratches or tracking errors before the defect is immediately obvious.

Compare the affected run with a retained good job using the same material family. Record speed, acceleration ramp, tension zones, wrap angles, nip or lay-on condition, roll diameter and which face contacts the roller. Change one approved variable at a time. Lowering tension may reduce one contact force but can also introduce flutter, tracking error or wrinkles. Raising tension can mask slack while increasing contact against the same damaging surface.

Separate a true scratch from scuffing or an uncured surface

Inspect whether material has been cut from the surface, displaced, polished or transferred onto another surface. Compare the line under magnification and perform the plant's approved rub, adhesion or cure test on both marked and unmarked areas. A mechanically scratched film, a scuffed varnish, ink transfer from incomplete drying and pressure marking inside the finished roll can look similar in normal light but require different corrections.

If the mark becomes visible only after rewinding or storage, open samples from the outside, middle and near the core. Review ink or coating cure, residual solvent where relevant, coefficient of friction, winding pressure and roll temperature with the approved suppliers and test methods. Do not use a stronger nip or harder roll as the first response to a surface that may be blocking or transferring under pressure.

Release the job with a contact map and retained samples

After correcting the confirmed source, test through startup, normal speed, a planned slowdown and the intended finished diameter. Inspect every slit lane and both web faces, not only the original fault lane. Retain accepted and failed samples with the threading diagram, contact point, roller or component identification, cleaning or repair performed, speed, tensions and final surface test. This gives maintenance and the next shift evidence that the fault was removed rather than hidden by a temporary setting.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about printed-film scratch diagnosis in slitting and rewinding

Why does printed film scratch only at higher slitter speed?

Higher speed can change roller acceleration, web tracking and relative motion between the web and a roller surface. Map the first marked point and observe the relevant roller through the speed change before altering several settings.

Does a continuous machine-direction scratch mean the slitter knife is damaged?

Not necessarily. A fixed contaminant, rough roller lane, dragging idler, guide surface or another stationary contact can produce the same direction. Locate the first appearance and measure its cross-web position.

Should I reduce web tension when the film is scuffed?

Only as a controlled trial after the contact point is known. Less tension can reduce contact force but may introduce flutter, wrinkles or tracking error, while leaving the damaging surface unchanged.

How can I tell a scratch from ink or varnish transfer?

Use magnification and the approved rub, adhesion or cure test on marked and unmarked areas. Also inspect the contacting roller and samples from different depths of the finished roll.

What information should be recorded for a finishing-line scratch?

Record the affected web face and lane, direction, repeat interval, first contact point, threading route, material and surface construction, speed, tension zones, roller IDs, finished diameter and retained accepted and failed samples.
