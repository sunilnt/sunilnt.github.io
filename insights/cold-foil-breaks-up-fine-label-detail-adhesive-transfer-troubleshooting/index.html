Cold-Foil Label Finishing Troubleshooting

Cold Foil Breaks Up on Fine Label Detail? Separate Adhesive Laydown From Foil Release

Diagnose incomplete cold-foil transfer by checking the artwork, substrate surface, adhesive image, cure, nip and foil path before changing several settings at once.

19 November 20267 min readRoyal Coat

Fine text, lines or screened areas can leave a cold-foil unit with broken metallic coverage even while larger shapes look acceptable. The fault may begin in the artwork, the substrate surface, the printed adhesive, the curing stage, the transfer nip or the foil web itself. Treating every missing area as a pressure problem usually hides the evidence. First mark exactly where transfer is lost, then compare the adhesive image and the released foil at the same position. That comparison shows which part of the process should be changed.

Map the failure before touching the settings

Stop on a representative repeat and mark the machine direction, operator side and drive side. Photograph the accepted and failed areas under the same light. Record the substrate, print or coating beneath the foil, adhesive batch, foil identification, line speed, web tension, cure setting and nip condition. Note whether the defect appears only in fine detail, only in solids, at one side of the web or at a fixed position around the repeat.

Keep three samples together: the finished label, the adhesive image before transfer if it can be inspected safely, and the spent foil after release. A clean gap in the adhesive image points upstream. A complete adhesive image with patchy metallic transfer moves the check towards cure, contact, release and the foil path. A fault that repeats in one physical position may indicate contamination, damage or a local contact problem rather than an artwork-wide limitation.

Confirm that the artwork suits the process

Review the smallest line, reversed detail, screen and gap against the approved capability for the complete cold-foil process. Do not approve fine metallic detail from a PDF view alone. The adhesive must print as a controlled image, hold its intended shape through cure and contact the foil without filling adjacent gaps. Output a dedicated test element when the job pushes the present process window, then retain the accepted sample with the job data.

Check trapping and sequence when the foil will be overprinted or protected by a later coating. FINAT's technical report describes cold foil as primarily using metallised foils that may be overprinted to obtain the required colour. That means the finished appearance depends on more than metallic transfer. The foil, later ink or coating, substrate and final use must be qualified together instead of accepting the foil stage in isolation.

Prove the substrate and printed surface first

Run the fault on an unprinted area and on the actual printed or coated construction. Look for silicone, release material, spray, dust, set-off, wax, oil or an incompatible coating in the failed position. If the same foil and adhesive transfer on one construction but fail on another, hold the machine settings and investigate the surface before increasing nip load or cure energy.

Surface condition can change between extrusion, printing, storage and finishing. Flexographic Technical Association guidance on filmic substrates recommends checking surface energy before printing, before applying adhesive and again before coating. Follow the substrate and adhesive suppliers' current methods and limits; a dyne result by itself does not prove chemical compatibility. Record roll age, treatment history and the side of the web tested so the comparison can be repeated.

Inspect the adhesive image and cure as one system

Verify that the specified adhesive, printing form, anilox or metering arrangement and cure route are installed for the job. Mix, condition and handle the adhesive exactly as its supplier requires. Check whether the adhesive image is weak in the same fine areas that lose foil, whether it spreads beyond the intended shape or whether it changes across the web. Clean the printing and metering components by the approved method before deciding that more adhesive is needed.

Change cure only within the adhesive and equipment supplier's approved window. Too little or unsuitable cure can leave the transfer layer without the required state at the nip; excessive or incorrectly matched exposure can also move the process outside its window. Record the actual lamp or LED unit, approved output check, web speed and any cooling condition. Do not substitute a dashboard percentage for a maintained measurement or for a finished-job adhesion test.

Check the transfer nip and foil path without adding force blindly

Inspect the nip surfaces, bearings, alignment and approved pressure-setting method. Look for contamination, wear, damage or a cross-web contact difference. Use a controlled test element across the usable width if the equipment procedure allows it. When one side transfers and the other does not, map the width first. Increasing the complete nip load to cover one weak lane can overload the accepted lanes and distort fine adhesive detail.

Trace the foil web from unwind to waste rewind. Confirm the correct side, stable tension, alignment and clean contact through the transfer zone. Watch for wrinkles, flutter, steering corrections, a damaged foil edge or waste rewind behaviour that changes as diameter builds. PRATI lists cold foiling among its label and packaging finishing processes and provides configurable lamination and foil-handling arrangements. The correct configuration still has to be matched to the web width, substrate, foil and job sequence.

Qualify the finished label and save the process window

Approve more than visual coverage at the machine. Check rub and tape response where relevant, overprint or coating adhesion, rewinding, die-cutting and the label's final application. Fold, curve or handle the sample as the real pack will be handled. Use the brand owner's approved test and acceptance standard for the end use; a bright sample at the transfer nip is not evidence that the complete label will survive converting and use.

Save the accepted artwork limits, substrate and surface condition, adhesive and foil identifications, metering setup, cure check, nip method, speed, tensions and finished tests. Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about cold-foil label finishing troubleshooting

Why does cold foil transfer in solids but break up in fine text?

Fine detail may expose an artwork, plate, adhesive laydown, cure, nip-contact or foil-release limit that a larger solid can tolerate. Compare the adhesive image and spent foil at the same repeat before changing pressure.

Should I increase nip pressure when cold foil has pinholes?

Not until surface condition, adhesive coverage, cure, alignment and the foil path have been checked. More load can hide one weak lane while distorting accepted detail or increasing wear.

Does a dyne test prove that cold foil will adhere?

No. It is one surface-energy check. The actual substrate, ink or coating, adhesive, foil, cure route and finished-use test still have to be qualified together.

What samples help diagnose incomplete cold-foil transfer?

Keep the finished label, the adhesive image before transfer where it can be inspected safely, the spent foil, and accepted and failed samples marked with direction and repeat position.

What should a cold-foil job record contain?

Record artwork limits, substrate and surface checks, adhesive and foil IDs, metering setup, cure verification, nip-setting method, speed, tensions and the finished acceptance tests.
