Rotary Die-Cutting Pressure Control

Die-Cutting Pressure Rises During the Run? Record the Gauge Before Adjusting the Station

Control rotary die-cutting pressure drift by logging warm and cold readings, cut quality, material, speed and tool condition before adjusting the station.

23 September 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A rotary die-cutting station can produce an acceptable kiss cut at startup and begin marking the liner later in the roll. If the operator responds by repeatedly backing off one side or adding pressure elsewhere, the original setting is lost and the tool condition remains unknown. Keep the failed web, record the gauge reading and establish whether pressure changed as the station warmed before touching the adjustment.

Confirm that the cut changed during the same run

Mark samples from startup, normal production and the point where the fault appeared. Identify the operator side, drive side, machine direction, speed, roll position and elapsed running time. Check the face material, adhesive and liner under the same light. A cut that gradually deepens through one roll needs a different investigation from a missing cut that returns at one cavity on every repeat.

Record the die, magnetic cylinder, anvil, station, laminate batch and pressure reading at each sample. If gauges are fitted on both sides, write down both values. Do not record only the final number after the operator has corrected the cut. The cold startup and warm running values are the evidence needed to understand the movement.

Confirm that the material did not change at a splice and that tension, speed and matrix path remained stable. Measure the laminate and liner using the plant's maintained method where required. Pressure drift should not be blamed for a construction change or a damaged section of web that entered the station at the same time.

Use the lightest pressure that produces the approved cut

Start a new or remounted die at low pressure using the press manufacturer's safe setup procedure. Increase in small, even steps until the face stock is cut, the label releases as required and the liner remains within the approved condition. Keep that first acceptable sample and the two gauge readings with the job record.

Wilson Manufacturing advises using the lightest pressure that achieves the necessary cut. Its pressure-gauge guidance warns that excess load can damage tooling and other station components. More pressure is therefore not a reserve to be added whenever one label fails. It is another process variable that must be justified by the cut result.

If one side needs materially more load than the other, stop and inspect before treating the difference as normal. Check die seating, magnetic-cylinder cleanliness, anvil condition, bearer contact, bearing housings and cross-web material thickness. A pressure gauge helps reveal the imbalance, but it does not identify which mechanical or material condition created it.

Watch what happens as the tooling warms

Wilson notes that friction between rotating components generates heat, the steel expands and cutting pressure can rise beyond the initial setting. That is why a pressure value taken immediately after setup cannot represent a complete production run. The station needs a second check after it reaches its normal operating condition.

Agree a fixed check point, such as after the first approved roll and again after a defined running period. Record speed, room condition where relevant, gauge readings and a labelled cut sample. Use the same timing on repeat jobs. The purpose is to see a trend, not to collect isolated numbers that were taken under different conditions.

If pressure rises while the cut becomes deeper, return only to the approved running setting using the maintained procedure. Do not keep compensating for continued movement. Inspect lubrication, bearings, gears, support rolls, anvil and the tool installation according to the machine and tooling instructions. An unexplained increase is a maintenance signal, not a new standard.

Interpret pressure spikes separately from slow drift

A slow change from the cold reading is different from a gauge needle that jumps at one part of the revolution. Wilson states that large pressure spikes can indicate a die bouncing or lifting from the anvil when it meets high resistance, often around a long horizontal cut. Mark the angular position and keep several consecutive repeats so the pattern can be matched to the die layout.

Increase load only in small increments and only within the maintained setup method. If the spike remains, inspect die geometry, mounting, magnetic hold, the anvil and station condition rather than forcing the tool through the high-load area. Excess pressure applied to quiet one section can increase wear around the complete cylinder.

Record the difference between a steady value, a slow warm-up increase and a repeating peak. These three conditions should not share one troubleshooting note called high pressure. The description determines whether the next action is a controlled running adjustment, a tool inspection or a mechanical check.

Make the pressure reading part of the repeat-job record

Save the cold startup reading, warm running reading, left-to-right balance, speed, laminate code, die ID, cylinder and anvil IDs, and approved sample. Add the finishing result: label release, liner mark, matrix behaviour and any pressure spike. The next operator then starts from verified evidence instead of reconstructing the station from memory.

Wilson recommends tracking starting and ending pressure because it can shorten the next setup. It also notes that an increasing ending pressure may indicate the tool is approaching the need for resharpening or the end of its useful life. Treat this as a trend across comparable jobs, not as a stand-alone rejection limit. The die supplier should assess the tool using its history and physical condition.

Rotometal describes rotoSET Control as a gauge for adjusting and monitoring pressure on rotary tools, with the aim of repeatable setup and avoiding excessive force. Whatever measuring system is used, identify its units, range, calibration or verification status and exact installation. Numbers from different gauges or stations should not be compared without that context.

Escalate with samples and a complete station history

When the acceptable setting is narrowing, send the tooling or equipment supplier more than a photograph of one failed label. Include the die drawing and ID, material construction and batch, tool and anvil history, cold and warm pressure readings, speed, run time, adjustment sequence and marked samples from before and after the fault.

State whether the cut changed across the width, around the repeat or with time. Add photographs of the flexible die back, magnetic-cylinder surface, bearer and anvil where the maintained inspection permits it. If the die needs progressively more pressure on comparable work, include the previous job records so wear can be separated from a one-off material change.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about rotary die-cutting pressure control

Why can die-cutting pressure increase after startup?

Wilson Manufacturing notes that friction generates heat and steel components expand, which can increase pressure beyond the cold setup. Record both cold and warm readings before changing the standard.

Should pressure be added whenever one area does not cut?

No. First map whether the fault follows one die cavity, one side of the web, the material or elapsed running time. Extra load can hide the cause and increase wear.

What can a repeating pressure spike indicate?

Wilson says a large spike can indicate the die bouncing or lifting when it reaches a high-resistance feature, often a long horizontal cut. Inspect the pattern and tooling rather than treating it as slow thermal drift.

What pressure information should be stored for a repeat job?

Keep cold and warm readings from both sides, speed, material and tool IDs, run time, approved samples, liner condition, matrix result and any repeating spike.

Can pressure readings from two presses be compared directly?

Not without confirming the gauge units, range, condition, installation and station design. Store the measuring-system details with each job record.
