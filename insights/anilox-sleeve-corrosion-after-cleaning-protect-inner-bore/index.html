Anilox Sleeve Cleaning

Anilox Sleeve Corroding After Cleaning? Protect the Bore Before the Wash

Prevent dirt, cleaner and corrosion inside an anilox sleeve by sealing the bore, controlling the cleaning method and checking the sleeve before it returns to press.

12 September 20267 min readRoyal Coat

The engraved surface can leave the wash looking clean while the inside of the anilox sleeve comes back wet, stained or difficult to mount. That is not a cosmetic problem. Cleaner, ink and debris that reach the bore or sleeve ends can interfere with mounting and encourage corrosion in areas the operator may not inspect before the next job. Protect the inner body before cleaning, then check the seal, bath, rinse and drying steps as one controlled routine.

Confirm where the contamination is entering

Keep the sleeve out of service until the source is understood. Mark the operator and drive ends, photograph the stain or corrosion and record the cleaner, concentration, temperature, exposure time and equipment used. Check whether the fault is inside the complete bore, concentrated at one end or sitting behind a removable component.

Inspect the end faces, inner sleeve, seals and any cleaning adapter under good light. Dried ink on a mating face can hold a seal open. A cut, flattened seal or adapter that does not match the sleeve can allow the bath to reach the inner construction even when the outside appears properly closed.

Compare another sleeve cleaned in the same cycle. If several sleeves show the same entry point, inspect the adapter and cleaning process. If only one sleeve is affected, record its dimensions and condition before assuming the complete washer is at fault. Do not mount a wet or corroded sleeve to see whether it will run.

Seal the bore with an adapter made for the sleeve

Zecher recommends fitting a sleeve cleaning adapter before each cleaning session to protect the inner surface. Its accessories guidance explains that side seals protect the inner body from dirt and that the adapter has to match the individual sleeve dimensions. A cover that is nearly the right size is not a controlled seal.

Clean the adapter and sleeve mating faces before assembly. Remove every trace of dried ink that could create a path under the rubber seal. Inspect the seal position and condition as it is fitted, rather than tightening the assembly and trusting pressure alone.

Zecher's cleaning guidance states that spring tension can compensate for sleeve expansion in a temperature-controlled bath, but the tension should be only enough to maintain the seal. Excess spring load can create breaking stress in the sleeve. Use the adapter instructions and sleeve dimensions instead of tightening until the end cover cannot move.

Control the cleaner, temperature and exposure time

Identify the ink or coating that has to be removed and use a cleaner approved for the anilox surface, sleeve construction, seals and wash equipment. Zecher's care instructions specify recommended cleaning products in the pH range 5 to 11 and require the chemical manufacturer's directions to be followed. Do not mix cleaners or increase concentration to recover a neglected sleeve without technical approval.

Record bath temperature and contact time for every cycle. Temperature changes can alter both the cleaning action and the way the sleeve and adapter fit. A longer soak is not a substitute for selecting the correct method, and it exposes the sleeve, seal and any vulnerable internal area for longer if the closure is imperfect.

Remove add-on components or bearings when the supplier's procedure requires it. Position the sleeve in the washer as specified so its precision surfaces are supported correctly. A sleeve that rolls, knocks another tool or rests on an unsuitable edge can leave the machine with clean cells and new mechanical damage.

Choose interim cleaning and deep cleaning for different jobs

A controlled interim clean between colours or jobs can keep ink from drying in the cells. Zecher describes a micro-pore sponge for efficient interim cleaning, but also states that wiping does not replace the automatic in-machine cycle or a suitable external cleaning device. Use the light method for the deposit it can remove, not as proof that the complete cell volume has been restored.

When routine in-press cleaning no longer recovers the print result, inspect the roll before repeating aggressive cycles. Zecher lists ultrasonic and dedicated external washing as intensive methods when normal cleaning is insufficient. Suitability still depends on the roller or sleeve construction and the equipment procedure.

Do not run a dry blade chamber merely to polish the surface. Zecher warns that dry running with a blade chamber can lead to scoring and other mechanical damage. If the cells remain blocked, preserve the print sample and cleaning record and choose the next approved cleaning or inspection step.

Rinse, dry and inspect the hidden surfaces

After cleaning, rinse according to the chemical and equipment instructions. Remove the adapter without allowing trapped liquid to run into the bore. Dry the engraved surface, end faces, seal area and inner sleeve completely using the approved method. Pay attention to joints and edges where liquid can remain after the outside looks dry.

Inspect the bore for moisture, residue, staining, swelling or a rough area. Check both ends and compare the feel and appearance with the sleeve's accepted condition. If corrosion or construction damage is present, quarantine the sleeve and ask the manufacturer or authorised service route for an assessment instead of scraping, sanding or forcing it onto a mandrel.

Confirm the engraved surface under suitable magnification and, where the print result requires it, use a documented volume or condition check. Zecher recommends verifying the cleaning result and recording it in a cleaning schedule. The record should identify the sleeve, method, chemical, cycle details, inspection result and person releasing it.

Prove that the sleeve returns to press correctly

Before mounting, inspect the air mandrel and air outlets under the approved lockout procedure. The bore must be clean and dry, and the sleeve should mount with the normal maintained air supply and method. If it hesitates or stops, remove it safely and find the restriction. Levering or striking the sleeve can turn a cleaning fault into a dimensional fault.

Run a controlled check at normal production conditions. Watch for difficult mounting, runout, vibration, gear-side marks and a print-density change across or around the repeat. Keep the first accepted print with the cleaning record. This links the wash result to the job instead of relying only on a visual check at the cleaning station.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about anilox sleeve cleaning

Why is the inside of an anilox sleeve wet after external cleaning?

Check whether the cleaning adapter matches the sleeve, whether its mating faces are clean, and whether the rubber seals are correctly positioned and undamaged. A poor seal can allow cleaner into the bore.

Should the cleaning adapter be tightened as much as possible?

No. Follow the adapter instructions. Zecher states that spring tension should be only enough to maintain the seal because excess load can create breaking stress in the sleeve.

Can manual sponge cleaning replace a machine cleaning cycle?

No. It is useful for interim cleaning, but Zecher states that it does not replace automatic in-machine cleaning or a suitable external cleaning device.

Can a blade chamber be run dry to clean the anilox surface?

Do not use that method. Zecher warns that dry running with a blade chamber can cause scoring and other mechanical damage.

What should be recorded after cleaning an anilox sleeve?

Record the sleeve ID, cleaner and concentration, temperature, cycle time, adapter and seal condition, rinse and drying checks, bore inspection, surface result and the first accepted print after mounting.
