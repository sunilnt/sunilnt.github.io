UV Lamp Replacement Specification

Replacing a UV Lamp? Match the Spectrum and Unit Before Ordering by Length

Specify a replacement UV lamp from the curing unit, lamp marking, electrical data and required spectrum, then inspect the contacts, reflector and cooling path.

24 September 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A failed or weak UV lamp creates pressure to order a replacement quickly. Length is easy to measure, but it is not a complete lamp specification. The curing unit, electrical operating point, lamp marking and emission spectrum must agree with the ink, coating or adhesive being cured. Record that evidence before the old lamp is removed, then inspect the surrounding components so a contact, reflector or cooling fault is not carried into the replacement.

Freeze the evidence before removing the lamp

Photograph the lamp marking, unit nameplate, cassette position, connector arrangement and cable routing before anything is disturbed. Record the press, curing-unit model, serial number, lamp position and accumulated operating hours if the controller provides them. Note whether the fault affects one lamp, one side of the web or every unit on the press.

Keep the production evidence with the maintenance record. Identify the ink or coating, colour, substrate, press speed, power setting and failed cure test. Record any formulation, substrate or speed change separately. A replacement lamp will not correct a mismatch between the chemistry and the curing process.

Do not discard the old lamp until the replacement has been identified and the fault has been reviewed. Follow the press and curing-system procedures for isolation, cooling, handling and disposal. A lamp may retain heat and the unit contains high-voltage components, so removal is a trained maintenance task rather than a production adjustment.

Read the lamp and unit identification, not only the glass length

Transcribe every visible lamp code and compare it with the curing-unit documentation or maintained spare-parts record. Include the overall length, arc length if specified, end fittings, connector type and orientation. Two lamps that appear similar on a bench can have different electrical or spectral requirements and may not be interchangeable.

Use the unit manufacturer and lamp supplier to confirm the complete part reference. Supply photographs and the curing-unit serial number when the marking is incomplete. Do not rely on an old purchase description unless it is traceable to the same unit configuration.

IST METZ states that its UV lamps are produced in a range of lengths and outputs and that the electrical parameters of each lamp are checked. This is useful context, but the published range is not an ordering table. The exact unit and application remain the basis for selecting the replacement.

Confirm the spectrum against the curing chemistry

Ask the ink, coating or adhesive supplier which lamp spectrum the formulation requires. Provide the product name, colour, film weight where known, substrate and press conditions. Do not substitute a lamp because its dimensions fit or because another press uses it. The formulation must absorb the radiation delivered by the installed curing system.

IST METZ describes application-specific lamp spectra for inks, varnishes and adhesives, including standard and doped lamp types with different spectral emphasis. That is why a replacement request should include the original lamp marking and the material being cured. The words mercury lamp or metal-halide lamp alone may not identify the required spectrum.

If the press runs difficult colours, opaque whites, heavy coatings or adhesives, document the full job range. Treat any proposed spectrum change as a controlled process change, with written confirmation and a production trial.

Inspect the contact, reflector and cooling path

A new lamp will not restore output if the optical or electrical path is damaged. With the system isolated and cool, inspect the contacts, holders, reflector, quartz plate where fitted, cooling path and filters. Record oxidation, wear, contamination, damaged insulation, loose parts or blocked airflow before correction.

IST METZ advises checking the unit-side FLC contact surfaces for visible oxidation or wear during a lamp change. It also says an installed lamp should click securely into the FLC contact. That instruction applies to its FLC arrangement; other systems must follow their own manual. A loose or damaged contact requires correction before the unit is energised.

The manufacturer also explains that fine dust on the lamp and reflector can burn in and reduce radiation efficiency. Cleaning intervals depend on the environment and cooling-air condition. Use only the approved cleaning material and handling method. Do not touch optical surfaces with bare hands, and do not improvise with an unapproved solvent or abrasive cloth.

Install the replacement as a controlled maintenance job

Check the delivered lamp against the approved part reference before opening the protective packaging. Compare the marking, fittings, orientation and condition with the purchase record and the removed lamp. If any detail differs, stop and ask the supplier to confirm the change in writing rather than attempting to make the lamp fit.

Use clean gloves and the manufacturer-specified installation procedure. Confirm that all connections and retainers are correctly seated, the reflector or cassette is in its operating position, covers and interlocks are restored, and tools or packing materials have been removed. Record the new lamp code, batch or serial identification where available, installation date and starting hours.

Replace paired or grouped components only when the system documentation requires it. Do not assume that every weak-cure event needs a full set of lamps, and do not mix unverified specifications across positions to use available stock.

Verify cure with the same method used before the change

Start at the maintained commissioning or restart condition and allow the system to reach its specified operating state. Check the unit for alarms, abnormal sound, cooling problems or unstable power before running saleable material. Stop immediately if the lamp does not start correctly or the unit shows an unsafe condition.

Measure the result using the plant's controlled cure check and, where used, a suitable maintained UV instrument. Compare like with like: the same measurement location, instrument, spectral response, power setting and press speed. A higher displayed power percentage is not proof that the correct radiation reached the product.

Run the actual ink or coating on the intended substrate, then complete the approved adhesion, resistance, blocking and customer-specific checks. Keep the sample and readings. If cure remains weak, review the optical path, cooling, power supply, chemistry and measurement method before condemning the new lamp.

Build a spare-lamp record that purchasing can use

Create one controlled spare-lamp record for each curing-unit configuration. Include the manufacturer part reference, unit and press identification, lamp marking, spectrum, fittings, electrical data supplied by the manufacturer, approved supplier, storage requirement and the production materials for which it was confirmed. Attach clear photographs so stores can distinguish similar lamps without unpacking them unnecessarily.

Record each installation and removal with operating hours, reason for change, cure result and condition of the contacts, reflector and filters. This separates normal replacement from recurring faults and prevents unidentified lamps returning to stores.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about uv lamp replacement specification

Can a UV lamp be ordered from its length alone?

No. Confirm the curing unit, original lamp marking, electrical specification, fittings, orientation and required emission spectrum with the manufacturer or approved supplier.

Why does the lamp spectrum matter?

UV inks, coatings and adhesives are formulated to respond to particular radiation ranges. A lamp can fit physically and still be unsuitable for the material being cured.

What should be inspected when an IST FLC lamp is changed?

IST METZ says the unit-side contact surfaces should be checked for visible oxidation or wear and that the lamp should click securely into the FLC contact. Follow the exact unit instructions.

Should the reflector be checked during a lamp replacement?

Yes. Contamination or damage in the optical path can reduce radiation efficiency. Inspect and clean it only by the system manufacturer's approved method.

What information should be kept after the new lamp is installed?

Keep the lamp identification, installation date and hours, unit position, contact and reflector condition, power setting, speed, material details, measurement readings and approved cure samples.
