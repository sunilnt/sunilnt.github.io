Banded Anilox Roll Trial: How to Choose an Engraving From Press Results

Plan a banded anilox roll trial with controlled ink, plate, substrate, speed and measurements so the selected engraving works in production.

25 August 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A banded anilox trial can save a converter from ordering a complete roller set on the strength of one specification. It can also produce a misleading answer if the ink is adjusted between bands, the press crawls through the test, or the team chooses whichever patch looks darkest. The purpose is to compare engraving options under one controlled production condition. Start with the job the roller must run, decide what will be measured, and keep the remaining variables still.

Define one production question

State the application before discussing cell count or volume. A process-colour screen, opaque white on film, varnish, adhesive or solid spot colour asks for a different ink film and different acceptance checks. Include the substrate, ink system, plate screen and tape, normal speed, drying or curing limit, and the visual or measured result required.

Do not combine every unresolved print problem into one trial. If the current issue may come from contaminated rollers, unstable viscosity, worn blades or excessive impression, correct those conditions first. A banded roll compares engravings; it should not be expected to diagnose an uncontrolled press.

Select bands around a sensible working range

Give the anilox supplier a good sample, the present roller specification and condition, the ink technical data, the required colour or opacity, and the plate and substrate details. Zecher lists print-sample evaluation and the conception of banded-roll tests among its application services. That work should narrow the test to engravings with a realistic chance of meeting the job.

The bands may vary volume, cell count, geometry or a controlled combination of them. Avoid a random ladder of numbers. Each band needs a stated reason, such as checking whether a finer ruling can hold the screen while maintaining the solid, or whether another geometry transfers an opaque ink more evenly.

Record how each volume was measured

A stated volume is incomplete without its measurement method. Zecher distinguishes actual scoop volume, determined after an application-related doctoring process, from geometric volume calculated from a three-dimensional cell model. The results depend on different factors and are not automatically interchangeable.

Keep the supplier's inspection report with the trial. Record the engraving, cell count, stated volume, measurement method and measuring positions for every band. When the production rollers are ordered or checked later, use comparable settings and methods. Otherwise a difference between reports may come from the measurement rather than the roller.

Build a test form that exposes the trade-off

Use patches that represent the actual work. For process and combination printing, include solids, fine type, reverses, highlight and mid-tone screens, vignettes and register targets. For white, coating or varnish, include a large enough area to show pinholes, streaks, lay and cross-web uniformity. Add measurement patches that fit the instruments used in the plant.

Make sure every important feature crosses all bands in the same direction and at the same plate condition. Identify the bands clearly outside the evaluation area. A trial becomes hard to read when the plate changes at the same point as the engraving.

Hold the press condition still

Use one approved ink batch and record viscosity, temperature and pH where relevant. Keep the doctor blade, chamber pressure, plate, mounting tape, substrate, impression, drying or curing setting and web tension unchanged across the comparison. If a correction is unavoidable, mark the exact point and do not compare samples taken on opposite sides of it.

Run at the normal production speed or at agreed speeds that represent the operating range. Allow the ink circulation and press condition to stabilise before retaining samples. A band that looks acceptable only at a slow setup speed has not proved that it can run the job.

Measure more than the darkest solid

Agree the checks before the run. Depending on the job, these may include density or L*a*b* values, opacity, tone value, minimum printable dot, reverses, pinholing, mottle, line sharpness, drying or cure, rub resistance and cross-web consistency. Measure repeated samples rather than choosing one unusually good pull.

The highest transfer is not automatically the best selection. Prefer the band that reaches the approved result with a stable ink film while preserving the fine work and running inside the press's drying, curing and cleaning capability. Record rejected bands and the reason. That evidence is useful when the same question returns with another substrate or ink.

Convert the trial into an ordering standard

Save the approved print sample with the test conditions, measurements and band identification. When ordering production rollers, specify the engraving and the measurement basis used for the trial. On receipt, verify the supplier report, identification and condition before the roller enters the normal inventory.

Before approving the order, repeat the selected band from a fresh stable condition and compare it with the first retained sample. This catches a choice based on one transient result. If the production set will serve several presses, confirm that their chamber, blade, speed and ink-control conditions are close enough for the same specification to remain useful.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about anilox trial planning

What is a banded anilox roll?

It is a test roll or sleeve carrying several defined engraving bands so their ink-transfer behaviour can be compared during the same controlled press run.

Should every band have a different volume?

Not necessarily. A planned trial may compare volume, cell count, geometry or a controlled combination, depending on the production question.

Can the ink be adjusted during the trial?

Avoid adjustments while comparing bands. If a correction is unavoidable, record it and take a new stable sample set rather than mixing conditions in one comparison.

Why does the anilox volume measurement method matter?

Actual scoop-volume and geometric cell-volume methods respond to different factors. Results should be compared only when the method and settings are understood.

Which band should be selected?

Choose the band that meets the approved colour, opacity or coating result while retaining required detail and running reliably at production speed.
