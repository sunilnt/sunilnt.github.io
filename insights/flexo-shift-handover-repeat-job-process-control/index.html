Flexo Shift Handover and Repeat-Job Control

Repeat Job Starts Differently on the Next Shift? Treat Handover as Process Control

Keep repeat flexo jobs stable across shifts by handing over component IDs, measured settings, approved samples, open faults and authorised changes.

9 November 20266 min readRoyal Coat

A flexo job can run steadily for one shift and restart with different colour, register, cure or web behaviour on the next. The press has not forgotten the job. More often, the accepted condition was reduced to a recipe name and a verbal message such as ‘running fine’. A useful handover preserves the physical components, measured process values, accepted sample, interventions and unresolved faults that explain how the job is actually running.

Freeze the accepted condition before the shift changes

Start the handover while the job is still stable. Identify the press, job revision, substrate roll or batch, ink batch, stations in use and the time of the last accepted sample. Record the fitted anilox, plate or sleeve, doctoring components and any rotary tooling by their permanent IDs. A saved job name does not prove that the same physical components are on the machine.

The Flexographic Technical Association says every press variable and job component should be defined in the job recipe or ticket, together with the job's targets and tolerances. That principle makes the handover practical: the incoming operator should see what must match, the acceptable range and the evidence used to approve the running condition.

Keep one clearly labelled accepted sample from the outgoing shift. Note the press speed and inspection point at which it was taken. For process colour work, use the plant's approved measurement and viewing method. For spot colour, white, coating, lamination or die-cutting, retain the relevant colour, opacity, cure, bond, release or liner-strike result rather than relying on a general visual approval.

Separate recipe setpoints from measured running values

A recalled setpoint is only the instruction given to the machine. Record the actual condition beside it. Depending on the job, that may include viscosity or pH, ink temperature, web tension, dryer or curing setting, measured cure result, register trend, impression reference, chamber pressure, speed and rewind tension. Use the instruments and frequency already approved for the process.

FTA's FIRST Press Operator Certification emphasises data verification for press setup, troubleshooting and statistical process control. The incoming shift therefore needs more than a single number. Show whether the value has been stable, moving towards a limit or corrected repeatedly during the last hour. A value inside tolerance but drifting steadily deserves attention before it becomes a defect.

Do not turn the handover into an uncontrolled data dump. List the variables that influence acceptance for that job, their targets or ranges, the latest actual readings and the time measured. If the plant uses an electronic trend, link or identify the correct record. If it uses paper, keep the sheet with the job rather than in a separate shift file that the next operator may never see.

Record changes, interventions and open faults

Write down what changed after approval. Examples include solvent or reducer additions, pH correction, blade or end-seal replacement, anilox cleaning, plate remounting, splice removal, web-guide adjustment, lamp change, counter reset or a temporary speed reduction. State the reason, time and result. An unexplained correction is likely to be repeated or reversed by the incoming operator.

Keep temporary workarounds separate from approved settings. A job that runs only after an operator holds a guard, bypasses an alarm or makes an undocumented adjustment is not under control. Stop and follow the plant's safety, maintenance and change-authorisation procedure. The handover should identify an unresolved fault and its status, not normalise an unsafe or unapproved practice.

FTA's guidance on consistent optimisation recommends replacing steps based on feel with measured steps where possible, proving the agreed method and auditing that it is followed. It also treats change management as a written process. Apply that discipline to the shift boundary: authorised changes enter the job standard; temporary corrections remain visible until they are tested, approved or removed.

Make the incoming shift prove the job before changing it

The incoming operator should first verify component IDs, substrate, ink, speed and the current process readings. Then compare a fresh sample with the outgoing shift's accepted sample and the defined control targets. If the result is inside tolerance and the trend is stable, continue the job. Avoid changing a setting simply because another operator normally prefers a different number.

If the result is outside tolerance, hold the affected material and change one relevant variable at a time. Confirm what changed at the shift boundary: a new substrate roll, ink addition, wash-up, restart, splice, changed speed or component replacement. Several simultaneous adjustments may recover an acceptable-looking sample but leave no repeatable explanation for the next start.

Agree the material boundary as well as the process boundary. Record the last accepted roll or pallet, first output under the incoming shift and any material placed on hold. This prevents a later defect investigation from becoming an argument about which shift produced the work. The objective is traceability, not operator blame.

Use a handover record short enough to complete and detailed enough to audit

A practical record fits the machine and application. Use mandatory fields for job revision, material and component IDs, last accepted sample, control values, interventions, open faults, held material and the outgoing and incoming acknowledgements. Add free text only where it explains a deviation. A long generic form invites blank fields and copied answers.

Review a sample of handovers against production evidence. Check whether the recorded component IDs match the press, whether measurements were made at the stated time, whether accepted samples are identifiable and whether repeated faults were escalated. FTA's optimisation guidance recommends scheduled audits that can move to spot checks as the standard becomes established.

For a Royal Coat process review, send the press and application, substrate construction, job revision, ink and curing system, component IDs, target ranges, sample records, present handover sheet and two examples where the next shift could not reproduce the accepted result. That evidence helps separate a weak handover from an equipment, consumable, maintenance or measurement problem.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about flexo shift handover and repeat-job control

What should a flexo shift handover record?

Record the job and material revision, physical component IDs, latest measured process values, accepted sample, changes made, open faults, held material and the outgoing and incoming acknowledgements.

Is recalling the HMI job recipe enough?

No. The recipe shows saved setpoints, not necessarily the fitted components, actual running values, recent interventions or present material condition. Verify those items at the machine.

Should the next shift adjust settings immediately?

First verify the job, components and measured values, then compare a fresh sample with the accepted standard and control targets. Change a setting only when the evidence identifies a variable outside the approved condition.

How should a temporary workaround be handed over?

Identify it as an unresolved deviation with its maintenance or approval status. Do not turn an unsafe, bypassed or untested workaround into a repeat-job setting.

What information helps with a process-control review?

Provide the press and application, substrate, ink and curing system, component IDs, target ranges, measurement records, accepted and failed samples, current handover form and examples of failed repeat starts.
