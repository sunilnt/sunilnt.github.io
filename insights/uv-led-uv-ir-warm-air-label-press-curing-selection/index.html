Modular Curing and Drying Selection

UV, LED UV or IR/Warm Air on a Label Press? Match the Drying Route to the Job

Specify UV, LED UV and IR/warm-air drying on a narrow-web label press by matching chemistry, substrate, speed, heat load and the finished-job test.

1 November 20266 min readRoyal Coat

A narrow-web press may need to run conventional UV inks, LED UV formulations, water-based inks and coatings over its working life. The useful question is not which curing technology is best in isolation. It is which route gives the required result for a defined ink or coating, substrate, laydown, speed and end use. Write that process window first; then decide whether a fixed system or a modular platform is justified.

Start with the chemistry and the finished test

Classify every job family by what must be dried or cured: ink, opaque white, varnish, adhesive or functional coating. Record the supplier, formulation code, colour, target laydown and whether the material is conventional UV, LED UV or water based. A lamp, LED array or warm-air unit cannot compensate for chemistry that is incompatible with its wavelength or drying mechanism.

Conventional UV and LED UV are radiation-curing processes, but they do not automatically use the same formulation. The photoinitiator package must respond to the installed output, and the web must receive adequate energy at production speed. Water-based material follows a different route: water must evaporate, and the humid boundary layer above the coating must be removed. IST METZ describes THERMOcure as a combination of infrared energy and controlled warm air for this purpose, rather than as another UV source.

Define the release test before comparing systems. Depending on the job, that can include surface tack, rub or scratch resistance, tape adhesion, blocking after rewind, odour or migration controls, overprintability, laminating or foiling performance and the customer's application test. A green lamp indicator is machine evidence; it is not proof that the finished construction has passed.

Map substrate heat load, web speed and unit position

List the thinnest and most heat-sensitive films as well as the heaviest papers and boards. Record normal and maximum speed, web width, ink sequence, coating weight and whether the unit is used for pinning, intermediate treatment or final cure. Heat that is acceptable on paper can distort a thin unsupported film, while a low-laydown colour and a heavy opaque white may require different operating windows.

IST METZ notes that LED UV has a lower proportion of infrared radiation than conventional lamp UV, which can reduce thermal load on suitable temperature-sensitive substrates. That is useful, but it does not remove the need to measure web temperature and confirm the complete construction. Cooling, distance, shielding, reflector condition, exhaust and machine geometry still affect the result.

For IR and warm-air drying, document air volume, temperature and exhaust as a system. Adding heat without removing moisture can move the bottleneck rather than solve it. Check where the wet layer first becomes stable, whether the web cools before winding and whether the printed surface blocks after the roll has been stored. The acceptance trial should include restart and low-speed conditions as well as the planned running speed.

Treat modularity as a controlled changeover, not a promise

IST METZ's swap4cure platform is built around interchangeable routes for conventional UV, LED UV and IR/warm-air drying within one machine concept. The practical value is the ability to match the curing or drying technology to changing ink, coating and substrate requirements. The purchasing case still depends on the work actually expected on the press and on how each changeover will be controlled.

Write a changeover sheet for every approved route. Include the installed cassette or module, power and cooling connections, exhaust or air settings, permitted ink and coating codes, recipe, speed window, web-temperature limit, cleaning and inspection checks, safety interlocks and the test needed before the job can be released. Prevent an operator from selecting a stored UV recipe for a water-based coating or assuming that a successful LED colour job proves a heavy varnish.

Also confirm what is physically interchangeable and what remains fixed. Ask how the press control identifies the module, which alarms and interlocks change, how recipes are locked, how maintenance hours are recorded and where a removed module is stored. Modularity is valuable only when the plant can change technologies without losing traceability or safe setup discipline.

Build the acceptance trial from real job families

Prepare samples that represent the expected production range: paper and film, dark and light colours, opaque white where relevant, the heaviest coating, minimum and maximum web widths and the planned speed range. Use the actual ink, varnish and coating systems with written supplier compatibility. Record module type, irradiance or thermal settings, distance, cooling, air and exhaust settings, web temperature, speed and the test result.

Run each route long enough to evaluate the wound roll, not only a strip at the delivery. Check front-to-back set-off, blocking, roll hardness and any change in colour, gloss, adhesion or substrate shape after storage. If the job will be laminated, foil stamped, die-cut or applied at speed, include that next process in qualification. A cure that looks acceptable immediately can still fail when trapped in a warm roll or stressed downstream.

Compare the fixed and modular options on approved job coverage, changeover time, utilities, maintenance, module storage, operator training and evidence required for release. Do not justify a multi-technology platform only with a list of possible applications. Use the jobs the plant expects to sell and the tests its customers will require.

For a Royal Coat review, send the press model and web path, substrate constructions, web width and speed, ink and coating systems, target laydowns, current drying or curing arrangement, utilities, temperature limits, failed and accepted samples and the finished-job test. That information is enough to start a technical discussion with IST METZ around a fixed, retrofit or modular curing route.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about modular curing and drying selection

Can the same UV ink always run under LED UV?

No. Confirm that the formulation and photoinitiator package are compatible with the installed LED wavelength and validate the complete job at production speed.

Why does water-based ink need warm air as well as heat?

Heat helps evaporation, while controlled airflow and exhaust remove the humid boundary layer above the wet film. Both conditions affect drying.

Does lower infrared output make LED UV safe for every film?

No. It can reduce thermal load, but substrate temperature still depends on power, distance, cooling, speed, laydown and the complete press geometry.

What should be recorded for a curing-module changeover?

Record the module, approved chemistry, recipe, speed window, cooling or air settings, web-temperature limit, interlocks, maintenance checks and release test.

How should a modular curing system be accepted?

Qualify each planned technology with real substrates, inks, coatings, speeds and downstream tests, then inspect the wound roll after the defined storage period.
