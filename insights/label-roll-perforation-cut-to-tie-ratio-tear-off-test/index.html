Rotary Perforation for Tear-Off Label Rolls

Label Roll Perforation: Specify the Cut-to-Tie Pattern and Test the Tear

Specify a rotary perforation for tear-off label rolls. Check cut and tie lengths, liner grade, slit edges and tear strength before approving the blade.

26 November 20266 min readRoyal Coat

A cross-web perforation between labels has two jobs. It must hold the liner together through converting, rewinding and feeding, yet let the user tear off a label or a strip without dragging the tear into the next one. If the roll breaks at the perforation during a restart, or if the user cannot start the tear at the finished edge, the blade pitch alone will not explain the failure. Work from the finished lane, the actual liner and the way the roll will be used before changing the tool.

Put the separation line on the finishing drawing

Show the label repeat, cross-web perforation position, finished slit widths and winding direction on one drawing. Mark which material layers are present at the line. For a pressure-sensitive job, a perforation through the exposed paper liner between labels is a different operation from a kiss cut through the face and adhesive. If the perforation crosses a label, retained matrix or a thicker part of the construction, show that explicitly so the toolmaker can review it.

Write down how separation is meant to start. A person tearing from one side of a roll applies a different load from a web being pulled straight through a machine. If the roll must feed through a printer or dispenser before separation, get the customer's roll and feed requirements and, where possible, an accepted sample. For fanfold work, include the fold position and specify whether the fold must also tear by hand.

Give the toolmaker both the cut length and the tie length

The pitch gives the spacing of repeating features. It does not tell the supplier how much material remains uncut. Ask for the intended cut length and the tie length, meaning the uncut bridge between cuts. Two patterns with the same pitch can leave different amounts of liner intact. Avery Dennison's perforations bulletin distinguishes tooth count from the cut-to-tie balance and treats tear resistance as an application requirement.

Review the line against each finished slit lane. A master web can look uniform and still leave different edge conditions after slitting: in one lane the knife may land in a cut, while in another it may leave an uncut section at the edge. Mark the intended tie or cut at each tear-start edge where the tool and slit tolerances allow it. Check the actual finished edges in the trial rather than assuming that the master-web pattern carries through unchanged.

Use production stock when selecting the blade

Send the exact liner grade, measured thickness where available, finished width and a sample from the intended stock. On a laminate, also identify the face, adhesive and any coating or overlamination that is present at the perforation. Segregate the trial samples by stock lot. Wilson Manufacturing recommends testing cutting tools on the actual production construction. An old roll with the same nominal width is not a useful substitute if its liner is different.

Treat a proposed change from paper to film liner as a fresh application. Avery Dennison generally recommends paper for liner perforations in label work. Do not assume a blade approved on glassine will give the same hand tear or feed behaviour on film. If the job requires film, have the material and tooling suppliers review the construction and qualify it on the actual route before accepting the substitution.

Confirm the tool fits the press and the repeat

A straight cross-perforation at a fixed repeat may suit a rotary sheeter or perforator with a replaceable blade. Wilson lists removable-blade tools with slot arrangements for different repeats; Rotometal also documents rotary sheeters with replaceable blades for perforating and cross-cutting. Ask the toolmaker to check the press station, repeat, usable blade length, mating anvil and the slot positions against the drawing. A perforation that must follow a shaped label or register with another cutting feature may need a different tooling arrangement.

If the cut is incomplete in the centre but clean at the edges, record the position across the web and have the station and tooling checked to the supplier's procedure before adding load. Compare the perforation at several points under magnification. Look for missed cuts, ties that have been severed and a fault that recurs at the same cylinder position. Correct a seating or contact fault before drawing conclusions about blade pitch.

Release against two separate tests

First test survival with the actual job. Run at the intended production speed, include a planned stop and restart, and take samples after slitting and rewinding from each lane. Record web breaks, the point of failure and whether the tear began at a slit edge or farther across the line. Compare the samples with the accepted stock, using the plant's approved pull-test method if the job has a defined strength requirement.

Then test separation as the customer will use it. Pull from the specified edge and in the expected direction, using finished rolls and the agreed handling conditions. Record whether the tear starts cleanly, follows the full line and leaves the adjoining label readable and usable. If both checks do not pass, retain the good and failed samples and change one variable at a time. A line that tears neatly on the bench is not ready if it parts during normal feeding.

For a Royal Coat tooling review, send the roll drawing, liner grade and lot, actual stock, press and anvil details, planned speed, and samples showing where the tear starts and where it leaves the line.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about rotary perforation for tear-off label rolls

Can I order a label perforation blade by teeth per inch alone?

Use tooth count to describe pitch, but provide both cut and tie lengths, the material at the line and the release requirement. Confirm the pattern on the finished slit lanes.

Why does a perforated label roll break after slitting?

Compare where each slit edge lands on the perforation and where the tear begins. Check the actual liner, cut quality and feed or rewind condition before changing the blade pattern.

Can the same perforation be used on paper and film liners?

Do not carry the setting across without a trial. Have the material and tooling suppliers review the construction, then verify both web survival and tear-off in use.
