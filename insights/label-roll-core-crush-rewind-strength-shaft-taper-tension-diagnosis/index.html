Label Roll Core-Crush Diagnosis

Finished Label Roll Core Crushes Before Full Diameter? Check Core Strength and Winding Profile First

Diagnose crushed rewind cores by checking the failure pattern, core specification, shaft fit, starting tension, taper and lay-on condition in a controlled order.

22 November 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A finished label roll may look normal at the start and then develop a flattened bore, split core or inward buckle as the diameter builds. The operator may first notice that the roll will not unload cleanly, will not fit the customer's mandrel or has become difficult to rewind concentrically. Do not reduce the complete tension recipe on sight. First establish whether the core was damaged before loading, crushed by the shaft, overloaded by the wound roll or weakened after storage. The failure shape and the point in the cycle when it appears decide which check comes first.

Record when and where the core changes shape

Mark the machine, shaft, lane and core position before removing the roll. Photograph both ends and measure the bore in at least two directions using the plant's approved method. Record whether the distortion is local to one expanding element, spread around the circumference, greater at one end or common to every lane. A single dent at a shaft lug suggests a different load path from a core that closes progressively under the full wound roll.

Note when the fault becomes visible: immediately after shaft inflation, during acceleration, above one roll diameter, after stopping, during unloading or only after storage. Keep an unused core from the same batch and one accepted finished roll for comparison. Also record substrate construction, slit width, finished diameter and weight, winding direction, speed, tension zones, taper, lay-on setting and the core supplier's exact identification.

Verify the complete core specification, not only its inside diameter

Check inside diameter, outside diameter, wall thickness, length, end squareness, material construction and the strength requirement agreed for the finished roll. Inspect for moisture damage, crushed edges, delamination, dents, ovality and debris inside the bore. Confirm storage conditions and batch traceability. A nominal 76 mm core is not automatically suitable for every slit width, roll weight, shaft design or winding profile.

TAPPI's paper on fibre cores warns that flat-crush performance alone does not describe the complete requirement because winding and unwinding apply loads in several directions. Use the core supplier's current test data and recommendation for the real roll dimensions and process. Do not substitute a visually similar core from another job without proving its strength, dimensional tolerance and compatibility with the shaft and customer's unwind.

Check shaft fit and expansion before changing web tension

Load an unused core on the correct shaft and confirm full engagement, straight seating and the specified clearance before inflation. Inspect expanding lugs, leaves or elements for uneven height, wear, contamination and sharp edges. Verify the regulated inflation pressure and operating sequence against the shaft manufacturer's instruction. Excess pressure can concentrate load into the core, while insufficient or uneven grip can let the roll slip and then damage the bore under torque.

Check whether the fault stays with one shaft position when cores and slit lanes are exchanged. If it does, inspect that shaft, adaptor, support and drive before changing the master recipe. PRATI's published converting configuration includes several interchangeable pneumatic and differential shaft sizes. That range reinforces the application point: shaft and core must be specified as a working pair, not chosen independently from the label roll.

Separate the tight start from the full-diameter winding profile

The first wraps need enough control to anchor the web and prevent a loose core start. They do not justify holding the same winding force as diameter and roll mass increase. TAPPI guidance on flexible-film winding explains that residual stress builds inside the roll and recommends a hardness profile that reduces as the roll grows. Another TAPPI troubleshooting paper lists core crush among the damage associated with winding too tight.

Compare the actual tension or torque profile with the approved job, not only the starting setpoint shown on the screen. Confirm diameter measurement, taper activation, units, calibration and any recipe limits. If the core begins to distort above a repeatable diameter, record that threshold. A smooth, controlled taper trial can then be evaluated without making the core start loose or changing several zones together.

Review tension, torque and lay-on as separate controls

Web tension controls the moving web, centre-winding torque acts through the shaft, and lay-on or nip pressure acts at the roll surface. They influence roll hardness but are not interchangeable. Record each one independently through startup, acceleration and diameter build. Verify the load cells, diameter signal, drive response and lay-on mechanics before accepting a software value as the force reaching the roll.

Make one approved adjustment at a time and build the trial to the intended diameter. Reducing web tension may protect the core yet create a loose roll, telescoping or poor slitting if the real problem is shaft pressure or an underspecified core. Reducing nip may change hardness without correcting excessive centre torque. The accepted combination must produce a sound bore, stable roll edge, correct hardness and clean unwind in the next process.

Release the roll only after an unload and unwind check

After the controlled trial, stop and unload the roll by the approved handling method. Re-measure the bore, inspect both core ends and allow the roll to stand for the agreed period. Then prove it on the intended mandrel or a qualified gauge and unwind enough material to check edge stability, layer slip, curl and tension response. A roll that merely comes off the production shaft is not yet evidence that the core will work at the customer's machine.

Save the accepted core specification, supplier and batch, shaft ID and inflation setting, web and slit widths, finished diameter and weight, tension and taper profile, lay-on condition, bore measurements and retained sample. Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about label roll core-crush diagnosis

Does a crushed label-roll core always mean rewind tension is too high?

No. Core strength, shaft fit, inflation pressure, centre torque, lay-on condition, finished diameter and storage can contribute. Record the failure pattern and timing before changing the recipe.

Why does the core crush only on one rewind lane?

A one-lane fault can follow that core, shaft element, differential position, support, drive setting or local roll build. Exchange known components in a controlled trial to see which position the fault follows.

Should rewind tension stay constant as roll diameter increases?

Not as a universal rule. The approved winding profile should suit the material, core and finished diameter. Flexible-film guidance commonly uses a controlled reduction in tightness as the roll grows.

Is core inside diameter enough when ordering label cores?

No. Wall, construction, strength, length, squareness, dimensional tolerance, finished-roll load, shaft design and the customer's unwind requirement also matter.

What should be sent for a core-crush application review?

Send the core specification and batch, unused and failed cores, bore measurements, shaft details and inflation setting, material, slit widths, finished diameter and weight, speed, tension, taper and lay-on records.
