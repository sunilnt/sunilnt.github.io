Flexographic Print Halo and Impression Control

Dark Halo Around Flexo Solids or Text? Reset Impression Before Changing Ink

Diagnose dark outlines around flexo solids and text by separating impression, mounting package, cylinder condition and ink metering before changing the recipe.

17 November 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A dark outline around solid text, a rule or the edge of a reverse is often treated as an ink problem. The operator may thin the ink, change the anilox or add pressure until the centre of the solid looks acceptable. That can hide the symptom while moving the press further away from the approved condition. A flexo halo is commonly linked to excess squeeze at the plate-to-substrate or anilox-to-plate contact, but the pressure may be compensating for a mounting, cylinder or metering fault. Reset the two impressions first, then find why more contact was needed.

Confirm that the defect is a halo

Examine the first failed pull under magnification and compare it with the approved sample. A halo appears as an unwanted outline around printed type or image edges. It may look like a darker rim, a double edge or ink squeezed beyond the intended image. Record the colour station, repeat position, operator side and drive side. Also note whether the outline follows every edge or is stronger on one side of the image.

Do not group halo, feathering, dirty print and slur under one description. Feathering has irregular edges, while slur normally shows directional elongation or doubling. A halo linked to squeeze tends to follow the image boundary. Keep the sample, mark web direction and photograph one controlled area at the same magnification after every adjustment. The shape and direction of the fault narrow the search faster than a general instruction to improve print quality.

Return both impressions to the lightest complete contact

The Flexographic Technical Association defines kiss impression as the lightest contact that transfers ink across the complete plate surface or from the plate to the substrate. Its technical material also links a halo or double outline to excessive plate impression and explains that excess squeeze can distort image areas. Back the deck away, establish anilox-to-plate contact, then establish plate-to-substrate contact. Make one small change at a time and retain a labelled pull.

Do not use additional plate-to-substrate pressure to correct weak inking from the anilox side. Likewise, do not drive the anilox into the plate to compensate for a weak solid caused elsewhere. Where the job includes a suitable impression or slur target, use it with the print sample. If the halo clears as pressure is removed but the solid becomes weak, the remaining weakness is useful evidence. Investigate ink delivery, plate package and mechanical condition instead of restoring the squeeze.

Check the complete plate package against the press undercut

Measure or confirm the plate, mounting tape, sleeve or cylinder build and any packing used on the deck. DuPont's troubleshooting guide advises adjusting the overall print-package thickness to match the press undercut when diagnosing halo. An incorrect total build changes the real contact even when the press display repeats a saved setting. Compare the installed components with the approved job record rather than relying on tape colour or memory.

Inspect the back of the plate and both sides of the tape for overlaps, trapped debris, wrinkles, air pockets and local patches. A small high spot can produce a halo only at one repeat position. Confirm that the plate is seated through the joint and around the full circumference. If the fault follows one mounted plate when other process variables stay stable, remove that plate package for controlled inspection rather than increasing global impression.

Match tape response to the image on the plate

Mounting tape hardness changes how the plate package responds under load. The 3M flexographic mounting selection guide describes softer tapes for halftone reproduction, firmer tapes for solid ink and crisp lines, and combination grades where solids and dots share the same plate. This is not a reason to change tape during an uncontrolled press correction. It is a reason to verify that the mounted tape matches the approved plate and print requirement.

If the job was fingerprinted with one tape family or foam response, keep that condition while diagnosing the immediate fault. A harder tape may improve a solid but can increase dot growth or make a pressure error more visible. A softer tape may protect fine dots but may not deliver the same solid. Evaluate any tape change with the actual solid, fine type, reverses and screen work on a controlled trial, then save the accepted specification in the job record.

Inspect cylinders, sleeves and contact surfaces

DuPont's halo checklist includes clean print cylinders free from adhesive, score marks and low spots, along with checking the selected cushion. Clean the sleeve or cylinder surface and inspect the plate back before remounting. Adhesive residue, dried ink or damage under the plate raises the local print surface. Check the impression cylinder for contamination at the corresponding machine position and confirm that the substrate is not carrying a splice or thickness change through the sample area.

If the halo changes across the web or repeats once per revolution, measure rather than guess. Check cylinder or sleeve total indicated runout using the press and component supplier's method, and inspect bearings or journals when the contact varies through the rotation. A deck that needs noticeably different pressure at different positions has a mechanical or build condition to resolve. Repeatedly resetting impression will not make that condition stable for the next roll.

Check ink metering after contact is controlled

DuPont also identifies an ink-metering system that is out of adjustment or damaged as a possible contributor to halo. Once impression is at the approved light contact, confirm the correct anilox identity and condition, chamber alignment, doctor-blade condition, ink circulation and the ink values specified for the job. Do not replace the anilox solely because the edge is dark if the same roller printed the approved sample under a different contact condition.

Keep viscosity, temperature and other ink controls within the ink supplier's approved range while comparing pulls. Change one variable, allow the circulation loop to stabilise and record the result. If the halo appears only after the press warms or reaches production speed, compare the warm running condition with the start-up sample. That points the investigation towards pressure stability, tape response, ink condition or mechanical movement instead of a static artwork fault.

Release a repeatable impression record

Save the plate and tape specification, total package build, sleeve or cylinder ID, anilox ID, impression references, ink condition, press speed and the accepted sample. Include one magnified image of the problem edge and one after correction. State which contact was changed and by how much in the press's own reference system. A note saying pressure reduced is not enough for another shift to reproduce the result.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about flexographic print halo and impression control

What causes a dark halo around flexo text or solids?

Excessive plate-to-substrate or anilox-to-plate contact is a common cause. Incorrect print-package thickness, unsuitable or damaged mounting material, dirty or damaged cylinder surfaces and an ink-metering fault can create or encourage the extra pressure.

Should the operator reduce ink viscosity to remove a halo?

Not as the first correction. Return both impressions to the lightest complete contact and verify the plate package and mechanical condition before changing the approved ink recipe.

Can mounting tape hardness affect halo and dot gain?

Yes. Tape response influences solid transfer, fine-line sharpness and dot reproduction. Keep the approved tape during diagnosis, then qualify any change with the complete image content under controlled press conditions.

Why would a halo appear in only one area of the repeat?

Look for a local high spot such as debris, adhesive residue, a tape overlap, an air pocket, plate damage or cylinder and sleeve runout. Mark the exact repeat and cross-web position before removing the plate.

What should be recorded after correcting a flexo halo?

Record plate and tape details, total package build, sleeve or cylinder and anilox IDs, impression references, ink condition, speed and labelled before-and-after samples.
