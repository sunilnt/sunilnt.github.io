Mid-Web Digital Converting Line Selection: Match Modules to the Jobs You Will Sell

Specify a mid-web digital converting line from material, printed-web input, coating, embellishment, slitting and roll-or-sheet output requirements.

15 August 20268 min readRoyal Coat

A modular converting line can do many things, but unused modules add cost and complexity while a missing web-handling or finishing function limits sellable work. Start with actual jobs: printed roll width, material thickness, coatings, embellishment, registration, output and changeover frequency. Build the line around that production mix and a realistic growth case, then trial the hardest constructions.

Define the incoming web

Record press, maximum printed width, roll diameter, core, substrate range, thickness, print repeat, registration marks and any primer or overcoat. Note whether material is self-adhesive, unsupported film, shrink sleeve, wrap-around or another construction.

Include roll quality and splice conditions from the digital press. Finishing cannot correct unstable input rolls without cost.

List required processes by job

Separate mandatory operations from possible future work: full or selective coating, printing, lamination, embellishment, slitting, sheeting and roll output. Estimate volume and changeover frequency for each.

PRATI describes DIGIFAST20000 as a modular mid-web platform for PSL, IML, shrink sleeve and wrap-around work, with roll or sheet finishing options. Confirm the offered modules against the exact application.

Check registration and curing

Provide printed samples and registration marks for trials. Evaluate re-register performance through acceleration and steady speed, including transparent and low-contrast webs.

Match water-based or UV coating and curing to the material, laydown and end-use test. Confirm heat management for sensitive film.

Evaluate web handling and output

Review unwind, guiding, tension zones, inspection, slitting, waste handling, rewind and sheet delivery. A high-value coating result is not commercially useful if the finished roll telescopes or the sheet stack is unstable.

Specify cores, roll diameters, slit patterns and downstream equipment. Trial the complete route, not an isolated module.

Measure changeover and waste

Time cleaning, coating change, tool or module setup, web threading and output conversion. Record saleable material from start to approved production rather than quoting only rated speed.

Modularity has value when upgrades fit a defined demand. Ask what can be added in the field, what requires factory work and how software or controls are licensed.

Build the investment case from jobs

Calculate capacity and cost with realistic run lengths, utilisation, consumables, tools, maintenance and staffing. Include work currently outsourced or rejected only when sales evidence supports it.

Agree an acceptance test using actual files, rolls and quality criteria. The result should be a configuration tied to products and customers, not a catalogue of every available option.

Run a controlled production trial

Use real printed rolls covering the widest web, thinnest film, heaviest coating and required roll or sheet output. Agree the test roll, normal operating range and responsible operator before starting. Begin from verified mechanical condition and approved consumables. Change one variable at a time, allow the process to stabilise and retain samples from make-ready, steady production and the end of the roll. A short demonstration at an easy setting does not prove that the process will hold through a commercial job.

During the trial, monitor guiding, re-register, tension, coating or printing, curing, slitting, waste handling and delivery. Mark every deliberate change and production event on the same timeline. Acceptance should require approved register, coating, adhesion, roll build or sheet stack, saleable output and measured startup waste. If the result is outside the agreed limit, preserve the sample and machine state long enough to identify the cause instead of making several unrecorded corrections.

Turn the result into a pressroom standard

Save input roll, file, substrate, modules, settings, speed, waste, changeover time and quality results. Attach photographs, measurement reports and an approved finished sample where useful. Give the recipe a revision and link it to the exact material, artwork or machine configuration. Operators should be able to see what may be adjusted, the permitted range, the time allowed for stabilisation and who approves an exception.

Define a stop and escalation rule for unstable web handling, cure failure, register loss, unacceptable waste or output that cannot run downstream. Review repeat faults by station, material and shift instead of treating each one as a separate surprise. A good standard does not remove judgement; it gives experienced operators reliable evidence and prevents the next shift from undoing a successful correction.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about mid-web digital converting

What is mid-web digital converting?

It is post-print coating, embellishment, converting and finishing for wider digitally printed webs than conventional narrow-web label work.

Should every module be purchased at the start?

Not necessarily. Separate current mandatory work from a supported growth case and confirm field-upgrade paths.

What belongs in an acceptance trial?

Actual printed rolls, difficult materials, registration, required coating or embellishment, full output format, speed, waste and quality checks.

What should be changed first during the trial?

Start from verified mechanical condition and approved consumables, then change only one controlled variable at a time.

What makes the result repeatable on the next shift?

A revision-controlled recipe with component identity, settings, measurements, approved samples and clear stop rules.
