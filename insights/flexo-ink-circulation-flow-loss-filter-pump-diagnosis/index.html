Flexo Ink Circulation

Ink Flow Falls During the Run? Check the Circulation Loop Before Thinning the Ink

Diagnose falling flexo ink flow by checking the tank, pump, hoses, filter, inline sensor, chamber and return before changing viscosity or adding reducer.

18 September 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A chamber that starts to starve halfway through a run does not automatically need thinner ink. The viscosity reading may still look high because the sensor is seeing poor or intermittent flow, while a blocked filter, restricted hose, weak pump delivery or obstructed return is the real fault. Adding solvent at that point changes the ink without restoring circulation. Preserve the reading, print sample and loop condition, then check the route the ink actually takes.

Prove that circulation has changed

Record what the operator first noticed: a low chamber level, surging return, bubbles, colour movement, a noisy pump, an unstable viscosity value or loss of print density. Mark the press, station, ink, substrate, speed and time into the run. Keep print samples from before and after the change and note whether the fault is continuous or comes and goes.

Compare the supply and return behaviour with an accepted job on the same station. Use the plant's approved observation or measurement method and do not open a guarded or pressurised line while the equipment is running. A steady tank level with unstable print may require a different investigation from a chamber that is visibly running short of ink.

Check the trend rather than one display value. InkSpec describes a control sequence of measurement, analysis, control and mixing, with process ink flowing through the viscometer. If the flow through that part of the loop is not representative, the number on the screen needs to be treated as evidence to investigate, not as an instruction to add reducer immediately.

Trace the complete ink route

Draw the route from the pail or tank through the pickup, pump, filter, viscosity sensor, supply hose and chamber, then through the return hose back to the reservoir. Mark every valve, connector, bend and change in hose size. The drawing does not need to be formal. Its purpose is to stop the team checking only the component nearest the visible symptom.

Inspect the safe external condition of the hoses and connections. Look for a crushed section, tight bend, loose fitting, air entry on the suction side, dried ink at a connector or a hose that has softened or collapsed. Confirm that valves are in the approved operating position and that the pickup remains submerged at the normal working level.

Check the return as carefully as the supply. A restricted return can change chamber level and make the pump adjustment look wrong. Record where the return enters the reservoir and whether foam, splashing or intermittent discharge appeared when the fault began. Do not change pump setting, valve position and ink condition together, or the useful evidence will disappear.

Inspect the filter without losing the evidence

Stop and isolate the system using the press and ink-system instructions before opening the filter. Photograph the filter element and retain a representative sample of any material found. Note the ink batch, time since the last clean, mesh or filter specification, orientation and whether the element was fitted correctly.

InkSpec states that its magnetic ink filter is designed to capture debris and metallic particles before they reach critical printing components. The company links filtration with protection of the anilox and plates and with avoiding print defects. A loaded element can therefore be useful process evidence, but the source of the material still has to be identified rather than simply cleaned away every shift.

Separate dried ink, fibres, damaged seal material and metallic particles where the plant can do so safely. Check whether the contamination appeared after a container change, maintenance task, wash-up or component replacement. If metallic material is present, preserve it and inspect the approved wear points before restarting. Fit only the correct clean element and seal arrangement for the system.

Separate flow, viscosity, temperature and mixing

Once circulation is stable, compare the inline value with the maintained plant reference method. Take the sample from the agreed point, at the recorded temperature, and use the same cup and timing method applied to the approved job. InkSpec notes that operators can use cup readings to establish or check the control setpoint, while its inline system continuously reads the ink flowing through the sensor.

Do not assume that a high reading has one cause. Evaporation can raise viscosity, temperature can alter the reading and ink behaviour, incomplete mixing can delay the effect of an addition, and poor flow can leave the sensor with an unrepresentative sample. InkSpec's controller uses a mixing-time delay after an addition to avoid repeated corrections before the new condition has circulated through the system.

Restore one baseline at a time. First prove the normal circulation path, then confirm temperature and measurement agreement, and only then correct the ink according to the ink supplier's instruction. Record any fluid addition by type and quantity. If the ink has already been over-reduced, isolate it for technical review rather than trying to hide the correction with more concentrate on press.

Check the pump and chamber under production load

A loop can appear normal while idling and fail when the press reaches production speed. After the safe inspection and approved restart, observe the chamber level, supply and return behaviour, pump sound, viscosity trend and print at setup speed and normal speed. If the site measures flow or pressure, record the value and instrument used at the same operating conditions each time.

Ask maintenance to compare pump condition, seals, drive, pickup and capacity with the equipment specification. Do not compensate for wear by increasing speed beyond the supplier's limit. Check that the chamber, end seals and doctor-blade arrangement are not losing more ink than the loop can supply, and that a leak or excessive foaming is not being mistaken for low pump delivery.

Use a controlled run to confirm the repair. The job should hold the approved ink condition and print result through acceleration, steady production and a planned stop and restart. Keep the readings with the print sample. A few clean metres immediately after filling the chamber do not prove that the circulation loop will remain stable for the full job.

Build a repeatable station check

Give each station a short record for hose route, valve position, filter specification, normal reservoir level, pump setting, sensor identification and approved cleaning method. Add the accepted circulation or pressure value where the system provides one. This makes a gradual restriction easier to recognise and reduces the temptation to correct every colour movement with solvent.

Trend filter findings, hose replacements, pump work, viscosity corrections and print defects by station. If the same debris or restriction returns, investigate its source and timing. A checklist that only says filter cleaned will not show whether the contamination is coming from ink handling, dried material, maintenance damage or component wear.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about flexo ink circulation

Should solvent be added when the chamber ink level starts falling?

Not until the circulation path and the actual ink condition have been checked. A blocked filter, restricted hose, pump problem or return fault can reduce flow, and solvent will not repair it.

Can poor ink flow affect an inline viscosity reading?

The sensor is intended to measure process ink flowing through it. If that flow is intermittent or unrepresentative, investigate the loop and compare the value with the maintained reference method before changing the ink.

What should be recorded when a filter is blocked?

Record the station, ink and batch, run time, filter specification, last cleaning, element orientation and the type and location of material found. Keep a safe representative sample where possible.

Why check the return hose if the chamber is starving?

The supply and return form one circulation loop. A restricted or badly routed return can disturb chamber level and overall flow, so both sides need to be checked under the same operating conditions.

When is the repair proven?

When the station holds the approved ink condition, circulation and print result through setup, normal production speed and a planned stop and restart.
