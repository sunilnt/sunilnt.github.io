Anilox Roll Identification and Job Control

Correct Anilox on the Job Sheet but Wrong Roll on Press? Fix Identification Before Adjusting Ink

Prevent anilox selection errors by linking the roller ID, engraving, volume, condition record and press position to one verified job standard.

7 November 20266 min readRoyal Coat

A repeat flexo job can start with weak colour, heavy solids or dirty reverses even when the ink and plate are correct. Before changing viscosity or impression, confirm that the anilox fitted to the station is the exact roller approved for the job. A lineature written on a job sheet is not enough when several rolls share a similar appearance or when measured volume has changed through wear, plugging or cleaning.

Identify the physical roller, not only the nominal specification

Record the manufacturer's serial number or permanent roller ID at the press. Then confirm the engraved lineature, nominal volume, engraving type and usable face width against the job record. Two rolls may carry the same line count but use different cell geometry or volume. They are not automatically interchangeable for the same solid, white, varnish or fine-screen requirement.

Zecher's ColorTags system is designed to make anilox rolls and sleeves easier to identify from their face area. The colour marking can also be combined with information for pick-up volume and engraving ruling. The useful principle is broader than one marking system: the operator should be able to identify a roll without guessing from its location, surface colour or storage rack.

Do not make colour names the only code. A blue tag that means 360 lines per centimetre in one plant can be misread after a new volume or engraving is added. Link the visual mark to the unique roller ID and keep the complete specification in the pressroom register. The tag speeds selection; the ID proves which roll is actually being used.

Separate nominal volume from current working condition

The engraved certificate describes the roll when supplied. Production condition changes. Dried ink can reduce open cell volume, while mechanical or chemical damage can alter transfer in another way. If a previously stable job now needs a large ink correction, compare the fitted roll with its condition history before rewriting the recipe.

Zecher explains that actual scoop volume and geometric scoop volume are different measurements and are not necessarily comparable. Its VoluCheck process measures fluid carried after an application-related doctoring step, while Dotscope calculates geometric cell volume from a three-dimensional depth model. When building a condition trend, use the same method and comparable settings each time. A change of instrument or setup can look like a change in the roller when the measurement basis has changed.

Measure more than one point across the face. A single centre reading can miss local plugging or wear near one lane. Save the method, settings, date, measurement positions and cleaning status with the result. The objective is not to produce one impressive number. It is to know whether the roll fitted today still behaves close enough to the approved job standard.

Make the press check part of job setup

Add an anilox verification line to the setup sheet: press, colour station, roller ID, lineature, nominal volume, engraving and condition status. Require the operator or supervisor to sign that line before ink correction begins. For sleeve systems, record the sleeve ID separately from the mandrel or adaptor so that the trace follows the engraved component.

At startup, compare the first approved pull with the saved standard under controlled viewing conditions. If colour is wrong, first confirm the roller ID, chamber and doctor blade setup, ink batch, viscosity or pH as applicable, temperature, press speed and substrate. Changing ink strength before proving the roll can create a new formula that only compensates for the wrong component.

If two nominally similar rolls are approved as substitutes, document the trial rather than assuming equivalence. Run the same ink, plate, substrate, speed and doctoring condition, then compare the accepted print and the process settings. Keep the substitute relationship in the register only when it has produced an acceptable result for that application.

Use one register for storage, cleaning and repeat jobs

The anilox register should show where each roll is stored, its current status and the last meaningful condition check. Mark rolls as available, on press, awaiting cleaning, under inspection or held for repair. A roll that is physically present but awaiting cleaning must not appear as a ready substitute on the job sheet.

Zecher's inspection guidance uses mechanical checks and high-resolution examination of the engraving to assess roller condition. It also describes checking several points on the roller. Use that principle when a roll repeatedly causes a transfer problem that routine cleaning does not resolve. Do not keep increasing ink strength or pressure around a component whose condition is unknown.

For a new or revised job, send Royal Coat the substrate, ink system, plate screen, required solid or opacity, press and chamber details, running speed, usable face width, existing roller IDs, engraving data and accepted print sample. That information lets the anilox requirement be reviewed as an application, rather than as a line-count request.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about anilox roll identification and job control

Is lineature enough to identify an anilox roll?

No. Record the unique roller ID together with lineature, nominal volume, engraving type and face width. Rolls with the same line count can have different volumes or cell geometries.

Why should anilox volume readings use the same method?

Actual scoop-volume and geometric-volume methods measure different things. Trend condition with the same method, settings and measurement positions so that the readings remain comparable.

Should I adjust ink strength when a repeat job starts weak?

First verify the fitted roller ID, roller condition, doctoring, ink control variables, substrate and press speed. An ink correction can hide a wrong or plugged anilox and create a poor repeat standard.

What status should an anilox register contain?

At minimum, record available, on press, awaiting cleaning, under inspection and held for repair, plus storage location and the last condition check.

What details are needed to review an anilox application?

Provide the substrate, ink system, plate screen, required solid or opacity, press and chamber details, speed, face width, existing engraving data, roller IDs and an accepted sample.
