Lightweight Magnetic Cylinders

Heavy Magnetic Cylinder Slowing Job Changes? Check the Complete Tool Specification

Evaluate a lightweight magnetic cylinder for label die-cutting by checking the present tool weight, press interface, repeat, magnetic hold, handling method and job mix.

13 September 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A large magnetic cylinder may need two operators or a lifting aid at every changeover. Replacing steel with a lighter construction can reduce the handling burden, but weight is only one line on the specification. The new cylinder still has to match the press, flexible die, anvil, repeat and cutting gap. Start by recording the present tool and changeover method. Then decide whether a lightweight cylinder solves a real production constraint or simply changes the material on the purchase order.

Measure the present handling problem

Record the actual cylinder weight, teeth or repeat, body length, working width and the number of times it is changed in a normal week. Time the complete change from the last acceptable roll to the first acceptable roll on the next job. Separate lifting and removal time from cleaning, die mounting, station setup and approval time.

Observe how the cylinder moves from storage to the die station. Note the height, reach, available aisle space, trolley or hoist, lifting points and number of people involved. A lighter tool can help with the physical step, but it will not shorten a change dominated by die alignment, material threading or approval delays.

Include near misses, awkward hand positions and any point where the operator has to support the cylinder while locating a journal or gear. Use the plant's handling assessment and press procedure. The purchasing question should be based on the real movement, not a catalogue weight in isolation.

Lock the press and tooling interface before comparing materials

Give the supplier the press make and model, tooth count, circular pitch or module, working width, body and bearer dimensions, journal details, gear specification, running direction and required mounting references. Confirm the undercut or drop, magnetic-cylinder to anvil relationship and the flexible-die thickness used for the job.

Rotometal defines drop as the diameter difference between the bearer and working body of a magnetic cylinder, while the corresponding radial value is the gap to the anvil. A correct repeat alone does not prove that a cylinder belongs in the station. Keep the approved drawing and dimensional report with the tool record.

Specify the die-positioning method as well. Rotometal lists mounting lines, optional positioning pins and anti-movement features for AluLite cylinders. These options should match the flexible dies and changeover method already used, or be introduced through a controlled tooling change.

Compare lightweight aluminium with the other cylinder options

Rotometal makes AluLite magnetic cylinders with a hard aluminium body, hardened steel raceways and axles, and a hard-anodised wear-resistant surface. Its current product information states that cylinders above 120 teeth can be up to about 50 percent lighter than conventional steel versions. Ask for the calculated weight of the exact drawing because repeat, face length, journals, gear and options change the final figure.

The lighter option is relevant when frequent handling of a large repeat is the bottleneck. A small cylinder that is already moved safely may give little practical gain. At the other end, a very large tool can still require a hoist even after a substantial weight reduction. The handling plan must use the delivered weight, not the percentage reduction.

Rotometal also offers standard steel, IMAG and non-magnetic stainless-steel cylinder constructions. Its stainless option is positioned for corrosion resistance, dimensional stability and strong magnetic adhesion. Compare the operating environment, washdown exposure, tool size, service route and required magnetic layout before selecting the body material.

Confirm magnetic hold and flexible-die compatibility

The flexible die must seat evenly and remain held through acceleration, production speed and deceleration. Ask the supplier to confirm the magnet layout for the die thickness, size, cutting pattern and application. Rotometal states that ferrite magnets are standard and that neodymium magnets can be added in selected areas when higher adhesion is needed.

More magnetic force is not a substitute for a clean cylinder and die back. Inspect both mating surfaces before every mount. A particle, corrosion spot or kink can locally lift the die and alter cutting depth, regardless of the cylinder body material.

Send the actual laminate construction and an approved material sample when the die is being specified. The cylinder, flexible die and anvil work as one cutting system. A lightweight cylinder cannot correct a die made for the wrong face, adhesive and liner or an anvil that is worn or damaged.

Plan storage, lifting and maintenance before delivery

Check whether the existing rack, trolley, hoist and lifting accessories suit the new cylinder dimensions and weight. Mark the safe lifting points and protect the bearers, journals, gear and magnetic surface during transport. Operators need the confirmed weight and handling method at the storage location, not only in a purchasing file.

Use the manufacturer's cleaning and corrosion-protection instructions for the selected construction. Rotometal describes regular cleaning and proper handling as essential to maintaining the cylinder and magnetic hold. Keep hard tools separated so another gear, die or loose object cannot contact the precision surface in storage.

Identify the cylinder, drawing revision, weight, gear, tooth count, undercut or drop, magnet arrangement and approved dies in the maintenance system. Record surface damage, bearing or gear condition and any change in the pressure needed to achieve the cut. A trend in those records is more useful than waiting for a visible failure.

Accept the new cylinder on the job it was bought for

Check the supplier's dimensional and quality report against the approved drawing before the cylinder enters production. Inspect the delivered surface, bearers, journals, gear, mounting reference and identification. Confirm that the flexible die mounts correctly and that removal can be completed with the approved tool and method.

Run the intended laminate from setup through normal speed and roll length. Check die registration, cut depth, liner condition, matrix stripping and any side-to-side or repeat variation. Time the complete changeover again using the same start and finish points as the baseline. This shows whether the lighter tool changed the constraint that justified the purchase.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about lightweight magnetic cylinders

How much lighter can an aluminium magnetic cylinder be?

Rotometal states that AluLite cylinders above 120 teeth can be up to about 50 percent lighter than conventional steel versions. Ask for the exact calculated weight of your drawing.

Does a lighter cylinder remove the need for a hoist?

Not automatically. Use the delivered cylinder weight, lifting points, reach and plant handling assessment to decide the required trolley, hoist and number of operators.

Can an AluLite cylinder use positioning pins for the flexible die?

Rotometal lists optional positioning pins and anti-movement features. Confirm their layout against the flexible die and mounting method before ordering.

Which details must match the press?

Confirm the press and station, tooth count or repeat, pitch or module, working width, body, bearers, journals, gear, undercut or drop, running direction and die-positioning references.

How should a lightweight magnetic cylinder be accepted?

Check the drawing and quality report, inspect the delivered tool, run the intended laminate and flexible die, verify cut and liner condition, and compare the complete changeover time with the recorded baseline.
