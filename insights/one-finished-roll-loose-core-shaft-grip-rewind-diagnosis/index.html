One Finished Roll Winds Loose While the Others Are Firm? Check Core and Shaft Grip Before Changing Tension

Diagnose one loose finished roll on a multi-lane rewinder by checking the core, pneumatic or differential shaft grip, lane evidence and roll build before changing master tension.

30 October 20266 min readRoyal Coat

When one slit lane produces a soft or loose roll while the neighbouring rolls are acceptable, the common rewind setting is not the first place to intervene. The affected core may be out of tolerance, damaged or slipping on the shaft, or one differential element may not be transmitting torque consistently. Preserve one good roll and the failed roll, compare the local mechanical condition and prove where movement occurs before changing a setting that acts on every lane.

Separate a local lane fault from a common tension fault

Identify the affected lane, shaft position, roll number, operator side or drive side, substrate, thickness, slit width, speed, finished diameter and the point in the run when the roll first appeared different. Keep the adjacent accepted roll from the same shaft as the reference. Record roll hardness with the plant's maintained method and check sidewall condition, telescoping, core position and whether the roll can be turned by hand relative to its core.

If every lane is soft, review the common web path, rewind tension, taper, nip, torque command and calibration. If only one lane is loose while the others are firm, inspect the items unique to that lane first: its core, grip position, differential element, separator clearance and the web entering that roll. Increasing master tension to correct one lane can over-tighten the rolls that were already acceptable.

Do not disturb the failed roll before marking the evidence. Draw a witness line across the roll edge and core, mark the core's position on the shaft where it can be done safely and retain the job settings. Photograph the lane arrangement and compare the failed roll with a good roll at the same diameter. This creates a baseline for one controlled correction rather than several simultaneous adjustments.

Measure and inspect the core before blaming the control system

Confirm that the core identity matches the job specification. Measure internal diameter, external diameter, wall thickness and length using the approved tools and sampling plan. ISO 11093-4:2022 specifies test methods for those four dimensions for paper and board cores. Compare the result with the agreed purchasing and machine specification; the standard itself does not provide a universal acceptance tolerance for every rewinding application.

Inspect the bore for ovality, swelling, crushing, delamination, dust, adhesive, moisture damage, burrs and local polishing where the shaft should grip. Check that the ends are square enough for the plant's handling and support arrangement and that the core sits in the intended position. A nominal core size printed on a bundle is not a substitute for measuring the affected core and a sample from the accepted batch.

Record the supplier, batch and storage condition. If several cores from one bundle show the same dimensional or surface problem, quarantine that bundle for measurement rather than mixing it with released stock. Do not reject every core only because one roll was loose; match the actual defect pattern to measurements, grip marks and a controlled run.

Prove that the shaft or differential element is transmitting torque

Verify that the installed shaft type and diameter match the core and job standard. Follow the machine supplier's procedure for inflation pressure, air connection, loading and support; do not substitute an assumed pressure value. Inspect expanding lugs, strips, leaves or differential elements for wear, contamination, damage and free movement. Compare the affected position directly with a position that produced a firm roll.

With the machine stopped, isolated and made safe under the plant procedure, confirm that the core cannot rotate independently when the shaft is correctly engaged. Look for uneven grip marks or a polished band inside the failed core. A short controlled trial can use a witness mark across the core and shaft or another approved reference. If the mark moves during acceleration, steady running or deceleration, the trial has shown local slip rather than a need for more common web tension.

PRATI's published digital-finishing configuration distinguishes pneumatic and differential rewind shafts and lists automatic rewind-tension control across shaft sizes. That distinction matters during diagnosis: the control can command a tension profile, but torque still has to pass through the selected shaft and into each core. Check the physical interface and the relevant differential setting before changing the overall control recipe.

Change one variable and release the roll through the next process

After the core and grip pass, compare the affected lane's differential or torque setting, roll diameter input, calibration and mechanical drag with the accepted lane. Then review taper, lay-on or nip condition and the material path. Keep the substrate, slit width, speed, finished diameter and other common settings unchanged while testing one correction. This preserves the evidence needed to decide whether the cause was the core, the shaft position, the lane setting or the incoming web.

Produce a separately identified trial roll and measure it by the same method used on the original. Compare hardness, diameter, sidewall, core location and unwind behaviour with the retained good roll. Run enough material to include acceleration, stable production and deceleration, because local slip can appear only when torque changes. Do not approve the roll from its outside appearance alone.

Release the trial through the next process at the required unwind direction and operating tension. Confirm that it feeds without core slip, telescoping, blocking, wrinkles or tension disturbance and that the product requirement still passes. Save the core batch, shaft identity and position, inflation or differential check, recipe, speed, diameter and accepted sample with the job record.

For a Royal Coat review, send the substrate or laminate, thickness, mother-web and slit widths, core material and measured dimensions, finished diameter, speed, rewind shaft type, lane position, settings, photos of the bore and grip marks and one accepted and one failed roll. Those details allow the local torque path to be examined before a common tension change is recommended.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about slitting and rewinding

Should master rewind tension be increased when only one finished roll is loose?

Not first. Compare the affected core, shaft grip and lane-specific torque transmission with a good lane. A common tension increase can over-tighten rolls that already pass.

How can a converter check for core slip?

Use the machine supplier's safe procedure, inspect the bore and grip marks and run a controlled trial with an approved witness mark or reference that shows whether the core moved relative to the shaft.

Which core dimensions should be recorded?

Record internal diameter, external diameter, wall thickness and length, along with the core material, batch, bore condition and the plant's applicable acceptance specification.

Does automatic rewind-tension control eliminate shaft-grip problems?

No. The control sets the required operating condition, but the shaft and core interface must still transmit torque consistently to each finished roll.

When is a corrected roll ready for release?

After its roll build passes the documented checks and it unwinds through the next process without slip or tension disturbance while the finished product meets its specification.
