Sleeve Mounting Troubleshooting

Flexo Sleeve Will Not Slide Onto the Air Mandrel? Check Airflow Before Using Force

Troubleshoot a flexo sleeve that is difficult to mount or remove by checking temperature, mandrel edges, dynamic airflow, hoses, fittings and air holes.

6 September 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A sleeve should not need to be hammered onto an air mandrel or pulled off by force. If it stops after covering the first air holes, the correct response is to stop and find what has reduced the air cushion. More static pressure on the regulator is not proof that enough air is reaching the mandrel. Check the sleeve temperature and bore, mandrel condition, hose, fittings, airflow and outlet holes before another mounting attempt damages the sleeve edge or inner surface.

Stop at the first unusual resistance

Remove the plate or tape operation from the diagnosis and concentrate on the mechanical fit. Note where the sleeve stops, whether the problem occurs on one mandrel or every mandrel, and whether the same sleeve mounted normally on the previous job. This separates a single damaged component from a change in the common air supply.

Do not strike the sleeve, lever against its end face or continue pushing after the normal air cushion has disappeared. Polywest's handling guidance says the sleeve should move with little force after the first row of radial air holes is covered. If it does not, the manufacturer advises against forcing or hammering it into position.

Mark and quarantine any sleeve that has been forced. Inspect it before reuse even if it later comes off the mandrel. Damage at the bore, notch or end face can turn the next mounting attempt into a repeat fault.

Condition and inspect the sleeve before blaming the mandrel

Bring sleeves and adaptors into the mounting area early enough to reach the working temperature specified by the manufacturer. A component moved directly from cold storage or a hot vehicle can fit differently from the same sleeve after it has stabilised. Polywest's product note specifies a handling temperature range and separate storage conditions for its sleeve systems. Use the current product document for the actual construction.

Inspect both end faces, the bore and the notch under good light. Look for chips, cracks, raised fibres, dried adhesive, ink, cleaning residue or a local polished area. Wipe only with the cleaning method approved for that sleeve. The bore and end faces need to be dry before mounting.

Confirm the sleeve identity and nominal repeat. A sleeve that has been repaired, recoated or stored without its identification can be mistaken for a similar format. Measure and record only with the maintained method; do not sand the bore or alter the edge to make an uncertain component fit.

Check the mandrel edges and running surface

Inspect the operator-side lead-in, both end faces, the working surface and any register or locking feature. Polywest specifically calls for checking the air mandrel edges for damage, burrs and wear before a sleeve or adaptor is pushed on. A small burr can catch the inner layer and damage it before the air system has a chance to expand the bore.

Clean the mandrel using its approved procedure and confirm that no tape, adhesive, rust or hard deposit is restricting the lead-in. Do not polish or dress a mandrel without the equipment owner's authorised maintenance method. Removing material can change a controlled diameter and create a permanent fit problem.

If several sleeves stick at the same axial position, mark that position on the mandrel and inspect it carefully. If one sleeve sticks on several verified mandrels, move the investigation back to that sleeve. This simple comparison prevents repeated adjustment of a serviceable air system.

Measure airflow at the mandrel, not only static pressure

Read the pressure while air is flowing freely from the mandrel. A normal gauge reading with the outlet closed is a static check and can remain acceptable even when a restricted hose or fitting cannot supply the volume required for mounting. Polywest's checklist uses dynamic measurements for both pressure and air quantity.

Compare the measured result with the current Polywest requirement for the sleeve, adaptor and mandrel combination. Keep the test point, hose arrangement and measuring method in the maintenance record. Do not raise the regulator beyond the approved system limit to compensate for a restriction.

Check whether the mounting station and press behave differently. A sleeve that mounts in one department but not the other points to the local air connection, hose length, bore, regulator, valve or shared demand. Record which supply was used rather than describing the sleeve simply as tight.

Trace restrictions through the complete air path

Follow the line from the supply to the mandrel. Look for a kinked hose, a long narrow connection, a leaking coupling, a loose fitting or another consumer drawing from the same line. Polywest identifies kinks, leaks, excessive hose length and reductions in hose diameter as conditions that can reduce airflow.

With the supply isolated and under the approved maintenance procedure, inspect the radial outlet holes for dirt, rust or blockage. Do not enlarge them with an improvised drill or wire. Their size, number and position belong to the mandrel design. If cleaning does not restore the verified flow, the mandrel or supply needs qualified service.

Listen for leakage at the connection and repeat the dynamic test after each correction. Change one condition at a time. Replacing the hose, fitting and regulator together may restore the result, but it leaves no evidence of which fault caused the loss.

Prove mounting and removal without damaging the component

After the air system and surfaces pass inspection, mount the sleeve straight and support its weight. Avoid twisting or allowing the far end to drop. It should pass the working length under the manufacturer's normal handling method, without striking or excessive manual effort.

Run the sleeve only after the normal concentricity and condition checks are complete. Then remove it using the same verified air supply and support. A sleeve that mounts easily but remains difficult to remove may be warming during production, carrying residue or meeting a damaged section of the mandrel. Record the temperature and point of resistance before it cools.

Save the sleeve ID, mandrel, supply point, dynamic pressure and flow results, hose and fitting condition, air-hole inspection, component temperature and corrective work. Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about sleeve mounting troubleshooting

Can I tap a tight flexo sleeve onto the air mandrel?

No. Stop when the sleeve needs unusual force. Check temperature, bore and edge condition, mandrel damage, dynamic airflow, hoses, fittings and outlet holes before trying again.

Why is the air-pressure gauge normal but the sleeve still will not mount?

The gauge may show static pressure while a restricted hose, fitting, valve or blocked outlet hole limits flow. Measure pressure and air quantity dynamically at the mandrel using the manufacturer's method.

Why does a sleeve mount in the plate room but not on the press?

Compare the local hoses, fittings, regulators, line diameter, air demand and mandrel outlet condition. The two locations may not supply the same airflow even when their gauges show a similar pressure.

Should a sleeve be mounted immediately after cold storage?

Condition it to the handling temperature specified for the sleeve system first. Inspect and dry the bore and end faces before mounting.

What details should be sent for a sleeve mounting problem?

Provide the sleeve and mandrel IDs, dimensions, component temperature, point of resistance, dynamic pressure and flow measurements, hose and fitting details, outlet-hole condition, recent repairs and clear photographs.
