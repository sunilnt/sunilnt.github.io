Web Wrinkle Diagnosis

Web Wrinkles Before Rewinding? Find the First Span Where They Appear

Trace wrinkles on a slitter rewinder by finding their first span, then checking the incoming roll, roller alignment, tension, traction, guiding and spreading.

11 September 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A wrinkle found in the finished roll may not have started at the rewind. It can enter with the parent roll, form across one misaligned span, develop where traction is lost or appear when slit lanes are brought together under the wrong tension. Increasing the master tension may flatten the web for a short run and damage the roll elsewhere. Mark the wrinkle, run the web slowly and find the first span where it becomes visible. That location is the beginning of the diagnosis.

Find the first visible wrinkle, not the final damaged roll

Stop with enough web threaded to inspect the path from unwind to rewind. Mark the operator side, drive side, direction of travel and the affected slit lane. Check the web before and after every guide, roller, nip, inspection point, slitter and spreading device. The first span showing the crease matters more than the place where it is finally wound into the roll.

Record whether the wrinkle is diagonal, centred, confined to one edge or repeated at an interval. Note when it appears during acceleration, steady running, deceleration and roll build. A wrinkle that begins only as diameter increases calls for a different check from one that is already visible at the unwind.

Keep samples from the incoming roll, the first affected span and the finished roll. Record material, thickness, web width, slit pattern, roll direction, speed, tension zones, nip or lay-on setting and guide position. Do this before changing several controls and losing the original pattern.

Check whether the parent roll is supplying a flat web

Inspect the parent roll face, core, edge and outer wraps. Look for telescoping, baggy lanes, crushed areas, gauge bands, moisture effects on paper, poor splices or a roll that is not centred and square on the shaft. Rotate the roll slowly and watch whether one edge or one cross-web band repeatedly becomes slack.

A web can look flat while stopped and develop an uneven tension profile when moving. TAPPI's study of lateral instability reports that cross-machine tension variation can make a web move towards the low-tension side, with wrinkles, misregistration and breaks among the possible results. It also identifies non-uniform material properties as a possible source of that tension profile.

If the same wrinkle position remains with a particular parent roll, compare another approved roll of the same construction before adjusting the machine. If the fault stays at the same machine-side location across different rolls, continue through alignment, traction and guiding checks.

Inspect rollers and alignment before raising tension

Clean the rollers and check that each one turns freely. Inspect for adhesive, ink, coating deposit, damaged covering, bearing play and a diameter change across the face. A local deposit can alter traction and effective diameter enough to steer or buckle a thin web.

Use the maintained alignment method for the machine. Do not judge parallelism only by looking from the aisle. TAPPI's web-wrinkling material treats misalignment as a central cause and notes that wrinkles are tension-sensitive. More tension can therefore change the symptom without correcting the roller or span that introduced it.

Check the normal-entry condition through the suspect span. The web should approach the roller according to the designed path and remain supported. If a roller, shaft or idler has been removed for service, confirm that its position and orientation were restored before changing the process recipe.

Separate tension level from tension profile

Review each tension zone independently. Confirm unwind brake or drive response, dancer or load-cell condition, nip engagement and rewind torque using the approved machine checks. A stable displayed number does not prove that tension is uniform across the width or that the web has traction on every roller.

Make one small change within the approved operating range and observe the first affected span. Record whether the wrinkle position, angle or severity changes. If a large tension increase is required to make the web appear flat, stop and return to alignment, roll quality and traction rather than saving that value as the new standard.

Watch transitions as well as steady speed. A dancer reaching the end of travel, a brake response that lags during acceleration or a rewind change that pulls one lane differently can create a wrinkle that disappears once the line settles. The job still has to pass through those transitions without folding the web.

Use guides and spreaders for the problem they can correct

Check the edge guide sensor, actuator and steering roll only after confirming the incoming edge is suitable for guiding. A guide can move the web laterally, but it cannot remove a cross-web tension profile. Constant guide correction may be evidence that the roll, alignment or upstream span is steering the web.

Inspect the spreader or bowed roller for cleanliness, condition, wrap and position. TAPPI's web-handling presentation notes that spreader action depends on web tension and that the suitable arrangement depends on the material and process. Restore the approved geometry rather than increasing bow or wrap until the crease disappears.

After slitting, confirm that lanes have room to separate and enter their rewind positions without crossing, rubbing or pulling against one another. One wrinkled lane can come from its core, shaft grip, slit edge or local rewind path even when the unslit web was flat.

Prove the correction through a complete roll build

Run from the normal parent-roll condition through acceleration, production speed and the expected finished diameter. Inspect the web at the previously marked span and check every finished roll face and edge. Confirm that the correction does not create telescoping, loose edges, blocking or width variation.

Save the parent-roll ID, material and thickness, affected cross-web position, first wrinkled span, roller and guide checks, tension-zone records, speed transitions, slit pattern, core and shaft details, rewind settings and accepted samples. Photographs or a short video showing where the wrinkle first forms are more useful than a picture of the finished roll alone.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about web wrinkle diagnosis

Why does a wrinkle appear only near the end of the finished roll?

Check changes in rewind diameter, torque, lay-on or nip condition, shaft grip and roll build. Also confirm that the parent roll and earlier spans remain flat as the line runs.

Should I increase web tension to remove a wrinkle?

Use only a controlled change within the approved range. Wrinkles are tension-sensitive, but added tension can hide misalignment, poor traction or an uneven cross-web profile and may create another roll defect.

Can an edge guide remove wrinkles?

An edge guide corrects lateral position. It does not correct an uneven tension profile, material bagginess or a misaligned span, so repeated guide movement should be investigated rather than treated as the cure.

Why is only one slit lane wrinkling?

Inspect that lane's slit edge, core, shaft grip, entry angle, separation from adjacent lanes and local rewind contact. Confirm that the web was flat before slitting.

What evidence should be sent for a rewinder wrinkle review?

Provide material and thickness, parent-roll and core details, web and slit widths, tension zones, speed, guide and rewind settings, roll photographs and a marked video showing the first span where the wrinkle appears.
