Flexo Dirty Print and Highlight Dot Bridging

Flexo Highlights Turning Dirty During the Run? Separate Plate Loading From Excess Ink Transfer

Diagnose dirty flexo highlights by separating plate-dot condition, ink loading, anilox and chamber setup, impression and drying before changing several variables.

26 October 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A highlight can begin clean and become muddy, bridged or surrounded by unwanted ink as the run continues. Stopping to wipe the plate may restore the image for a short period, but it does not show why the deposit returned. Dirty print can begin in the plate, the ink film, the metering system, impression or drying condition. Preserve the first failed sample, mark when the defect entered and separate those causes before cleaning away the evidence or changing several press settings together.

Define what dirty print means on this job

Keep consecutive samples from the last accepted print and the first visible failure. Mark press, deck, colour, lane, machine direction, speed, time, ink temperature, viscosity and pH where relevant. Note whether the defect appears in highlights, fine reverses, text edges, solids or one local image area. A general note saying dirty print is not precise enough to guide a correction.

Inspect the sample under the maintained magnification used by the plant. Look for bridged highlight dots, fuzzy edges, unwanted ink in non-image areas, filled reverses, a hard break in a vignette or isolated blobs beside the image. Compare the defect with the plate position and the repeat. A mark that remains at one image location needs a different investigation from contamination that moves or appears across the complete web.

Record when it begins. A fault present on the first pull can point towards plate structure, screening, setup or an incorrect component. A clean start followed by gradual filling can point towards plate loading, ink drying, excessive film or an operating condition that changes as the press and ink reach temperature. Do not combine those patterns into one diagnosis.

Separate plate structure from press-side loading

Flexographic Technical Association reporting on dirty print distinguishes plate and prepress causes from pressroom causes. Its guidance identifies malformed or unstable highlight dots, inappropriate minimum-dot or screen choices, polymer redeposit and plate loading among the plate-side possibilities. It also lists anilox choice, chamber flooding, stray air, impression and ink drying among press-side checks.

Remove and inspect the plate only under the approved stop and isolation procedure. Before washing it, photograph the affected image and compare it with a clean area. Check whether ink sits between dots, whether a dot is bent, missing or visibly malformed, and whether the deposit follows a plate edge, repair, tape joint or damaged region. Keep the plate ID, artwork revision, plate batch and imaging or processing record with the sample.

FTA's plate-to-print guidance explains that poorly supported highlight dots can lean or fall over on press and print their shoulders, while unsuitable relief or minimum-dot selection can create unwanted print. A press adjustment cannot rebuild a malformed dot. If the physical plate does not match the approved target, hold it for prepress or plate-supplier review instead of increasing cleaning frequency.

Check the ink film and metering system

Confirm the anilox serial number, engraving and last verified condition against the job standard. Inspect the roller, chamber or fountain, doctor blade, seals, filter and circulation route using the approved method. An incorrect anilox or unstable metering condition can deliver more surface ink than the plate and image were qualified to handle. Cleaning the plate will not correct that film at the source.

Record ink identity and batch, temperature, viscosity method and result, pH for water-based ink where specified, pump setting, return condition and any approved additions. Measure at the same sampling point and allow the system to circulate after a correction. Do not thin the ink or add retarder from appearance alone. The ink supplier's operating range and the accepted print remain the reference.

Look for flooding, foam, a restricted return, uneven chamber loading or a blade that is not wiping cleanly. Make sure no unapproved air jet or fan is blowing directly onto the plate or anilox. Air introduced to dry one area can accelerate ink drying on the plate and create the deposit that the operator is repeatedly removing.

Return impression and drying to the approved baseline

Reset plate-to-substrate and anilox-to-plate impression through the press maker's method. Excess impression can spread the printed dot, push ink into non-image areas and make unstable highlights print harder. Missing kiss impression can also lead an operator to compensate elsewhere. Record the final setting instead of relying on a visual memory of where the controls were.

Check the plate, mounting tape, sleeve or cylinder and impression roll for cleanliness, correct identification and secure mounting. Compare the actual plate and tape combination with the qualified job record. A plate that is swollen, loose or mechanically unsupported will not be corrected by an ink change.

Review drying or curing only after the mechanical and ink-transfer baseline is known. Record interstation airflow, dryer temperature or UV condition, web speed and the position of any local air source. The aim is to keep the ink open only as required for transfer while still meeting the final drying, cure, adhesion and resistance requirements. Do not reduce drying simply to keep the plate wet if the printed construction then fails downstream.

Make one controlled change and prove the full run condition

Choose the first correction from the evidence. Replace or remake a defective plate when the dot structure is wrong. Restore the approved anilox, ink condition, metering, impression or airflow when the plate is sound. Change one controlled variable, allow the press to stabilise and retain a labelled sample before moving to the next check.

Run through startup, acceleration, normal production speed and a representative production period. Inspect the same highlights, reverses, text and solid areas at planned intervals. Record the time or metres before any buildup returns, together with ink condition and plate temperature where the plant measures it. A clean five-minute sample does not prove a fault that normally enters after an hour.

Accept the correction from the complete job requirement. Confirm colour or density, tone value, clean highlights, reverse detail, register, drying or cure, adhesion and the downstream test required for the product. Keep the failed and accepted samples, plate photographs, anilox and ink records, impression settings and corrective action under one job reference.

For a dirty-print review, send Royal Coat the press and station, substrate, ink and batch, anilox certificate and condition record, chamber and blade setup, plate and tape specification, artwork and plate revision, speed, impression, drying or cure settings and labelled samples showing when the defect begins. Those details help separate a plate problem from excess transfer or a changing press condition before more production is lost.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about flexo dirty print and highlight dot bridging

Should the plate simply be cleaned when flexo highlights turn dirty?

Cleaning may restore the image temporarily, but preserve the deposit and failed sample first. Check plate-dot condition, ink film, anilox and chamber setup, impression and drying so the reason for repeat loading is identified.

Can an incorrect anilox cause dirty print?

Yes. An unsuitable engraving, excessive ink film or poor doctoring can deliver more ink than the qualified plate and image can handle. Confirm the roller identity and condition before changing other variables.

Can too much impression make highlights print dirty?

Yes. Excess impression can spread dots and print areas that should remain clear. Reset kiss impression by the approved press method and compare the result with the retained sample.

Why can dirty print return after the plate is wiped?

The plate may be loading because ink is drying too quickly, the ink film is excessive, the metering system is unstable, impression is too high or the plate dots are malformed. Wiping removes the symptom, not those causes.

What evidence should be kept for a dirty-print investigation?

Keep sequential print samples, plate photographs and identification, anilox and chamber details, ink measurements, speed, impression, drying or cure settings, timing of the fault and the accepted result after correction.
