Flexo Plate Distortion and Repeat Length

Flexo Register Aligns at One Point but Drifts Around the Repeat? Check Plate Distortion Before Remounting

Diagnose flexo register that aligns at one target but opens or closes around the repeat by checking plate geometry, repeat data and mounting separately.

20 October 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A flexo plate can be positioned accurately at the mounting target and still produce an image that opens, closes or misses register farther around the repeat. Remounting the same plate may reproduce the fault because plate position and plate geometry are different controls. Before moving a camera, mark or plate, preserve the first printed sample and confirm the exact repeat, cylinder or sleeve, plate construction and prepress file used for every separation. The aim is to determine whether the image is in the wrong position or was made for the wrong wrapped geometry.

Read the register pattern before moving the plate

Mark the machine direction, operator side, drive side, print unit and the point in the repeat where the colours first align. Then follow the same targets and image features around one complete repeat. A constant offset can point towards plate position. An error that grows with distance from an aligned target is different evidence and should trigger a check of repeat data, output scale and plate geometry.

Keep an unadjusted web sample from startup and identify the affected separations. Measure between defined features in the printed result rather than judging only a complex image. Note whether the error returns to the same position once per cylinder revolution, continues across multiple repeats, or changes with tension, impression or speed. These patterns should not be grouped under a single description such as bad register.

Do not loosen every plate at once. Choose one agreed datum and compare each colour against it. If all colours move together relative to the printed repeat, inspect the common job and press data. If one separation changes while the others agree, preserve that plate, its file and its mounting record for a focused review.

Lock the mechanical repeat and plate construction

Record the press, unit, cylinder or sleeve identification, nominal print repeat, gear or servo job setting and the approved undercut arrangement. Add the plate type and thickness, mounting-tape product and thickness, sleeve or cylinder face width and any adaptor used. The job record must describe the physical stack that carries the image, not only the finished label or pack repeat.

Confirm that the cylinder, sleeve or adaptor belongs to the recorded press and repeat. Inspect its identification and approved condition before assuming that the prepress file is wrong. A wrong component, mixed sleeve or incorrect job selection can place a correctly made plate onto a different circumference. Quarantine any doubtful item and do not alter its identity to make the job record appear consistent.

FTA operating-data sheets keep cylinder repeat, plate caliper, mounting tape and prepress details in the same production record. Apply that discipline locally. If any value is missing, stop the diagnosis and recover it from controlled specifications, measured components or the responsible supplier instead of copying a value from a similar previous job.

Verify distortion and output scale with prepress

FTA defines a distortion factor as a multiplier that compensates for image change when a flexographic plate is made flat and mounted around a cylinder for printing. That compensation belongs in the approved prepress workflow. The mounting operator should not try to correct a geometry error by stretching the plate, changing tape tension or deliberately misaligning one target.

Ask prepress to confirm the final cylinder or sleeve repeat, plate and tape construction, file revision, output device, imaging resolution and the compensation applied to every separation. Check that no colour was produced from an older file or from settings for another repeat. Keep the RIP record or approved output report with the job rather than relying on the filename alone.

Measure the plate between agreed targets using the approved method and compare it with the controlled plate drawing or output specification. Do not invent a universal permitted difference. The acceptable plate dimension and measurement method depend on the plate process, artwork, repeat and supplier specification. A plate outside that approved result should be remade from corrected data, not forced into position on the mounter.

Mount to one datum without changing the geometry

Clean and inspect the cylinder or sleeve, mounting tape and plate back before mounting. Confirm that the register marks are the approved size, shape and location and that the camera image is sharp on both targets. Heaford states that its plate mounters use high-resolution digital cameras to magnify register marks for precise positioning on cylinders, sleeves or carrier sheets. That supports placement; it does not replace the prepress geometry check.

Set the correct mandrel, cylinder or sleeve and repeat position for the current job. Mount from the agreed centreline, edge or target datum and lay the plate down without tension, trapped air or uncontrolled hand pressure. Record the mounter, operator, job, colour, component identification, target positions and any measured positional variance allowed by the site's procedure.

After mounting, inspect the complete plate rather than only the camera targets. Check the joint, leading and trailing areas, plate edges and features away from the datum. A target can be centred while the image length is wrong. If the distant feature remains outside the controlled position, return to the plate and prepress evidence before another mounting attempt.

Prove the correction on press without hiding another variable

Run the corrected plate with the approved cylinder or sleeve, tape, impression, web tension and press recipe. Bring register in using the normal press controls, then check defined targets and image features through the full repeat. Record the measured result at startup and normal production condition. Do not accept only the easiest target beside the plate joint or the camera mark used during mounting.

If the printed distance is correct but colours move with speed, acceleration or web position, investigate press register, web transport, tension and substrate behaviour separately. If the distance remains wrong at stable conditions, return to the physical repeat, plate dimensions and prepress output. Changing several press settings while evaluating a remade plate removes the comparison needed to close the cause.

Use representative production material and include the required substrate conditioning and inspection method. Retain the accepted print beside the failed sample. Mark the job revision, cylinder or sleeve, plate set, press settings and approval. The next repeat order should be released from that evidence rather than from a note saying that register was adjusted on press.

Build a repeat and distortion check into every job handover

The handover should connect customer artwork, approved finished repeat, press and unit, cylinder or sleeve, plate and tape construction, prepress compensation, plate output and mounting coordinates. Make one responsible person confirm that these records agree before plates reach the mounter. This is especially important when the same design runs on more than one press or repeat.

Keep changes under revision control. A new cylinder, sleeve, plate thickness, tape construction or press assignment can require the job to be reviewed even when the artwork has not changed. Label every remade separation and remove obsolete plates from the live set. Mixed revisions can create a fault that appears to be an operator's mounting error.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about flexo plate distortion and repeat length

Can a plate be mounted accurately and still print out of register around the repeat?

Yes. The mounting target can be positioned correctly while the plate image length or prepress compensation does not match the physical repeat. Check placement and geometry as separate controls.

Should an operator stretch a plate to make the trailing target align?

No. Preserve the plate, measure it by the approved method and verify the repeat and prepress output. A plate outside its controlled geometry should be corrected through the responsible prepress process.

What information should accompany a flexo plate to mounting?

Include the job and file revision, press and unit, cylinder or sleeve identity, exact repeat, plate and tape construction, register-mark drawing, mounting datum, prepress output record and the required QA result.

Does a clear camera image prove that the whole plate geometry is correct?

No. The camera supports accurate target positioning. Inspect dimensions and features away from the target and confirm the printed result through the complete repeat.

What should be retained after correcting repeat-length register drift?

Keep failed and accepted print samples, plate measurements, file and plate revisions, cylinder or sleeve identity, tape and plate details, mounting report, press settings and the approved full-repeat result.
