Plate Mounting Air Bubbles

Air Bubbles Under Flexo Mounting Tape? Check the Sleeve and Laydown Method

Trace air bubbles under flexo mounting tape through sleeve preparation, drying, tape handling, plate support and even laydown pressure before remounting.

21 September 20267 min readRoyal Coat

An air bubble found after a flexo plate has been mounted is evidence that one stage of surface preparation or laydown was not controlled. Pressing harder on the finished mount does not identify whether the air is under the tape, under the plate or beside contamination on the sleeve. Mark the bubble, find the affected interface and correct the mounting method before the cylinder or sleeve reaches the press.

Identify where the bubble was trapped

Inspect the mounted plate under low-angle light before it leaves the mounting area. Mark the position without writing on the image or cutting the plate. Check whether the bubble follows the edge of the mounting tape, sits between tape and sleeve, or lies between the tape and plate. A cluster around one speck, scratch or tape joint needs a different correction from a long band across the sleeve face.

Record the colour, plate code, sleeve or cylinder identification, tape product and lot, operator, mounting time and bubble position. Note whether the bubble is inside the printed image, close to a plate edge or outside the working area. Keep a photograph with a scale and the sleeve reference line visible. This makes repeat faults easier to compare than a note saying plate had bubbles.

If the same position shows a raised area after the plate and tape are removed, inspect the sleeve surface for dried ink, adhesive residue, a nick or local damage. If the bubbles move between mounts, concentrate on cleaning, liner removal, tape tension, plate support and laydown pressure rather than assuming the sleeve is dimensionally faulty.

Prepare and dry the sleeve surface

Remove the previous plate and tape using the approved procedure. Do not use a boxcutter on the sleeve. Polywest warns that cutting on the sleeve can deform its surface. Inspect the full face and edges under clean light, then deal with adhesive, ink and damage using methods accepted for that sleeve construction.

Polywest recommends cleaning the sleeve surface with a solvent-based cloth and diluted cleaning agents such as ethanol or isopropanol, selected according to the ink system. Its product note advises against soaking, calls for excess cleaner to be removed with a dry cloth and says the surface should be allowed to evaporate for about 30 minutes before it is taped again.

Treat that waiting period as part of the mounting route, not idle time to be shortened when the press is waiting. A surface that still carries cleaner, moisture, lint or adhesive residue is not ready for tape. Confirm the cleaner, dilution, cloth and drying time in the mounting record so a later adhesion problem can be traced to the actual preparation used.

Apply the tape without creating a second fault

Check the job record for the approved tape grade, width and layout. Confirm that the sleeve reference and mounting lines are visible and that the tape roll has been stored and handled according to its supplier's instructions. Damaged edges, contaminated adhesive or a distorted roll should not be corrected by stretching the tape harder during application.

Start the tape square to the sleeve reference and maintain a repeatable application path. Keep the release liner controlled so exposed adhesive does not touch gloves, clothing or the mounting table. Avoid overlaps and uncontrolled gaps. Where more than one strip is used, follow the maintained joint method and keep the joint away from a place where it can create a local step under the plate.

Heaford describes tape applicators and a silicone-foam laydown roller as methods for applying tape more consistently. The roller applies even pressure across the sleeve width and is intended to reduce the air-bubble risk associated with hand rolling. The useful point is the controlled pressure path. One operator pressing selected areas by hand cannot reproduce the same load across a wide sleeve.

Support the plate while it is laid down

Align the register marks before a large area of plate touches the tape. Support the unused portion of the plate so its weight does not pull it onto the adhesive ahead of the intended laydown line. Heaford lists rear plate support and plate-and-tape laydown equipment on its mounting systems for this controlled sequence.

Bring the plate onto the tape progressively and use even pressure across the working width. Do not chase a bubble by pressing from several directions after the surrounding plate is already fixed. If the plate lands early at the centre or one edge, air may be isolated rather than pushed towards a free edge.

After the first plate, inspect the tape and plate interfaces before mounting the remaining colours. If the first mount shows a repeated band or edge pattern, stop and correct the method. Continuing can put the same hidden fault into every colour and turn one remount into a complete job change.

Decide whether the mount must be rejected

Use the plant's documented acceptance rule for bubble size, location and plate-edge condition. A bubble within the image, beside a plate edge or over a raised contaminant should not be released to press because an operator managed to flatten its appearance temporarily. Remove the mount and identify the cause while the evidence is still available.

Do not puncture a bubble with a knife while the plate is on the sleeve. The blade can mark the plate, tape or sleeve, and the puncture does not remove contamination or uneven adhesive contact. Polywest specifically warns against cutting on sleeve surfaces. Use the approved demounting procedure and protect the sleeve from local heat and sharp tools.

When remounting, change only the factor indicated by the inspection. If the sleeve was not dry, correct cleaning and drying. If the tape application trapped a continuous band, correct the applicator or laydown path. If the plate dropped onto the tape early, improve plate support. Record the result so the team knows which correction removed the fault.

Make bubble prevention part of the job record

Keep the sleeve condition, cleaner, drying time, tape identification, application method, laydown equipment, plate code, mounting tolerance and inspection photographs with the job. For repeats, bring back the approved mounting route instead of relying on an operator to remember how the plate was handled months earlier.

Review where bubbles are found. Faults concentrated on one sleeve may point to surface condition or damage. Faults linked to one tape roll, one applicator position or one part of a wide plate suggest different checks. A simple position record is more useful than counting all bubbles as one mounting problem.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about plate mounting air bubbles

Can a small bubble under a flexo plate be pressed out after mounting?

Do not rely on extra local pressure without identifying the interface and cause. Use the plant's acceptance rule, and remount when the bubble affects the image, plate edge or a contaminated area.

How long should a cleaned sleeve dry before tape is applied?

Polywest's care note says excess cleaner should be removed with a dry cloth and the sleeve surface allowed to evaporate for about 30 minutes before taping. Follow the maintained instruction for the actual sleeve and cleaner.

Why does hand rolling leave bubbles under mounting tape?

Hand pressure can vary across the width and may close the adhesive around trapped air. Heaford uses an even-pressure silicone-foam laydown roller to reduce this risk during tape and plate application.

Should a trapped bubble be pierced with a blade?

No. A blade can damage the plate, tape or sleeve, and it does not correct contamination or poor adhesive contact. Demount and correct the cause using the approved procedure.

What evidence helps diagnose repeated mounting bubbles?

Keep photographs and positions with the sleeve ID, plate code, tape lot, cleaner, drying time, operator, applicator and laydown method. The pattern often indicates which stage needs attention.
