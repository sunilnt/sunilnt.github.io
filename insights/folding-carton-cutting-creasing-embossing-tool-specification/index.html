Folding Carton Cutting and Creasing

Cutting, Creasing and Embossing in One Pass? Specify the Carton Tool From the Board

Specify combined cutting, creasing and embossing tooling from the paperboard, grain direction, carton drawing, machine geometry and finished fold.

7 October 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A carton blank can leave the cutting station with clean edges and still fail when it is folded. The crease may crack, the panel may bow or the embossing may sit out of register with the print. These faults are difficult to solve when the tooling order contains only an outline drawing and grammage. A useful specification connects the actual paperboard, grain direction, printed construction, machine and finished carton before the cutting and creasing plates are made.

Begin with the finished carton, not the tool drawing

Mark the approved carton drawing with every cut, crease, perforation, partial cut and embossed area. Add the glue flap, product-opening direction and any panel that must remain especially flat. If the design carries Braille, a security feature or a registered logo emboss, identify the artwork revision and the inspection method used after converting.

State how the blank will be folded and glued and how it enters the customer's packing line. A crease that looks acceptable while flat may have excessive folding resistance or may crack when taken to 180 degrees. Define the angle, fold direction and appearance that production must achieve on the real carton rather than approving only the open blank.

Supply an imposed printed sheet, not only a CAD file. Show the gripper or leading edge, operator side, machine direction, print register marks and the position of varnish, foil or lamination. The toolmaker needs to see where a cutting or embossing feature sits against the printed image and which surfaces have already changed the board.

Specify paperboard thickness and grain direction

Record the exact board grade, supplier, batch where available, grammage and measured thickness. Thickness is not interchangeable with grammage. Holmen's paperboard guidance explains that different board types have different thickness-to-grammage relationships and physical properties, so the creasing tool should be designed for the selected type and thickness.

Mark machine direction and cross direction on the sheet and drawing. The same crease geometry may behave differently when it runs parallel or across the grain. Holmen notes that crease-rule width, groove width and make-ready thickness should be chosen with board type, thickness and crease direction in mind. Keep that information with every material sample sent for tooling review.

Include the complete construction. A film laminate, foil, heavy coating or varnish can change cutting, folding and surface appearance. Condition the board according to the supplier's guidance before trials and record the pressroom environment where it matters. A dry, brittle sheet and a properly conditioned sheet should not be treated as one tooling result.

Match the male-female system to the machine

Kisscut describes male-female plates for machines with double magnetic cylinders, combining cutting and embossing for folding-carton applications. Confirm that the installed machine, cylinder pair and mounting method match the proposed system. Similar working width does not establish compatibility.

Send the machine make and model, cylinder diameters, working width, repeat or sheet format, bearer arrangement, available air gap, running direction and the drawing for both magnetic cylinders. Kisscut also describes protection dies used to reduce air gap on suitable machines. That component must be engineered for the actual cylinder geometry, not added at press-side to recover an incomplete specification.

Identify all reference and mounting features used to locate the male and female parts. The two plates must meet each other and the printed sheet at the intended position. Ask how the tooling will be identified, mounted and checked before production. A register correction on the print cannot reliably compensate for a mismatched plate pair.

Separate cutting, creasing and embossing requirements

Write an acceptance requirement for each operation. Cutting should produce a clean edge without unacceptable fibres, chipping or damage to the reverse. Creasing should create a controlled hinge that folds in the required direction without surface cracking, liner splitting or panel bowing. Embossing should reach the approved relief and position without tearing, whitening or marking another panel.

Do not use one pressure increase to correct every feature. Too little crease penetration, a groove that is too wide, a worn matrix, poor rule alignment and unsuitable board condition can produce different faults. Holmen's guidance links good creasing to the rule height and width, groove width, make-ready accuracy and pressure. The physical sample shows which part of that system needs attention.

Where cutting and embossing share one tool, review the most demanding feature at the same time as the overall blank. A deep local emboss may change sheet support close to a cut or crease. Ask the tooling supplier to review the feature spacing, relief, board and machine gap together before the artwork is released.

Run a controlled material trial

Use production board from the intended supplier and include every surface treatment planned for the job. Run enough sheets to reach stable machine condition, then retain sequential samples. Record machine, tool IDs, board batch and thickness, grain direction, speed, pressure and every deliberate adjustment.

Inspect the blank flat and after folding. Check dimensional accuracy, print-to-cut register, cut edge, waste removal, crease position, cracking, folding resistance and embossing relief. For a glued carton, assemble samples and verify squareness, closure and packing-line presentation. One attractive flat sample is not proof of the finished pack.

Change one variable at a time. If pressure, make-ready and material are altered together, the accepted result cannot be repeated with confidence. When a defect appears, mark its exact position on the blank and keep the neighbouring sheet. Repeating faults can then be related to the tool, cylinder, board or machine path.

Release the tool with a repeatable job record

Store the approved carton drawing, imposed artwork, board specification, thickness range, grain direction, tool and cylinder IDs, mounting reference, machine settings and folded samples together. Photograph the approved embossing and crease under consistent light. The record should let another shift reproduce the job without interpreting an unmarked carton on a shelf.

Set a hold rule for material substitutions. A board with the same grammage but different thickness or construction needs review before the old setup is accepted. The same applies when lamination, varnish, foil, embossing relief or carton geometry changes. Reusing a tool does not mean the complete converting condition is unchanged.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about folding carton cutting and creasing

Can one tool cut, crease and emboss a folding carton?

Kisscut describes male-female plate systems that combine cutting and embossing, and packaging plates for cutting, scoring and perforation. Suitability must be confirmed for the machine, board and design.

Is paperboard grammage enough for ordering creasing tooling?

No. Record the board type and measured thickness as well. Different paperboards can have different thickness and physical behaviour at the same grammage.

Why does grain direction matter when creasing cartons?

The board responds differently along and across its fibre direction. Mark machine and cross direction so the crease geometry and trial can reflect the actual layout.

Should a carton tool be approved from a flat blank?

No. Check cutting, dimensions and register while flat, then fold, glue or assemble the carton and inspect cracking, resistance, shape and packing-line presentation.

Can the same settings be kept after changing paperboard supplier?

Only after the new board type, thickness, grain, surface and folded result have been checked. Matching grammage alone does not prove an unchanged converting condition.
