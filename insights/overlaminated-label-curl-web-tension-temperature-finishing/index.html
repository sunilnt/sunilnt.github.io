Pressure-Sensitive Label Overlamination Curl

Overlaminated Labels Curl After Finishing? Balance Both Webs Before Raising Nip Pressure

Diagnose curl in overlaminated pressure-sensitive labels by checking incoming roll shape, web tension, temperature, ink cure, nip conditions and finished-roll samples.

14 November 20267 min readRoyal Coat

An overlaminated label can leave the nip flat and curl after die-cutting, rewinding or dispensing. That sequence matters. Curl is often blamed on the laminate or corrected with more nip pressure, but the finished construction may be carrying unequal stress from the printed web and the overlaminate. Establish where the curl first appears, then balance the two webs and prove the result through the complete finishing route. A flat sample taken beside the laminating unit is not enough if the converted label will not dispense or lie correctly on the container.

Prove that lamination is creating the curl

Keep samples of the printed web before lamination, immediately after the nip, after die-cutting and from the completed roll. Mark machine direction, operator side and drive side. Cut each sample to the same size and allow it to rest under the same conditions before comparing it. This separates curl already present in the printed construction from stress introduced during lamination, die-cutting or rewinding.

Record the direction and shape of the curl. Avery Dennison notes that unequal tension between the substrate and overlaminate causes the finished label to curl towards the web that was under greater tension. An irregular, twisted or 'potato-chip' shape is useful evidence. Do not flatten the sample by hand before inspection, because that removes the symptom the setup team needs to diagnose.

Check both incoming rolls before changing settings

Confirm the printed facestock, adhesive, liner, overlaminate, thickness, width, roll age and storage condition against the approved job. Inspect both rolls for baggy edges, telescoping, tight bands, edge damage and splice build-up. A distorted incoming roll cannot be corrected reliably at the laminating nip. If one batch behaves differently, retain the roll labels and samples rather than combining the result with the normal setup record.

Condition the printed web and overlaminate in the same production area before the trial. Avery Dennison advises bringing the two materials to the same temperature because unequal expansion or contraction can form wrinkles or puckers as the construction reaches equilibrium. Record the conditioning time and room conditions used for the accepted trial so the result can be repeated on a later shift.

Balance tension instead of copying one number

Run the lowest stable tension that keeps each web controlled through its own path. The printed construction and the overlaminate can differ in thickness, stiffness and stretch, so identical displayed settings do not prove equal stress. Avery Dennison's 2024 tension bulletin explains that applied stress depends on material width and thickness and that unwind, process and rewind are separate tension zones. Treat the lamination unwind as its own controlled input.

Make a small, documented change to one web at a time. If the label curls towards the overlaminate, reduce the stress being carried by that web before increasing stress elsewhere. Watch the dancer or load-cell response through acceleration, steady running and deceleration. A sample made only at constant low speed may hide a control problem that returns during a production roll change or speed ramp.

Use nip pressure and temperature for the correct purpose

Set nip pressure to produce continuous contact and wet-out across the working width. More pressure is not a dependable cure for curl created by web strain. Check parallelism, roller condition and pressure across the width if one side shows bubbles, silvering or a different curl. Retain a full-width strip so the defect can be related to the machine position.

Do not add heat automatically. Pressure-sensitive overlaminates may be processed without a heated nip, depending on the approved material and supplier guidance. If controlled post-lamination heat is part of the process, record where it is applied and verify that both webs were conditioned. A hotter setting that improves immediate appearance can still leave dimensional stress that becomes visible after the roll stands.

Confirm ink cure, cleanliness and surface compatibility

Check that the ink or toner is fully dry or cured before overlamination. Avery Dennison warns that insufficiently cured ink can reduce overlaminate adhesion and may bleed into the adhesive, producing ghosting or blurred detail. Use the approved cure or drying test for the actual ink, substrate and coverage. A dry edge does not prove that a heavy solid area is ready to laminate.

Inspect the web immediately before the nip for dust, trim particles, static attraction and surface contamination. Dirt trapped under the laminate can form bubbles or visible specks and can weaken local adhesion. Also confirm with the ink supplier whether wax, silicone or another surface additive can interfere with the selected overlaminate. Run the printed colours and heavy coverage from the real job in the compatibility trial.

Qualify the complete finishing and dispensing route

PRATI documents digital finishing routes that can combine lamination with die-cutting, inspection, slitting and rewinding. Test the construction after every stage that changes web stress or roll pressure. Check die-cut waste removal, label flatness, edge lift, matrix behaviour and inspection stability. Then build a trial roll at the intended core, width, diameter, tension, taper and lay-on settings.

Allow the trial roll to stand for the agreed period and sample the outside, middle and near the core. Measure or compare curl in a repeatable orientation, then dispense labels through the intended peel path or a representative test. If curl grows deeper in the roll, review winding pressure with the lamination settings. If it appears uniformly before rewinding, return to the two incoming webs and the lamination zone.

Release a recipe that another shift can reproduce

The job record should identify both material codes and batches, roll orientation, conditioning time, room conditions, unwind and process tension settings, line speed, nip pressure, temperature where used, ink cure result, web-cleaning method, die-cut route and rewind recipe. Attach an accepted finished label and state where its sample was taken from the roll.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about pressure-sensitive label overlamination curl

Why does an overlaminated label curl towards one side?

Unequal stress between the printed web and overlaminate is a common cause. Identify the direction of curl and reduce the excessive stress in the responsible web instead of compensating with more nip pressure.

Should both unwinds use the same tension setting?

Not automatically. The two webs can have different thickness, stiffness and stretch. Use the lowest stable tension for each material and judge the combined construction from controlled samples.

Can additional nip pressure remove curl?

Nip pressure should provide even contact and wet-out. It does not reliably remove strain already introduced by unequal web tension, temperature or incoming roll distortion.

When should curl be checked?

Check before lamination, after the nip, after die-cutting and in samples from the outside, middle and near the core of a finished trial roll after it has stood for the agreed time.
