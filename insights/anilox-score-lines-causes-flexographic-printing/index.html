Anilox Damage Troubleshooting

Anilox Score Lines in Flexographic Printing: Find the Cause Before Another Roller Is Damaged

Trace anilox score lines through ink filtration, chamber condition, blade setup and contamination before returning the press to production.

15 August 20268 min readRoyal Coat

A score line is not solved by polishing the visible mark or fitting another roll and restarting. Something carried a hard particle into the doctoring contact, trapped debris at the blade, or allowed damaging metal-to-ceramic contact. Stop and preserve the evidence. If the cause remains in the ink circuit or chamber, the replacement roller can be marked in the same place within minutes.

Confirm that the line follows the anilox

Print a controlled sample and note the exact cross-web position of the streak. Inspect the anilox under suitable light while rotating it safely by hand according to the press procedure. A continuous circumferential mark at the matching position points toward anilox damage or a band of plugged cells.

Rule out plate damage, dirty impression surfaces, substrate marks and a stationary defect elsewhere in the web path. Do not drag a fingernail, metal pick or abrasive pad across the ceramic surface while checking it.

Quarantine the ink and filter circuit

Drain and label the ink from the affected station rather than returning it to common stock. Inspect magnets, filters, strainers, pump components, hoses and the chamber for metal fragments, dried ink, pigment agglomerates or damaged seals. Record what is found before cleaning everything away.

A filter is useful only if its rating, location and bypass condition suit the circuit. Check whether the filter was missing, incorrectly installed, overloaded or bypassed. Review recent maintenance work that could have introduced swarf, fasteners or seal fragments.

Inspect blades, seals and chamber alignment

Remove doctor and containment blades using the approved safety method. Look for localised nicks, trapped particles, uneven wear and signs that the blade was overloaded. Inspect end seals and the chamber body for hard deposits that can break loose during a run.

Check that the chamber sits squarely and that pressure is only high enough to seal and doctor correctly. Excess load increases blade deflection, heat and wear and can hold contamination against the surface. A pressure increase that stopped a leak may have hidden a worn seal or poor alignment.

Check for dry running and loss of lubrication

Zecher specifically warns that dry running a blade chamber can cause scoring and other mechanical damage. Review start-up, stop and wash cycles. The anilox should not continue rotating under blade contact after ink or approved cleaning fluid has left the nip.

Interlocks and standard work should define when the chamber loads, when the pump starts and how the station is flushed. Pay particular attention after production stops, because dried ink can harden at the blade edge before the next start.

Decide whether the roller is still usable

A light visual mark is not enough to judge serviceability. Examine cell structure and measure volume in and around the damaged band. Compare the result with the required application and print sample. Expert inspection may be needed to determine whether repair, recoating or replacement is appropriate.

Do not compensate for a damaged band with extra impression, blade pressure or a stronger anilox in another station. Those actions can create a second defect and make the original cause harder to identify.

Close the incident with preventive action

Document the position, photographs, ink batch, filter condition, blades, chamber settings and maintenance history. Replace failed components, clean the complete circuit and verify that no debris remains before installing a sound roller.

Add a start-up check for ink flow before blade loading, a filter-inspection interval and a clear stop rule when a new streak appears. The useful outcome is not only a repaired roll; it is a press condition that will not damage the next one.

Run a controlled production trial

Use the affected ink station after the circuit has been cleaned, filtered and inspected with a sound roller. Agree the test roll, normal operating range and responsible operator before starting. Begin from verified mechanical condition and approved consumables. Change one variable at a time, allow the process to stabilise and retain samples from make-ready, steady production and the end of the roll. A short demonstration at an easy setting does not prove that the process will hold through a commercial job.

During the trial, monitor ink, filters, magnets, pump, hoses, chamber, blades, seals, loading, start sequence and web position. Mark every deliberate change and production event on the same timeline. Acceptance should require clean printing without a recurring line and no new mark on the anilox surface. If the result is outside the agreed limit, preserve the sample and machine state long enough to identify the cause instead of making several unrecorded corrections.

Turn the result into a pressroom standard

Save station, roller, line position, ink batch, debris found, parts replaced, chamber settings and verification sample. Attach photographs, measurement reports and an approved finished sample where useful. Give the recipe a revision and link it to the exact material, artwork or machine configuration. Operators should be able to see what may be adjusted, the permitted range, the time allowed for stabilisation and who approves an exception.

Define a stop and escalation rule for any returning streak, trapped debris, dry blade contact, abnormal wear or visible damage to the replacement roller. Review repeat faults by station, material and shift instead of treating each one as a separate surprise. A good standard does not remove judgement; it gives experienced operators reliable evidence and prevents the next shift from undoing a successful correction.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about anilox damage troubleshooting

Can dried ink cause anilox scoring?

Hard deposits can trap at the blade or break into the circuit. The full chamber, filtration and wash process should be inspected rather than assuming one cause.

Can higher chamber pressure stop a score line?

No. Extra pressure may worsen wear and will not remove the damaging source.

Should ink from a scored station be reused?

Quarantine it until the contamination risk has been assessed and it has been filtered or disposed of under the plant's approved procedure.

What should be changed first during the trial?

Start from verified mechanical condition and approved consumables, then change only one controlled variable at a time.

What makes the result repeatable on the next shift?

A revision-controlled recipe with component identity, settings, measurements, approved samples and clear stop rules.
