Flexo Print Cylinder Changeover

Mounting Tape Leaves Glue on the Print Cylinder? Audit the Plate Change

Remove flexo mounting tape without damaging the print cylinder by controlling tape choice, dwell, peel method, cleaning, surface condition and remount checks.

17 September 20267 min readRoyal Coat

A plate can come off the press in minutes while its mounting tape leaves a hard band of adhesive on the print cylinder. The next plate then sits on a surface that is no longer clean or uniform. Scraping harder may remove the visible glue, but it can also mark the cylinder and turn a changeover delay into a repeat print problem. Keep the used tape and plate, record how they were removed, and inspect the complete cylinder before choosing a stronger cleaner or tool.

Record the residue pattern before cleaning it away

Photograph the cylinder as soon as the plate and tape are removed. Mark the gear side, operator side, cylinder identification, repeat, tape product and batch, plate, job length, press speed and time the assembly remained mounted. Note whether the adhesive stayed on the cylinder, the plate back or both.

The pattern is useful. A full-width film may point to the tape, dwell or removal method. A narrow band at the leading or trailing edge may belong to the edge seal, overlap or the way the operator started the peel. A local patch that returns at one position needs a close inspection of that cylinder surface and the mounting work carried out there.

Keep a short sample of the removed tape and do not combine several cleaning methods before the first condition is recorded. If one operator, tape roll or cylinder shows the problem more often, that comparison can shorten the investigation. A general complaint that the tape is sticky does not give the supplier enough evidence.

Check whether the tape suits the plate, cylinder and run

Confirm the mounting tape is approved for the plate material, cylinder surface, print work and expected dwell time. Record the foam hardness and thickness, adhesive system, storage condition and application date. A tape selected for print response still has to release predictably from the actual cylinder after the planned run.

Review how the cylinder was prepared before mounting. Residual cleaner, oil, moisture, lint or old adhesive can change the bond and create local high spots. Use only the cleaning material and preparation method approved by the cylinder and tape suppliers, then allow the surface to reach the required dry condition before the new tape is applied.

Look at the complete job history. Heat, long dwell, chemical exposure and repeated stop-start operation may alter removal behaviour. If a problem appears only after a particular ink, wash or long campaign, send those details to the tape and cylinder suppliers. Do not approve a different adhesive only from an easy short test.

Standardise the removal method

Write down where removal begins, the peel direction and angle, whether the plate and tape are lifted together, and which approved hand tool is used. Ask operators to remove the assembly at a steady rate without jerking or twisting it across the cylinder face. The method should protect the plate when it will be stored and keep the cylinder under control throughout the peel.

Do not use a knife, metal scraper or improvised abrasive to chase a small adhesive patch. A scratch or raised burr can disturb the next plate mount and may require professional inspection. Rotometal positions its ANTIFRICTION print-cylinder surface around easier tape and foam removal with less adhesive residue, while also providing additional surface protection. That does not remove the need for the approved removal and cleaning procedure.

If a release aid, heat or chemical is proposed, obtain written compatibility guidance for the tape, plate and cylinder surface first. Record the concentration, contact time and temperature where relevant. A method that releases the adhesive but softens a coating, attacks anodising or leaves another film has not completed the changeover correctly.

Inspect the cylinder after the adhesive is removed

Clean the surface with the approved product and lint-free material, then inspect it under low-angle light. Check the body, mounting lines, bearers, journals and gear. Look for glue haze, scratches, dents, corrosion, raised damage and any mark that can be felt even when it is difficult to see.

Rotometal manufactures print cylinders in steel and aluminium and offers anodised or ANTIFRICTION working surfaces. It also lists standard or hardened and ground gear options. Identify the exact cylinder construction and drawing before deciding how a damaged surface should be repaired. A cleaning instruction for bare steel should not be assumed suitable for every coated or anodised body.

If the next job has already shown banding, slur, bounce or registration variation, preserve the print sample and mark where the fault sits around the cylinder. Check concentricity, gear and bearer condition using the maintained plant method or arrange an inspection. Remounting another plate over a questionable surface only adds another variable.

Remount on a verified clean surface

Use the cylinder's axial and circumferential reference lines where provided and mount the new tape without trapping air, overlap or debris. Rotometal lists horizontal and vertical guide lines as options for print-cylinder setup. Confirm that the mounting reference matches the plate-mounter method and the repeat-job record.

After mounting, inspect the tape edges and plate seating around the full circumference. Check that the leading and trailing edges are secure and that no local rise remains where the old adhesive was found. If the plate needs extra pressure or local hand work to sit flat, stop and inspect the mating surfaces again.

Run the job from setup through normal speed and check register, print pressure and any once-per-revolution variation. Compare the result with an approved sample. The acceptance check should prove that the cylinder surface is clean and mechanically sound, not simply that the new plate survived a slow first pull.

Measure whether a surface treatment solves the real delay

Time the complete changeover from the last acceptable print to the first acceptable print on the next job. Separate plate and tape removal, adhesive cleaning, cylinder inspection, remounting and approval. This shows whether difficult tape release is the main constraint or one small part of a longer setup problem.

Rotometal states that its ANTIFRICTION coating is intended to let mounting tape and foam come away more easily and with less residue. Evaluate that option against the tapes, dwell times, cleaners and cylinder sizes used in the plant. Ask for the correct surface specification, care instructions and acceptance check for the exact press and cylinder drawing.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about flexo print cylinder changeover

Why does mounting tape leave adhesive on a flexo print cylinder?

Possible factors include the tape and cylinder combination, surface preparation, dwell time, heat or chemical exposure, tape storage and the removal method. Record the pattern before changing several variables.

Can a metal scraper be used to remove a small glue patch?

Do not use an unapproved sharp or abrasive tool. It can scratch or raise the cylinder surface and create a mounting or print defect. Follow the cylinder and tape suppliers' cleaning guidance.

Does an antifriction surface eliminate cylinder cleaning?

No. It is intended to make tape and foam removal easier and reduce residue, but the cylinder still requires the approved preparation, inspection, cleaning and maintenance routine.

What should be inspected after adhesive removal?

Check the full working surface, mounting references, bearers, journals and gear for residue, scratches, dents, corrosion, raised damage and any mark related to a repeated print fault.

How should a changeover improvement be measured?

Use the same start and finish points and time removal, cleaning, inspection, remounting and approval separately. Confirm the first acceptable print at normal production speed.
