Rotary Die-Cutting Width Diagnosis

Rotary Die Cuts at the Edges but Misses the Centre? Reduce Force Before Replacing the Tool

Diagnose a rotary die that cuts at the web edges but misses the centre by checking force, flex, alignment, bearers, anvil and material in order.

6 November 20266 min readRoyal Coat

A pressure-sensitive label may release cleanly at both edges of the web while the centre remains attached to the matrix. The usual reaction is to add more die pressure. That can deepen the edge strike and make the centre problem worse if the tool is bending under load. Before replacing the flexible die, map the cut across the width, reduce the applied force carefully and establish whether the fault follows the tool, the anvil or the press position.

Read the pattern before touching the pressure screws

Run a short strip at the current setting and mark operator side, centre and drive side. Check face-stock separation and inspect the liner strike under consistent light or magnification. Record whether the centre is light, whether one complete side is light, whether both sides are light, or whether one cavity repeats the fault. These patterns do not point to the same cause.

A light centre with acceptable or heavy strikes at both edges is a recognised sign of tool flex. Wilson Manufacturing explains that force, tool diameter and distance between the bearers affect bending. Adding force to recover the centre can load the ends harder while the middle continues to move away from the anvil. The correct trial is a controlled reduction in force, provided the station remains stable and the die still has enough force to complete the cut.

A light operator side or drive side needs a different test. Check that the die and anvil are aligned, that journal blocks and bearings are sound, and that the bearers are clean and properly supported. Wilson's current troubleshooting guidance says uneven cutting across the width can indicate a levelling or bearer problem. Do not condemn a flexible die from one width pattern without checking the station that supports it.

Separate die flex from a low cavity or worn anvil

Note the repeat of the defect. If one label fails at the same cavity position on every die revolution, inspect that cavity and its cutting rule. A local low or damaged blade normally follows the die repeat. If the light cut occurs at a different repeat or stays at one press position, inspect the anvil surface, bearer contact and cylinder condition instead.

Where the flexible die can be mounted in the opposite direction, reverse it as a diagnostic trial and keep the same approved material. If the fault moves with the die, the tool or its mounting deserves attention. If it remains on the same side of the press, look harder at the anvil, alignment and support. This test is useful because it changes one variable without immediately changing the cutting geometry.

Clean the magnetic cylinder face and the back of the flexible die before remounting. A label fragment, dried adhesive, rust spot or raised damage under the sheet can create a local high point and alter the cut nearby. Inspect the anvil for grooves and confirm that the gear and bearers are free of debris. These checks are slower than turning a pressure screw, but they prevent a setup fault from being blamed on a new die.

Use the least force that produces an accepted cut

Return to a low, known pressure and increase in small equal steps while checking all lanes. Stop at the first setting that gives the accepted face-stock cut without unacceptable liner strike. Wilson recommends the lightest pressure that achieves the necessary cut and notes that excessive pressure accelerates wear and can damage the die-cutting components.

Record pressure at the start of the approved run and again after the station has warmed. Steel rolls expand with heat, so contact pressure can rise during production even when the screws have not moved. If the job needs progressively more pressure, do not normalise that trend. Check material, tool condition, anvil wear, bearings and support before the higher setting becomes the repeat-job standard.

Pressure gauges make the trial repeatable, but a number alone does not prove an acceptable cut. Save the left-centre-right liner sample with the pressure reading, machine, material batch, die identity, anvil identity and running speed. That record lets the next operator reproduce the accepted condition and shows whether pressure is increasing over the life of the tool.

Treat gap adjustment as a correction tool, not a diagnosis

An adjustable anvil can compensate for changes in cutting depth without forcing the complete station harder. Rotometal describes its rotoGAP system as allowing fine adjustment of the gap between the anvil and magnetic cylinder across the web. This can be useful when material thickness, tool wear or a known width variation needs controlled correction.

Do not use gap adjustment to hide an unidentified alignment fault, damaged anvil or flexing tool. First establish whether the problem follows the die, stays with the station or changes with material. Then adjust only the affected zone in small steps and keep an accepted liner sample. A correction that produces a clean label but severely marks the liner is not an acceptable setup.

If the centre still will not cut at a safe pressure, send the tooling supplier the die layout, number around and across, web width, bearer spacing, substrate, adhesive, liner construction, actual liner thickness, accepted and failed samples, press model, anvil details and pressure record. Tool geometry and material must be reviewed together. Royal Coat can coordinate the application details with Wilson Manufacturing or Rotometal for the represented tooling route.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about rotary die-cutting width diagnosis

Why can more die pressure make a light centre cut worse?

If the tool is flexing, extra force can load the ends more heavily while the centre bends away from the anvil. Reduce force carefully and check the strike across the full width.

How do I tell whether the problem follows the flexible die?

Record the failed cavity and, where the die design permits, reverse its running direction. A fault that moves with the tool points towards the die or mounting; one that stays at the press position points towards the anvil or station.

Should the liner show a visible die strike?

Use the customer's release requirement and the material supplier's limits. The accepted setup must separate the face stock without unacceptable liner damage, not merely make every lane peel.

When is an adjustable anvil useful?

It is useful when a measured gap correction is required for material variation, controlled wear compensation or a known width difference. Diagnose alignment, anvil condition and tool flex first.

What should be sent when asking for tooling support?

Send the die layout, repeat, web width, material and liner details, actual thickness, tool and anvil identities, pressure log, press model, speed and marked accepted and failed samples.
