Opaque White in Flexo: Specify the Anilox From the Target Coverage, Not Line Count

Specify an anilox for opaque-white flexo printing from the film, ink, target coverage, plate, speed and drying or curing limit, not line count alone.

3 September 20268 min readRoyal Coat

An opaque white can look weak even when the anilox carries a large nominal volume. It can also look strong at setup speed and then lose uniformity when the press reaches production speed. Ordering a coarser roller without identifying the limiting condition may add ink while leaving the real fault untouched. Start with the film, the approved white result and the complete transfer path. The correct anilox specification must deliver enough usable ink to the plate and still allow the job to dry or cure, hold detail and run cleanly.

Define the white result on the actual construction

State where the white sits in the print sequence and what it must do. A backing white below reverse-printed colours, a surface white carrying fine text and a flood white below a metallic effect are different jobs. Record the film type, treatment, ink system, white formulation, number of white hits, overprint sequence and whether the finished pack is viewed through the film or from the printed surface.

Use an approved physical sample and a measurable control agreed with the ink supplier and customer. Depending on the job, the release may include optical density, opacity or contrast over a defined backing, colour, visual evenness and adhesion. Keep the measuring instrument, backing, aperture and sample orientation consistent. A density number taken on a different backing cannot settle an opacity complaint.

Include the minimum production speed and the available drying or curing capacity in the target. A roller that reaches the visual result only at low speed is not yet a working production specification.

Audit the present transfer before specifying more volume

Record the current anilox identification, engraving, nominal volume, age and last verified condition. Measure the printed white across the web and around the repeat. If the result changes by lane, circumference or press station, inspect the roller, chamber, doctor blade, plate, sleeve and impression before concluding that the volume is too low.

A nominal volume on an old record does not prove the roller is delivering that volume today. Dried ink, poor cleaning, cell damage and wear can reduce or disturb transfer. Compare the current roller with its baseline measurement where available and inspect the surface under the maintained pressroom method. If the condition is uncertain, a professional inspection is more useful than ordering a replacement from an unverified specification.

Check the ink at the same time. Record viscosity and temperature at stable production condition, mixing method, return flow and the point at which fresh ink or make-up fluid is added. An ink inventory that is separating, foaming or moving in viscosity can produce weak or uneven white even when the roller specification is suitable.

Compare cell volume and engraving geometry together

Line count describes how closely the engraved cells are arranged. It does not by itself state the usable volume, cell opening, emptying behaviour or support presented to the plate. Two rollers with a similar nominal volume can transfer differently when their cell geometry, engraving quality and surface condition differ.

Zecher lists several engraving approaches for different transfer requirements. Its established Hexa 60° is used across flexographic printing and coating. The company positions SteppedHex as a connected, staggered geometry that can raise the engraving resolution without a significant loss of volume and describes its transfer of white, lacquer, gold and silver as uniform and homogeneous. Zecher also positions H.I.T. for opaque white, lacquer and special colours through an optimised cavity geometry, with particular suitability noted for coating applications.

These descriptions are starting points, not a universal white specification. Ask for the proposed engraving, nominal volume, measurement method and application reasoning in writing. The supplier should know the film, ink, plate screen, image content, speed and present result before recommending a cell structure.

Protect the transfer path around the anilox

A higher-volume anilox cannot compensate reliably for poor metering or unnecessary impression. Inspect the doctor blade edge, blade angle, chamber alignment, end seals and pressure. Excess chamber pressure can distort metering and accelerate wear. Keep the minimum pressure that seals and meters correctly under the equipment supplier's procedure.

Check the plate and mounting tape against the white image. A flood area needs even support without bounce or local high spots. Fine reverses and small text require enough plate support and a transfer condition that does not close the image. Set anilox-to-plate and plate-to-substrate impression by the approved kiss-setting method rather than adding pressure to force a stronger white.

Cleaning must suit the engraving and ink chemistry. A white roller often handles a high pigment load, so the cleaning interval and verification method should be part of the job standard. Do not judge a new engraving against a partially blocked reference roller, and do not use an unapproved aggressive method that can damage the ceramic or cell walls.

Trial the white at the speed and temperature that matter

A controlled press trial should compare a practical range of engraving and volume options while holding the surrounding variables steady. Keep the film lot, ink batch and condition, plate, tape, blade, chamber pressure, impression and measurement method constant. A banded anilox trial can be useful when the supplier has selected a relevant range, but each band still has to be judged as part of the complete print result.

Bring the press to the normal production speed and allow the ink and web path to stabilise before taking the release samples. Measure across the web, inspect pinholing and mottle, check fine reverses and evaluate the colours printed over or below the white. Then verify drying or cure, adhesion, blocking risk and downstream converting under the approved job method.

Do not select the darkest band automatically. Reject an option that reaches opacity but cannot dry or cure at the required speed, produces poor reverse detail, creates unstable metering or makes cleaning impractical. The correct choice is the lowest-risk specification that meets the approved white result throughout the run.

Release a white standard that can be repeated

Once the trial is approved, save the anilox ID and specification, verified volume record, ink and batch, viscosity-temperature condition, mixer and circulation arrangement, plate and tape, blade, chamber setting, impression method, press speed, drying or curing condition and measured white result. Retain a protected approved sample with its backing and instrument settings.

Set a check frequency for the roller and printed white. Trend the measured result rather than waiting for a customer complaint. If the white begins to fall, compare ink condition, cleaning record, cell condition and press settings with the baseline before increasing impression or changing the setpoint.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about opaque white ink transfer

Does a lower anilox line count always print a stronger opaque white?

No. Line count alone does not define usable volume or transfer. Cell geometry, roller condition, ink, metering, plate, impression and production speed all affect the printed white.

Should opaque white be specified by density or opacity?

Use the control agreed for the job. Whichever measure is selected, keep the backing, instrument, aperture and sample orientation consistent and retain an approved physical standard.

Why can white look acceptable at setup speed but weak at production speed?

Ink condition, cell emptying, transfer, web temperature and available drying or curing can change with speed. Approve the anilox at the normal production condition, not while the press is crawling.

Can extra impression correct weak white coverage?

It may temporarily change the appearance, but it can damage highlight or reverse detail and increase wear. Verify ink transfer, roller condition and the approved kiss impression first.

What information should be sent for an opaque-white anilox recommendation?

Provide the film, surface treatment, ink, print sequence, white image, plate screen, current anilox data and condition, production speed, drying or curing limit, measured result and approved target.
