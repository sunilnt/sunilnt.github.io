Digital Label Finishing Register Drifts During the Roll? Check the Printed Web First

Troubleshoot register drift in digital label finishing by checking the printed roll, register mark, unwind tension, splice, tooling and correction trend.

7 September 20267 min readRoyal Coat

When the die, varnish or embellishment starts in register and moves later in the roll, it is tempting to keep correcting the finishing unit. That can hide the evidence. First record how the error develops and inspect the printed web entering the line. A change in repeat, edge position, tension, splice construction or register-mark quality can produce the same finished result as a tooling or control fault. The useful diagnosis separates those inputs before another roll is adjusted.

Describe the drift before changing a setting

Keep a marked sample from the beginning, middle and end of the affected section. Measure the error in the machine and cross-machine directions and note whether it grows gradually, moves suddenly, repeats at a fixed interval or changes only after a stop, acceleration or splice. These patterns lead to different checks.

A gradual movement through the roll points towards repeat, tension or roll-build variation. A single step at a splice calls for inspection of the joint and the way it passed the unwind and web guide. A repeating error at the same interval may be related to the printed repeat or converting tool. Do not reduce all of these symptoms to one description such as poor register.

Save the line speed, unwind diameter, correction trend and web position when the error appears. If the machine allows the register value to be exported, keep that record with the physical samples. An operator's memory of the correction direction is not enough for the next shift to reproduce the fault.

Inspect the incoming printed roll as a production component

Confirm the job revision, substrate, web width, print direction, repeat length, roll direction and required finished format. Check that the roll was wound and stored without telescoping, loose edges, blocking or core damage. A digital finishing line cannot hold a stable lateral or longitudinal reference if the incoming roll is moving on the core or entering at an angle.

Compare the printed repeat at several positions rather than measuring one label at the outer wrap. Also compare the left and right sides when the image or mark allows it. Film can respond to heat, tension and storage differently from paper. The job record should therefore identify the actual roll and print condition, not only the artwork file.

Mark any point where the printed roll changes behaviour. If the drift begins near a splice, retain the splice and the surrounding web. Record its thickness, angle, overlap, tape and alignment. A joint that pulls the web sideways or changes the effective length through the nip can upset register even when the machine returns to its normal reading afterwards.

Give the sensor a mark it can read consistently

Inspect the register mark for contrast, shape, size, contamination and clearance from nearby print. Look at it under the actual sensor condition, not only by eye on a bench. A mark that is strong on white paper may be difficult to distinguish on a clear, metallic or changing background. Confirm that no varnish, laminate edge or previous print is entering the sensing window.

Clean and set the sensor using the maintained machine procedure. Then watch the live detection through normal speed changes and across a splice. If the mark signal is intermittent, resolve that first. Increasing the control response around a weak or ambiguous signal can make the correction less stable.

PRATI's digital converting material describes automatic register arrangements using a gap, eye mark or measured length, depending on configuration. That choice must match the printed web and the job. The correct method is the one that gives an unambiguous reference for the full roll, not simply the method saved with a previous product.

Stabilise unwind tension and web position

Check the core grip, unwind brake or drive, dancer or load-cell movement, edge guide and rollers before altering register gain. Watch the line from low speed to production speed. A value that appears steady on the screen can still hide a sticking dancer, slipping core or short lateral movement that happens during acceleration.

Use the approved tension recipe for the actual construction. A pressure-sensitive labelstock, unsupported film and shrink-sleeve material do not necessarily respond to the same tension. PRATI positions DIGIFAST20000 as a modular mid-web platform for several label and packaging applications, so the job setup needs to identify the material and process route clearly.

Change one condition at a time and allow the complete web path to settle before judging the result. If tension, guide response and register correction are all adjusted together, a better roll may be produced without showing which change fixed it. That leaves the process vulnerable when the next batch arrives.

Separate printed-web movement from tooling error

Once the web and mark are stable, inspect the converting station. Confirm the tool, repeat, mounting, pressure, cleanliness and mechanical condition. For semi-rotary work, verify the programmed format and the maintained setup procedure. For a coating, white or embellishment unit, compare the register of that process separately from the die-cut result.

Use the error pattern to choose the check. If every process moves together against the printed image, the common reference is more suspect than one station. If the varnish remains correct while the cut moves, concentrate on the die-cutting station and its setup. If the left side and right side disagree, include web skew, tool alignment and material distortion rather than applying a single longitudinal correction.

PRATI's current DIGIFAST20000 information describes a configurable platform for printing, varnishing, embellishment and finishing in rolls or sheets. Its 2025 report also discusses registered processes on the line. A modular route makes station-by-station samples especially useful because they show where the error first enters the job.

Prove the correction through a complete roll sequence

Run the approved test through startup, normal production speed, an expected stop and restart, and at least one representative change in unwind diameter. If the job normally includes splices, prove a controlled splice as well. A short steady-speed sample can confirm the centre of the register window, but it cannot prove that the system remains stable through the events that caused the complaint.

Check the finished web against the actual acceptance method. Record the error by direction and process, the register correction trend, line speed, tension settings, sensor method, incoming-roll ID, splice position and tool ID. Add photographs of the mark and the retained samples. The next run should begin from this record rather than from an operator's recollection of the final adjustment.

Questions from production and purchasing teams

What to ask about digital finishing register control

Why does digital finishing start in register and drift later in the roll?

Check whether the printed repeat, unwind tension, roll build, edge position or register-mark signal changes as the roll runs. Record the correction trend and retain samples before changing the finishing station.

Can a splice cause a sudden register shift?

Yes. A thick, angled or misaligned splice can disturb web length or lateral position through the unwind, guide and nips. Retain the splice and compare register immediately before and after it.

Should register gain be increased when the sensor reading is unstable?

No. First confirm that the mark is clear, the sensor is clean and correctly set, and the signal remains reliable at production speed. A stronger correction around an unreliable reference can make the movement worse.

How can I tell whether the web or one converting station is moving?

Compare the printed image with each process separately. If every station moves together, inspect the common web and reference. If only one process moves, inspect that station, its tooling and setup.

What evidence should be saved after correcting register drift?

Save the incoming-roll ID, substrate, repeat, roll direction, splice positions, mark method, tool ID, speed, tension, correction trend and measured samples from startup, steady running, stops and the end of the roll.
